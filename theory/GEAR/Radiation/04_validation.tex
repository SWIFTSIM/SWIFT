\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

Two distinct kinds of check live in this section. Most of it validates
against Hu et al.\ (2017) and Smith et al.\ (2021)'s own reported
numbers for their specific multi-source clump test -- reproducing a
paper's result, not a test of the underlying physics in general (a
bug shared between this code and an assumption both papers happen to
make would pass this kind of check without being caught).
Section~\ref{sec:starbench} is different in kind: STARBENCH
\citep{Bisbas2015} is a single-source D-type expansion test where
twelve independent, differently-implemented radiation-hydrodynamics
codes agree with each other to $\sim$1--2\%, and this model is compared
directly against their consensus solution rather than against any one
paper's own subgrid scheme. It is a much more direct test of whether
this code reproduces the actual physics of D-type expansion, not just
whether it reproduces one particular paper's implementation of it.

\subsection{Setup}

\code{examples/SubgridTests/StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021/} reproduces
both papers' D-type expansion test directly: four co-located
19.2\,M$_\odot$ sources ($\dot N_{\rm ion}\approx2.5\times10^{48}\,
{\rm s^{-1}}$ each, $1\times10^{49}\,{\rm s^{-1}}$ combined), a uniform
$100\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$, $10^3\,$K background, and a 10\,pc-radius,
$10^4\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$ clump 20\,pc from the sources -- all confirmed
against the papers' own text, not assumed. Both papers report the
initial (equilibrium, no clump) Str\"omgren radius as 3.1\,pc for this
configuration, and reach $\sim$20--25\,pc by $t=8\,$Myr in the
uniform-medium, no-clump case; Smith et al.'s Fig.~2 (with the clump
present) shows their spherical scheme partially disrupting the clump
by 8\,Myr, and their HealPix scheme leaving it largely intact while the
front elsewhere reaches close to the no-clump reference. This is the
comparison every run below reproduces at two gas resolutions:
\code{gas\_mass=20} (matching \citeauthor{Smith2021}'s own resolution
for this test) and \code{gas\_mass=95} (a representative galaxy
zoom-in baryon particle mass).

\subsection{Why every run below uses $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$}
\label{sec:metallicity}

Both reference papers are effectively zero-metallicity (\citet{Hu2017}
state their D-type test medium is ``composed of pure hydrogen'';
neither paper's recombination rate or temperature floor has a
metallicity term at all) and clamp ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K.
This code's own temperature floor (Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}) is
metallicity-\emph{dependent}: at $Z=0$ the applied temperature is
$\approx$47,500\,K, not $10^4\,$K, which raises $r_{\rm HII}$ through
two independent channels -- a lower case-B recombination coefficient
(Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature}) and a higher D-type driving sound
speed ($c_s\propto\sqrt T$) -- so a $Z=0$ run is expected to overshoot
the papers' reference, and can run large enough to hit
\code{Stars:max\_HII\_search\_radius} (45\,pc) or the box half-width
(50\,pc).

Both channels are neutralized by construction at the metallicity where
$T_{\rm collisional}=10^4\,$K exactly (Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{0.86}{1+0.22\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)} = 1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z/Z_\odot \approx 0.231.
  \label{eq:z0231}
\end{equation}
Every validation run below uses this metallicity
(\code{initial\_metallicity=0.231}). This gives a direct,
apples-to-apples comparison against the papers' own convention --
without needing to widen the box or search radius, since the front no
longer overshoots enough to approach either default ceiling.

\textbf{The angular scheme only matches the papers at the fast rebuild
cadence.} At \code{rebuild=0.01\,Myr} (this example's default) it

and Figure~\ref{fig:gas20-slices} shows this is a genuine match, not a

close to $r_{\rm HII}=27.72\,$pc, with a visibly intact, symmetric

matching \citeauthor{Smith2021}'s HealPix panel. At
\code{rebuild=0.05\,Myr} the result degrades and scatters
(13.2--20.5\,pc, only one of four stars reaching the reference band),
and at \code{rebuild=0.1\,Myr} it collapses to the same
$\approx$13\,pc as the uniform scheme -- \emph{the angular scheme's
\emph{extent} advantage over uniform disappears at slow rebuild
cadences, not just numerically.} Comparing the \code{rebuild=0.1\,Myr}
slices directly (Figure~\ref{fig:gas20-slices}, right column): uniform
and angular show the \emph{same} overall shape -- a round bubble
flattened right at the clump boundary, $r_{\rm HII}$ within 0.5\,pc of
each other (13.60\,pc vs.\ 14.04\,pc). \textbf{This is a statement
about extent only, not about the clump's own survival}: the direct
clump-mass measurement (Section~\ref{sec:angular-vs-uniform-erosion})
shows angular still protects the clump's mass at least as well as
uniform at every cadence tested here, including this one -- the two
mechanisms (extent growth vs.\ clump protection) are not the same
thing, and only the former is cadence-fragile at this metallicity. The
per-pixel budget floor that protects angular from total clump
monopolization at a fast cadence provides no visible \emph{extent}
benefit once the cadence is slow enough that a starved pixel has
nothing left to spend for most of a rebuild cycle regardless. This
makes physical sense: each of the 12 angular pixels holds only
$\dot N_{\rm ion}/12$ of the budget: at a slow cadence a pixel that
exhausts its small share early has nothing left to spend until the next
rebuild, which is exactly the starvation the fast cadence avoids. A
second, not-yet-tested candidate contributor: at $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$
metal-line cooling is far more efficient than at $Z=0$ (unlike the
tagged/floored interior, gas that has aged out of its tag but not yet
been re-tagged cools on its own, per item e7,
Section~\ref{sec:open}), so a slow rebuild cadence may let more of the
front cool back below the tagging threshold between rebuilds at this
metallicity than it would at $Z=0$ -- consistent with the fast cadence
mattering more here than it appeared to in the earlier $Z=0$ sweep.
Not isolated from the pixel-starvation effect above; both point the
same direction.
\textbf{Conclusion: \code{rebuild=0.01\,Myr} is not just the papers'
own convention -- at this resolution, it is necessary for the angular
scheme to do what it is meant to do.} This is a stronger justification
for this example's existing default than was available before this
matrix.

\emph{bookkeeping artifact}, not a genuinely larger ionized region --
$r_{\rm physics}$ (the temperature-threshold measurement) stays at
$\approx$14\,pc for those same snapshots, matching the visibly compact
bubble in the slice, and matching uniform's own $r_{\rm physics}$ to
within 0.6\,pc (14.23\,pc angular vs.\ 14.85\,pc uniform, both at

the \emph{same} true ionized region here, not a better one for angular;
only $r_{\rm HII}$'s own bookkeeping disagrees. At \code{gas\_mass=95} an angular pixel has too
few nearby candidate particles for its budget to be reliably spent
locally; when a pixel's search radius keeps expanding hunting for
affordable particles, it can eventually tag one isolated, far-away
particle, inflating $r_{\rm HII}=h_{\rm hii}\times\gamma_{\rm kernel}$
without a contiguous ionized region actually extending that far. This
is a distinct failure mode from the previously-documented
$r_{\rm HII}$-\emph{understates}-the-truth case
(Section~\ref{sec:matrix-gas20}) -- here it overstates it, and only for
the angular scheme, only at this coarser resolution.

Figure~\ref{fig:gas95-growth} shows the full per-star trajectories.
\textbf{Conclusion: at \code{gas\_mass=95}, this test does not cleanly
reproduce the papers regardless of scheme or rebuild cadence} -- the
uniform scheme under-ionizes consistently, and the angular scheme's
apparent occasional matches are not real. This is itself a useful
result for production use: it says the D-type test in this exact
geometry is not a reliable calibration point at zoom-in resolution, not
that a particular rebuild cadence is preferred over another there.

\subsection{Is angular better than uniform in general? Direct clump-erosion measurement}
\label{sec:angular-vs-uniform-erosion}

Everything above judges the schemes by the \emph{ionized region's
extent} ($r_{\rm HII}$, $r_{\rm physics}$). That is not what
HealPix splitting was designed to protect
(Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}) -- it was designed to stop the
clump from monopolizing a star's photon budget and, in doing so,
being over-ionized/over-eroded relative to what its own small solid
angle should draw. Extent measurements missed this because a 2D
temperature slice at one $z$-plane does not reliably show
erosion that happens preferentially on the clump's near (star-facing)
hemisphere -- the density contour used in the slice figures throughout
this section can still look intact even when a meaningful fraction of
the clump's own mass has dropped below the density threshold. The
direct measurement is the clump's own gas mass, tracked by
\emph{particle ID}: identify, once, at $t=0$, every gas particle
within 10\,pc of the clump's centre (its nominal radius) with
$n_H>1000\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$ (the clump's own IC density), then at
$t=8\,$Myr look up those exact same particles -- wherever they have
since moved to -- and ask what fraction of their $t=0$ mass is still
above that same density threshold. ``Still dense'' is therefore a
wholesale-declumping metric: it does not register mild dilution, only
a particle actually being taken well below the clump's own density
scale.

Fixing the selection to the original particle IDs matters: an earlier
version of this measurement instead \emph{re-selected} gas by position
and density at $t=8\,$Myr (same 10\,pc, same threshold, recomputed
fresh each time), which implicitly assumes the clump neither drifts
nor has non-clump gas swept into its old spatial footprint. Neither
holds here -- the result was internally inconsistent (the ``far''
hemisphere below showed \emph{more} than 100\% of its $t=0$ mass
remaining under uniform, which is impossible for a fixed set of
particles under mass conservation) and is now replaced throughout this
section with the ID-tracked measurement. A direct sanity check
confirms the fix: summing the $t=8\,$Myr mass of the original
particle IDs, with no density cut at all, returns 100.0\% in every
configuration tested -- no gas particle is created, destroyed, or
lost track of, so any deviation from 100\% in the tables below is
real density evolution on real clump particles, not bookkeeping noise.

\textbf{Angular is never worse than uniform at protecting the clump,
at any configuration tested -- but the effect is modest, not large.}
At $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ both schemes retain essentially all of the
clump's mass (98.9--100.0\%); the two are within 0.2\,pp of each
other at every cadence and resolution, i.e.\ no meaningful difference.
At $Z=0$ a real, consistent gap opens: uniform loses 0.6--2.9\% of the
clump's mass depending on cadence and resolution (worst at
\code{rebuild=0.01\,Myr}), while angular loses at most 0.4\%. The gap
shrinks as the rebuild cadence slows, the same qualitative pattern
seen in the (unrelated, and separately cadence-fragile)
$r_{\rm HII}$-extent measurements of Section~\ref{sec:matrix-gas20}.

This section originally reported much larger numbers here (up to
16.7\% lost under uniform, and an 11--13\% gap even at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$) from the pre-ID-tracking measurement
described above; those numbers were the position-based selection
artifact, not the model's actual behaviour, and are retracted. The
qualitative conclusion they were built on -- angular protects the
clump at least as well as uniform, with a real advantage specifically
at $Z=0$ -- survives the correction; only the claimed magnitude does
not.

\textbf{The hemisphere split is what shows this is a real, mechanism-consistent
effect and not noise.} Mass-biasing (Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting})
is specifically a \emph{directional} failure: one shared budget
over-serves whichever gas happens to be closest, which for this
geometry means the clump's star-facing hemisphere. An isotropic cause
(bulk heating, clump settling) would erode both hemispheres equally; a
directional cause would not. Splitting the same ID-tracked measurement
by the star$\to$clump axis (known exactly -- the clump sits at $+x$
from the star, HealPix \code{nside=1} pixel 4's centre,
Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}) into a near (star-facing) and far
half, at $Z=0$, \code{rebuild=0.01\,Myr}:

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/clump_hemisphere_erosion}
  \caption{Clump mass remaining at $t=8\,$Myr (ID-tracked), split into
    the star-facing (near) and opposite (far) hemisphere, $Z=0$,
    \texttt{rebuild=0.01\,Myr}. $y$-axis starts at 90\%. The far
    hemisphere is unaffected (100.0\%) under both schemes, in both
    resolutions; all of the loss is on the near, star-facing side, and
    is smaller under angular.}
  \label{fig:clump-hemisphere}

The far hemisphere shows \emph{zero} measurable erosion -- exactly
100.0\% in all four cases -- under both schemes, at both resolutions:
gas shielded by the rest of the clump from the star's direct line of
sight is simply never touched, regardless of scheme. All of the
erosion is on the near, star-facing side: uniform loses 4.5--5.9\% of
the near hemisphere's mass, while angular loses only 0.3--0.8\%. That
near/far asymmetry, present only under uniform and much smaller under
angular, is the direct signature of the mass-biasing mechanism working
as described -- and it is direct evidence the effect is real, just an
order of magnitude smaller than the retracted position-based numbers
implied.

\textbf{The radial density profile, done the same ID-tracked way,
cannot separate the two schemes -- which is itself informative.}
Figure~\ref{fig:clump-radial-profile} bins the original clump
particles by their $t=0$ distance from the clump centre and plots the
median $n_H$ of those same particles at $t=0$ against $t=8\,$Myr, for
both schemes, at \code{rebuild=0.01\,Myr}. At every metallicity and
resolution the uniform and angular curves lie essentially on top of
each other throughout the clump's radius -- the aggregate, direction-blind
view dilutes the near/far asymmetry the hemisphere split isolates
above (far, where the two schemes agree completely, dominates the
bin counts at most radii), so it cannot resolve a few-percent,
one-sided effect. This is the same lesson as the retracted
compression narrative in the previous version of this section, arrived
at more carefully: a spatially/radially aggregated diagnostic is not
sensitive enough to test a directional mechanism, which is why the
hemisphere split above, not this profile, is the right tool for that
question. One feature is real and present in \emph{both} schemes
equally at $Z=0$: the median density at small radius sits somewhat
\emph{above} the $t=0$ profile by $t=8\,$Myr. Because it appears
identically under uniform and angular, it does not distinguish the
schemes and is not further interpreted here; a plausible cause is the
over-ionized ambient bubble (common to both schemes at $Z=0$)
pressure-confining the clump, but that is not isolated or tested by
this measurement.

\textbf{Caveat on generality.} All of the above is measured in one
idealized, static, single-clump geometry (Smith et al.\ 2021's own
test problem) with a fixed source and no self-gravity-driven clump
evolution beyond what the radiation itself induces. It demonstrates
that the mass-biasing mechanism angular was built to fix is real and
measurable, and that angular never makes it worse -- it is not a
claim about the size of this effect in a cosmological zoom-in, where
sources, clumps, and gas are all simultaneously moving, multiple
sources overlap, and clump geometry is not a clean sphere at a fixed
offset. Whether the few-percent, near-hemisphere effect measured here
matters at zoom-in resolution is a separate question this idealized
test cannot answer.

\subsection{The $Z=0$ matrix (this example's default) -- and why $r_{\rm physics}$ is not trustworthy here}
\label{sec:matrix-z0}

The two matrices above isolate the algorithm from the metallicity
effects in Section~\ref{sec:metallicity} on purpose. For comparison,
here is the same $2\times2\times3$ sweep (both resolutions, both
schemes, all three rebuild cadences) at $Z=0$, this example's actual
default -- deliberately \emph{without} widening
\code{Stars:max\_HII\_search\_radius} or the box, so some rows are
expected to run into the 45\,pc/50\,pc-half-width ceiling; that is an
accepted limitation of this comparison, not a new bug.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{llrrr}
  \hline
  & scheme & rebuild=0.01\,Myr & rebuild=0.05\,Myr & rebuild=0.1\,Myr \\
  \hline

\textbf{Two distinct, additive effects are at play here, not one.}
First, a genuine budget effect: at $Z=0$ the temperature floor
(Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}) holds ionized gas at
$T_{\rm collisional}\approx47{,}500\,$K, and
Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature}'s table shows
$\alpha_B(47{,}500\,{\rm K})$ is $\approx$75\% below
$\alpha_B(10^4\,{\rm K})$ (the value effectively used, via the
temperature floor, at $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$). Since the recombination
cost charged per particle (Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}) scales linearly
with $\beta$, a star's fixed photon budget can hold roughly $4\times$
as many particles ionized simultaneously at $Z=0$ as at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ purely from this coefficient, independent of
any cooling physics -- this alone predicts systematically larger
$r_{\rm HII}$ at $Z=0$, exactly what the table above shows relative to
Sections~\ref{sec:matrix-gas20}--\ref{sec:matrix-gas95}. Second, a
cooling-efficiency effect on top of it: \textbf{unlike at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$, the angular scheme's advantage over uniform
does not collapse at slow rebuild cadences here} -- at
\code{gas\_mass=20}, \code{rebuild=0.1\,Myr} still reaches
31.9--41.2\,pc, comparable to or above \code{rebuild=0.05\,Myr}, not
degraded down to the uniform scheme's $\approx$16\,pc the way it was at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ (Section~\ref{sec:matrix-gas20}). The lower
$\beta$ alone does not explain this specific pattern (it would inflate
all rebuild cadences roughly proportionally, not selectively rescue the
slow ones) -- this part is consistent with the cooling-efficiency
hypothesis instead: at $Z=0$, cooling is inefficient enough that the
pixel-starvation effect is less punishing, because gas that was ionized
earlier stays hot for longer regardless of whether its tag has been
refreshed. The uniform scheme itself is also not yet converged at
$t=8\,$Myr, unlike at $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ where it plateaus by
$t\sim3$--4\,Myr -- Figure~\ref{fig:gas20-z0-growth} shows all six
\code{gas\_mass=20} trajectories still rising at the end of the run.

\textbf{A more important finding: $r_{\rm physics}$ is not a reliable
diagnostic at $Z=0$.} Figure~\ref{fig:z0-vs-z0231-slice} compares the
same configuration (\code{gas\_mass=20}, uniform, rebuild=0.01\,Myr) at
both metallicities. At $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ (left), the background
outside the bubble is completely black -- efficient metal-line cooling
returns any perturbed gas to the ambient $10^3\,$K background quickly,
so $T>10^4\,$K cleanly isolates the photoionized region and $r_{\rm
physics}=14.74\,$pc closely tracks $r_{\rm HII}=14.28\,$pc. At $Z=0$
(right), the \emph{entire box} glows well above the background
temperature -- $r_{\rm physics}=44.87\,$pc, more than double
$r_{\rm HII}=21.25\,$pc, and this pattern repeats for every $Z=0$
configuration tested (see the $r_{\rm HII}$/$r_{\rm physics}$ values
printed by \code{plot\_temperature\_hii.py} for each run). This is not
a bookkeeping artifact of the kind found in
Section~\ref{sec:matrix-gas95} (an isolated far tagged particle) --
the heating is genuinely widespread. The most likely explanation:
inefficient cooling at $Z=0$ lets gas heated by the D-type shock's
swept-up shell, or gas that cooled only partway after its ionization
tag expired, stay above the $10^4\,$K threshold far from the actual
photoionized front and for a long time, exactly the mechanism raised
as a candidate for the angular scheme's rebuild-cadence insensitivity
above. \textbf{Practical consequence: at $Z=0$, $r_{\rm HII}$ (despite
its own known limitations, Section~\ref{sec:matrix-gas20}) is the more
trustworthy of the two diagnostics; $r_{\rm physics}$ should not be
used to validate $Z=0$ runs against the papers' reference without
correcting for this contamination first -- not attempted here.}

\subsection{D-type growth curve shape (e10)}
\label{sec:e10}

The matrix above compares only final-time values. \citet{Spitzer1978}'s
classical D-type expansion solution gives the full trajectory, not just
the endpoint:
\begin{equation}
  R(t) = R_{\rm st}\left(1 + \frac{7 c_s t}{4 R_{\rm st}}\right)^{4/7},
  \label{eq:dtype}
\end{equation}
where $R_{\rm st}$ is the equilibrium Str\"omgren radius and $c_s$ is
the isothermal sound speed of the ionized gas. \code{stromgren\_\allowbreak
analytic\_check.py} (sibling \code{StromgrenSphere} example)
reimplements this directly from the code's own $\dot N_{\rm ion}(m)$
fit (not an independently-guessed photon budget), using this run's
actual applied ionized temperature for both $\alpha_B$ and $c_s$
(Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}) rather than the classical $10^4\,$K
reference point, since using the latter would systematically
under-predict the analytic curve for any $Z=0$ run and make a real
match look like a mismatch.

A single-star run (no clump, no co-location, matching e10's
requirement) needed a box wide enough to contain the equilibrium
radius and a \code{time\_end} that stays within the star's own
ionizing lifetime -- the example's own defaults (25\,pc box
half-width, $R_{\rm st}=25.4\,$pc for the default
$29.7\,{\rm M}_\odot$ star) do not satisfy the first condition, so
this run uses \code{boxsize=0.08} (80\,pc) via a new \code{run.sh}
override added for this check. Metallicity matters here too: at this
example's own $Z=0$ default, the corrected (Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature})
temperature-dependent $\alpha_B$ pushes $R_{\rm st}$ to 40.5\,pc, larger
than even the widened 40\,pc box half-width -- so this check runs at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ (the papers' own $10^4\,$K convention,
Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}), which keeps $R_{\rm st}=25.4\,$pc
comfortably inside the box. \code{time\_end=5.5e-3} ($\approx$5.4\,Myr)
also required lowering \code{dt\_max} to \code{1e-3} via a new
\code{run.sh} override -- the example's default \code{dt\_max=1e-2}
exceeds a \code{time\_end} shortened this far, and
\code{engine\_config()} correctly refuses to start rather than
silently accept it.

\textbf{Methodology correction (2026-07-18): the comparison point
itself was wrong.} \code{stromgren\_analytic\_check.py} originally
compared at the run's final simulated time regardless of whether that
time was still physically meaningful. But the analytic D-type curve
(Eq.~\ref{eq:dtype}) starts at $R_{\rm st}$ and only grows from
there, so it exceeds this run's 40\,pc box half-width from
$t\approx1.37\,$Myr onward -- past that point the comparison is
periodic-box vs.\ infinite-medium, not a code check. The
41.7\%/$t\approx3.8\,$Myr figures previously reported here were
measured at $t=5.38\,$Myr, deep in that invalid regime, and are
superseded below. The script now picks the \emph{last} snapshot that
is both box-valid ($r_{\rm analytic}\le$ box half-width) and
physically alive ($r_{\rm HII}>0$, so a dead star's bookkeeping reset
to 0 is not mistaken for the region shrinking), and reports the
comparison there instead.

\textbf{Result: FAIL -- the growth curve's \emph{shape} does not
match the D-type solution, not just its endpoint.}
Figure~\ref{fig:e10-dtype} shows $r_{\rm HII}(t)$ against the analytic
curve. At the last box-valid time ($t=1.27\,$Myr), the relative error
is 75.2\% (9.8\,pc simulated vs.\ 39.4\,pc analytic) -- a clear fail
against the default 30\% tolerance, and the shape mismatch is more
informative than that one number: the simulated curve is a
\emph{staircase}, flat between rebuilds and jumping at each one, while
the analytic curve is smooth and already past $R_{\rm st}$ from very
early times. The simulated curve does not first cross $R_{\rm
st}=25.4\,$pc until $t\approx3.3\,$Myr -- past the point where the box
itself is even still valid for the analytic curve -- whereas the
D-type solution reaches $R_{\rm st}$ essentially at $t=0$ by
construction (the classical solution assumes the R-type phase that
establishes $R_{\rm st}$ is effectively instantaneous, then D-type
expansion proceeds from there).

\textbf{Likely mechanism: per-rebuild search-radius growth is
geometrically bounded, not radius-following.} This run's
\code{used\_parameters.yml} confirms \code{HII\_region\_rebuild\_time\_Myr=0.5},
\code{max\_HII\_radius\_expansion\_tries=5}, and
\code{HII\_radius\_expansion\_factor=1.1} -- so within a single
rebuild's pass, the search radius can expand at most
$1.1^5\approx1.61\times$ beyond its starting point
(Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}'s retry logic,
\code{runner\_dosub\_stars\_hii\_ionization\_feedback}), and the
starting point for the \emph{next} rebuild is set from
\code{h\_hii\_max} achieved at the \emph{end} of the current one. The
observed step sizes (0$\to$5, 5$\to$10, 10$\to$19, 19$\to$24, 24$\to$36\,pc)
are consistent with this compounding, capped-per-cycle growth, and the
occasional multi-cycle plateaus (e.g.\ stuck at 24\,pc for
$\sim$3 rebuild cycles) are consistent with cycles where the
buffer-retry path (Stars:max\_HII\_retry\_full\_buffer) fired instead
of the radius-expansion path, making no radius progress that cycle.
This is a plausible, parameter-grounded explanation, not a fully
diagnosed root cause -- confirming it would need per-rebuild
instrumentation of which retry branch fires and why (not done here).
\textbf{The practical implication: an HII region's growth toward its
equilibrium size is retry/rebuild-cadence-limited, not
recombination-physics-limited, for a significant fraction of its early
growth.} At this example's own \code{rebuild\_time\_myr=0.5\,Myr} that
delay is several Myr; a faster rebuild cadence (e.g.\ this project's
usual \code{0.01\,Myr}) would shrink but not eliminate it, since the
$1.61\times$-per-cycle cap is unrelated to the cadence itself. Recorded
as a new open item (T8, Section~\ref{sec:open}) rather than fixed
here.

\textbf{T11 verification: adaptive cadence measurably shrinks the
delay, but does not eliminate the staircase shape.} Re-ran the
identical setup (same box, same star, same $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$,
same \code{time\_end}/\code{dt\_max}) with
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence=1} and
\code{HII\_region\_rebuild\_safety\_factor=0.2} in place of the fixed
\code{rebuild\_time\_myr=0.5}. Figure~\ref{fig:e10-dtype} overlays both
curves. Two numbers, holding everything else fixed:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Time to first reach $R_{\rm st}=25.4\,$pc:}
    $1.76\,$Myr (adaptive) vs.\ $3.32\,$Myr (fixed) -- a 47\% reduction.
  \item \textbf{Relative error at the last box-valid time
    ($t=1.27\,$Myr):} 41.5\% (adaptive) vs.\ 75.2\% (fixed) -- roughly
    half the error at the one time both curves can be legitimately
    compared to the infinite-medium analytic solution.
\end{itemize}
Both curves remain a staircase (adaptive rebuilds far more often, but
each individual rebuild is still subject to the same
$1.1^5\approx1.61\times$-per-cycle search-radius cap identified above
as the likely mechanism), and both eventually run into the box's
periodic wrap and plateau near 40\,pc rather than tracking the analytic
curve's continued rise -- T8's mechanism is structurally independent of
the rebuild cadence, so this result is exactly what T8's own analysis
predicted: adaptive cadence attacks \emph{how often} the search radius
gets a chance to grow, not the \emph{per-attempt} growth cap, so it
narrows the delay without closing it. \textbf{Verdict: T11 delivers a
real, quantified improvement and is safe to recommend as the default
for future runs that care about early-time accuracy, but does not by
itself resolve T8} -- raising
\code{max\_HII\_radius\_expansion\_tries}/\code{HII\_radius\_expansion\_factor}
remains the more direct fix for the shape mismatch itself, and is
still open.

\textbf{Measurement correction (2026-07-18): $r_{\rm HII}$ itself was
being undercounted.} \code{HIIRegionRadii} ($h_{\rm hii}$) is the
distance at the moment a particle was tagged, recorded once and never
updated as that particle moves afterwards. A direct check confirmed
tagged gas keeps moving outward substantially after being tagged (mean
radial velocity several times that of untagged gas, growing with time),
so $h_{\rm hii}$ systematically lags the true current extent of the
ionized/expanding shell. \code{stromgren\_analytic\_check.py} now
instead takes the current (periodic-wrapped) position of every gas
particle a star has \emph{ever} tagged, using \code{HIIStarIDs} (set
once at tagging and, unlike \code{IsIonizedFlags}, never reset when the
tag later expires) rather than $h_{\rm hii}$. On the adaptive-cadence
run this drops the comparison-point error from 41.5\% to 32.4\% and the
time to first reach $R_{\rm st}$ from 1.76 to 1.17\,Myr. On the
fixed-cadence run the comparison-point error is unchanged at 75.2\%
(tagging is too infrequent for tagged gas to have drifted far by
$t=1.27\,$Myr) but time-to-$R_{\rm st}$ still improves, 3.32 to
2.44\,Myr. This is a measurement fix, not a physics change -- it does
not alter T8's underlying mechanism or verdict, but shows the earlier
numbers above understated how much of the true ionized shell had
already formed. Figure~\ref{fig:e10-dtype} has been regenerated with
the corrected ever-tagged measure (the adaptive-cadence curve is also
now from the run with the Grackle-bypass cooling fix applied, which
holds tagged particles exactly at the ionized-energy floor instead of
letting Grackle's own chemistry solve pull them back down within the
same step) -- both curves are visibly smoother than the original
$h_{\rm hii}$-based
staircase, since ever-tagged gas keeps contributing its continued
outward drift between rebuilds instead of only jumping at each one, but
the qualitative picture (adaptive well ahead of fixed, both eventually
running into the box's periodic wrap) is unchanged.

\textbf{Update (2026-07-20): $T_i=10^4\,$K, matching
\citet{Hu2017}/\citet{Smith2021}'s own convention, combined with the
loosened cap and adaptive cadence.} All comparisons above use
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$'s natural, metallicity-dependent
$T_i\approx47500\,$K (Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}). Rerunning the same
loosened-cap, adaptive-cadence, bigger-box setup with
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}
forcing the papers' own $T_i$ directly (same mechanism as the STARBENCH
check, Section~\ref{sec:starbench}) gives the first \textbf{PASS} on this
check: 27.74\% error at the box-valid comparison time ($t=3.81\,$Myr,
later than the earlier $T_i\approx47500\,$K comparisons since the lower
$T_i$ shrinks $R_{\rm st}$ to 25.4\,pc, taking longer to reach the
60\,pc box half-width). The curve's shape is also qualitatively
different for the first time: a sharp jump to $\approx$18\,pc by
$t\approx0.35\,$Myr, a brief plateau right at $R_{\rm st}$ from
$t\approx1$--$2\,$Myr, then a continued D-type climb that tracks the
analytic slope reasonably closely -- the R-type-then-D-type qualitative
behaviour the analytic solution itself has, not seen in any
$T_i\approx47500\,$K run this session. This does not by itself prove
$T_i$ was the missing factor from T8's earlier ``both known factors
exhausted, residual gap unexplained'' verdict -- three things changed at
once (cap, cadence, $T_i$) and were not tested pairwise against $T_i$
alone -- but it is the first configuration in this whole investigation
to pass the check at all, and is recorded here as the strongest current
result pending a cleaner isolation of which combination is doing the
work.

\textbf{Isolation follow-up (2026-07-20).} Two further runs, same box
and $T_i=10^4\,$K, each changing exactly one thing relative to the PASS
run above:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{$T_i$ alone, cap/cadence back to their defaults}
    (\code{HII\_max\_radius\_expansion\_tries=5},
    \code{HII\_radius\_expansion\_factor=1.1},
    \code{HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence=0}): 43.02\% error, FAIL --
    barely different from the historical natural-$T_i$ default-cap/
    cadence runs ($\sim$40--42\%), and the curve reverts to the same
    staircase shape (Figure~\ref{fig:e10-ti1e4-isolation}) seen
    throughout this investigation at default settings. $T_i$ by itself
    is not the driver of the PASS result -- the loosened cap and
    adaptive cadence are doing essentially all of the work.
  \item \emph{$Z=0$ (pristine, not $Z/Z_\odot=0.231$), loosened cap and
    adaptive cadence kept on}, with the STARBENCH-style
    \code{SPH:minimal\_temperature=1000}/\code{initial\_temperature=1000}

    $Z/Z_\odot=0.231$'s metal-line cooling was suppressing growth
    relative to a pristine medium; if anything, the pristine run is
    slightly slower.
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Current picture:} of the three variables varied this session
(search-radius cap, rebuild cadence, $T_i$/metallicity), the cap and
cadence fixes account for essentially all of the improvement seen; $T_i$
and metallicity are, at most, second-order effects at this box/
resolution, and lowering metallicity to zero does not help. The residual
gap between the PASS run (27.74\%) and the analytic solution remains
otherwise unexplained.

\subsection{STARBENCH: validating the physics, not a paper's implementation}
\label{sec:starbench}

\code{examples/SubgridTests/StromgrenSphereStarbench/} reproduces the
STARBENCH project's late-phase D-type expansion test
\citep[Section~4.1.2]{Bisbas2015}: a single ionizing source
($\dot N_{\rm ion}=10^{49}\,{\rm s^{-1}}$, star mass 26.75\,M$_\odot$ at
this code's own $\dot N_{\rm ion}(M)$ fit, Section~\ref{sec:blackbody})
at the centre of a uniform, pure-hydrogen medium
($n_H\approx3115\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$), purely hydrodynamical (no gravity,
no magnetic fields, matching the paper's own test exactly). Unlike
Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}--\ref{sec:e10}'s comparisons, the reference
here is not one paper's own subgrid implementation but STARBENCH's
semi-empirical blend of two independently-derived ODEs (Eqns 8, 11, 28,
29 of the paper): the exact pressure-driven thin-shell equation
(``Raga-I''), and \citet{HosokawaInutsuka2006}'s shell-inertia equation
(``Raga-II''), combined with a time-dependent weight
$f_{\rm SB}(t)=1-0.733\,e^{-t/{\rm 1\,Myr}}$ that interpolates between
them. This blend was tuned against, and agrees with, twelve
independently-implemented radiation-hydrodynamics codes to
$\sim$1--2\% -- a test of whether this code's D-type expansion physics
is right in general, not of whether it matches one specific paper's own
approximations.

\textbf{Temperature convention.} STARBENCH assumes a flat
$T_i=10^4\,$K (ionized) / $T_o=10^3\,$K (neutral) two-temperature
medium by construction -- an idealization no participating code
actually integrates through a real cooling network. This model does
not have that option (gas cools/heats via ordinary Grackle physics), so
two configurations were tested.

\textbf{First attempt (failed): $Z/Z_\odot=0.231$, unmodified neutral-gas
floor.} To hit $T_i=10^4\,$K via this model's own metallicity-dependent
collisional floor (Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}), the first attempt used
$Z/Z_\odot=0.231$ and left \code{SPH:minimal\_temperature} at its usual
value of 1\,K, expecting the gap between ``neutral gas cools/heats
freely'' and STARBENCH's ``neutral gas held fixed at $T_o$'' to be a
minor, second-order effect. It was not: a direct check found untagged
gas sitting at 2--5\,K from the very first snapshot onward, not
anywhere near $T_o=1000\,$K -- real metal-line cooling at
$Z/Z_\odot=0.231$ (absent from STARBENCH's literal pure-H medium),
combined with no floor to arrest it. A collapsed $T_o$ collapses $c_o$,
and since $R_{\rm STAG}\propto(c_i/c_o)^{4/3}$ (the paper's own
Eqn~14), this removes essentially all the pressure confinement the
late-phase stagnation radius depends on: instead of levelling off, the
simulated front grew straight through the STARBENCH prediction and kept
going (Fig.~\ref{fig:starbench-beforefix}, $r_{\rm sim}=5.96\,$pc vs.\
$R_{\rm SB}=2.25\,$pc predicted at $t=3\,$Myr, a 165\% miss).

  \caption{STARBENCH check, first attempt: $Z/Z_\odot=0.231$

\textbf{Fix: \code{SPH:minimal\_temperature=1000}.} Floors the neutral
gas near $T_o$ without forcing a full isothermal equation of state --
gas can still heat freely via compression/shocks, it simply cannot cool
below this floor. This is the same fix later found to matter for the
Hu/Smith clump examples too (item~T7's neighbouring bug hunts;
Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}'s discussion of why every other example
in this section avoids $Z=0$ specifically to sidestep needing this same
floor).

\textbf{Validated result: $T_i=10^4\,$K forced directly, matching the
paper's own convention exactly.} Rather than continue reasoning about
$Z/Z_\odot=0.231$'s approximate match to $10^4\,$K, this run used
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}
(decoupling $T_i$ from metallicity entirely, CLAUDE.md) together with
the \code{minimal\_temperature=1000} fix above -- the literal STARBENCH
convention, not a workaround. Result
(Fig.~\ref{fig:starbench-ti1e4}): $r_{\rm sim}=2.366\,$pc vs.\
$R_{\rm SB}=2.247\,$pc at $t=3\,$Myr (the full run -- $R_{\rm SB}(t)$
stays inside this example's 5\,pc box half-width for the entire 3\,Myr,
unlike the natural-$T_i$ case), a \textbf{5.30\% error, PASS} against
this example's 10\% tolerance (tighter than the classical Spitzer
check's 30\%, since STARBENCH's own claimed accuracy is $\sim$1--2\%).
Beyond the single comparison point, the simulated curve tracks
STARBENCH's characteristic \emph{non-monotonic} shape closely across
the whole window -- rising to a local overshoot near $t\approx1\,$Myr,
dipping, then re-settling -- a shape the naive Raga-I curve alone does
not have; matching it is a qualitatively stronger check than matching
one number at one time would be on its own.

\textbf{What this does and does not establish.} This is the strongest
single-source validation result in this logbook: unlike the Hu/Smith
comparisons (which inherit whatever approximations those papers'
own subgrid schemes make) or the classical-Spitzer e10 check
(Section~\ref{sec:e10}, which compares against a formula STARBENCH's
own paper shows \emph{overpredicts} the true late-phase solution), this
is a direct match to a consensus, code-independent physical solution.
It validates the single-source photon-budget, temperature-floor, and
momentum-coupling physics (Sections~\ref{sec:blackbody}--\ref{sec:algorithm})
in the regime this test covers. It does \emph{not} by itself validate
the multi-source angular-splitting machinery
(Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}) or explain the Hu/Smith
multi-source residual (Section~\ref{sec:metallicity} onward) --
STARBENCH is a single-star test, and V13 (item, Section~\ref{sec:open})
already found that forcing $T_i=10^4\,$K helped this single-source case
dramatically while leaving the Hu/Smith multi-source error essentially
unchanged, i.e.\ this result and the still-open multi-source residual
are consistent with each other, not in tension. \textbf{Also not yet
done}: validating this example's own \emph{default}, committed
configuration ($Z=0$, natural $T_i\approx47{,}500\,$K via
Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}, with the \code{minimal\_temperature=1000}
fix applied) against STARBENCH at its own natural temperature -- only
the $T_i=10^4\,$K forced variant has been validated so far. Flagged as
item~T21, Section~\ref{sec:open}.
