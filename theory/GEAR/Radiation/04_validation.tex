\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

This project validates against three published targets, listed in
Table~\ref{tab:validation-targets}. STARBENCH \citep{Bisbas2015} is a
single-source D-type expansion test where twelve independent,
differently-implemented radiation-hydrodynamics codes agree with each
other to $\sim$1--2\% -- a direct test of whether this code reproduces
the actual physics of D-type expansion, not just one paper's own
subgrid scheme. Hu et al.\ (2017)/Smith et al.\ (2021) 4-star and the
Smith et al.\ (2021) clump target instead reproduce those papers' own
specific multi-source tests and reported numbers. Section~\ref{sec:t5-validation}
separately checks the Grackle rate-coupled photoionization scheme (T5) --
neither paper tests rate-coupling, so that check is a self-consistency/
robustness validation against basic photoionization equilibrium physics,
not a fourth published target.

\textbf{2026-07-27 update}: a cadence sweep (varying
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_rebuild\_time\_Myr} at fixed resolution) found the
per-pass ionizing-photon budget was cadence-\emph{dependent} -- a
rate-vs-rate comparison with no time integration, and already-ionized gas
invisible to the budget entirely. Root-caused and fixed with a photon-count
budget integrated over the elapsed interval (Section~\ref{sec:husmith-current}
below); STARBENCH and Hu/Smith were both re-checked against the fix. The
pre-fix numbers this document previously reported are moved to
Section~\ref{sec:deprecated-2026-07-27} rather than deleted, since they
remain a valid record of what was true of the code at the time.

\subsection{Metallicity and the ionized-gas temperature floor}
\label{sec:metallicity}

Both reference papers are effectively zero-metallicity (\citet{Hu2017}
state their D-type test medium is ``composed of pure hydrogen''; neither
paper's recombination rate or temperature floor has a metallicity term
at all) and clamp ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K. This code's own
temperature floor (Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}) is metallicity-\emph{dependent}:
at $Z=0$ the applied temperature is $\approx$47,500\,K, not $10^4\,$K,
which raises $r_{\rm HII}$ through two independent channels -- a lower
case-B recombination coefficient (Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature})
and a higher D-type driving sound speed ($c_s\propto\sqrt T$).

Both channels are neutralized by construction at the metallicity where
$T_{\rm collisional}=10^4\,$K exactly (Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{0.86}{1+0.22\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)} = 1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z/Z_\odot \approx 0.231.
  \label{eq:z0231}
\end{equation}
All three validation targets instead use
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}
(forces $T_i$ directly, no metallicity side effects on cooling), which
matches the papers' own flat convention exactly. Each example's own
README documents the flag.

\subsection{Hu et al.\ (2017)/Smith et al.\ (2021), 4-star}
\label{sec:husmith-current}

\textbf{Cadence-independence PASS; absolute accuracy resolved as
reference-curve systematics (Section~\ref{sec:husmith-resolution}): the
simulation is bracketed by the two published reference conventions --
median 7.1\% against the Bisbas blend, 17--18\% against Raga-II but
converging to 7.1\% at $t_{\rm end}$.} This target is where the
2026-07-27 cadence-dependence bug was actually found: a diagnostic
sweep varying \code{GEARFeedback:HII\_rebuild\_time\_Myr} at fixed
resolution showed the simulated $r_{\rm HII}(t)$ curve depended on how
often the region was rebuilt -- a numerical parameter, not a physical
one, must not change the answer. Root cause
(\code{feedback\_iact\_HII\_ionization}, \code{feedback\_common.c}): the
per-pixel budget compared a bare \emph{rate} (a candidate's steady-state
recombination need) against a bare \emph{rate} budget
(\code{dot\_N\_ion\_pix}), with no time interval anywhere, and
already-ionized gas was charged nothing to stay ionized -- every pass
handed out a full rate's worth of photons regardless of elapsed time,
and the ionized volume could grow without bound as the cadence was
refined.

\textbf{Fix} (commits \code{1de90f62d}--\code{9d585c42e}): a photon
\emph{count} budget, \code{N\_ion\_budget\_pix} = (emission rate)
$\times\, \Delta t_{\rm back}$ (the interval since this star's previous
rebuild pass), spent in two phases each pass -- Phase~1 charges
recombination upkeep for gas already held ionized (budget-gated, so a
region can now genuinely recede, Section~\ref{sec:starbench-current}),
Phase~2 claims new candidates against what Phase~1 left, including a
one-off $N_H$ cost (stripping a fresh candidate's electrons) that the
pre-fix code omitted entirely. The $N_H+\Delta t_{\rm back}\times{\rm
rate}$ claim cost is exactly backward Euler on
$dS/dt=(Q-S)/t_{\rm rec}$, unconditionally stable at the $\Delta t
\gg t_{\rm rec}$ the default cadence produces. A per-pixel photon
overdraft (the one boundary particle a pass cannot fully afford) is now
carried forward as debt rather than forgiven every pass, and the
probabilistic-boundary RNG roll is keyed per (star, pixel) rather than
per candidate, both closing separate routes back to the same
cadence-dependence failure mode.

\textbf{Cadence-independence result}: six fixed values of
\code{HII\_rebuild\_time\_Myr} -- 0.5, 0.1, 0.03, 0.01, 0.003, and
0.001\,Myr --
(\code{HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence=0} forced, \code{gas\_mass=20},
$123{,}571$ particles), checked against Raga-II with the correct
reference temperature (\code{hu\_smith\_analytic\_check.py} with
\texttt{--T-ionized-K 1e4} -- see the caveat below):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
Cadence [Myr] & median error & min & max \\
\hline
0.5   & 31.75\% & 18.09\% & 47.56\% \\
0.1   & 20.49\% &  1.55\% & 27.83\% \\
0.03  & 18.07\% &  0.47\% & 67.33\% \\
0.01  & 18.41\% &  0.13\% & 39.20\% \\
0.003 & 17.28\% &  1.45\% & 67.35\% \\
0.001 & 17.34\% &  0.15\% & 91.06\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
The four finest cadences agree with each other to within
$\sim$1 percentage point -- cadence-independence achieved. The 0.5 and,
to a lesser extent, 0.1\,Myr points sit higher: at coarse cadence the
per-pass budget (rate $\times\Delta t_{\rm back}$) can exceed what a
single pass can actually spend, bounded by \code{Stars:HII\_max\_ngbs}
and its retry/radius-expansion ceiling -- a separate, known scope limit
(unspent photons past that ceiling are discarded, biasing coarse
cadence toward undershoot), not a budget-arithmetic error. This is
expected to bind less at production's coarser mass resolution
(fewer gas neighbours per HII region than this fine-resolution
idealized test), a question for the planned
IsolatedGalaxy/HomogeneousBox tests, not this idealized one.

\textbf{Caveats on the absolute-accuracy number}: two, both making the
17--18\% figure \emph{not} directly comparable to the pre-redesign
6.89\% (Section~\ref{sec:deprecated-2026-07-27}). (1) \emph{Reference
temperature}: \code{hu\_smith\_analytic\_check.py}'s
\texttt{--T-ionized-K}
defaults to $47{,}500\,$K (this code's natural, no-debug-flag $Z=0$
value), not the $10^4\,$K the sweep's build actually used
(\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4},
matching this example's own documented validated configuration and
README) -- omitting \texttt{--T-ionized-K 1e4} silently compared the
simulation against the wrong reference curve and was responsible for
essentially all of an initially-measured $\sim$60\% median error; the
numbers above already include the fix. As of commit \code{d9a371dd6}
this failure mode is closed at the source: all three checker scripts
now \emph{measure} $T_i$ from the run's own tagged gas by default and
warn loudly when an explicit \texttt{--T-ionized-K} disagrees with the
measurement by more than 10\%, so the same check command is correct for
any build. (2) \emph{Resolution and
cadence mode}: this sweep ran at \code{gas\_mass=20} ($123{,}571$
particles), the coarser of the two resolutions this document has
tested (Section~\ref{sec:deprecated-2026-07-27} notes \code{gas\_mass=20}
already degraded late-time accuracy under the pre-redesign code, at
fixed cadence), and forced \code{HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence=0} to
isolate cadence as the only varying quantity -- not the then-validated
\code{gas\_mass=4}/adaptive-cadence default. \textbf{Done in Phase 4b}
(Section~\ref{sec:husmith-4b} below): a like-for-like re-validation at
\code{gas\_mass=4}, now fixed cadence (adaptive was removed in Phase 4c).

\subsubsection{Multi-source ownership: investigated, not the dominant
effect at this cadence}
\label{sec:husmith-ownership}

The 4 sources here are exactly co-located (Hu et al.\ 2017's own test
setup); ownership of a gas particle is first-come-first-served
(\code{star\_id} written at tag time, checked by
\code{radiation\_is\_part\_tagged\_as\_ionized(pj,xpj) \&\&
radiation\_get\_part\_ionized\_star\_id(pj,xpj)==star\_id}), so an
unfairness between the 4 owners was a natural suspect for part of the
residual above. Checked directly: each star's own
\code{HIIRegionRadii}/\code{HIIRegionMasses} in the $0.001\,$Myr run,
across the four sources, at $t=0.98,2.93,4.89,8.00\,$Myr:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\hline
$t$ [Myr] & \multicolumn{4}{c}{$h_{\rm HII}$ [pc], one column per star} & spread \\
\hline
0.98 & 9.0 & 9.1 & 10.1 & 8.6 & 15.6\% \\
2.93 & 14.3 & 14.0 & 13.9 & 13.9 & 2.9\% \\
4.89 & 17.3 & 17.9 & 18.0 & 17.8 & 4.2\% \\
8.00 (final) & 24.1 & 24.0 & 24.1 & 24.2 & 0.9\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
(spread = $(\max-\min)/{\rm mean}$ across the four stars.) The four
co-located stars track each other closely throughout, and the spread
between them \emph{narrows} over time -- 15.6\% at the earliest
snapshot checked (small radii, small-number statistics) down to under
1\% by $t=8\,$Myr -- with no single star systematically lagging or
racing ahead over $\sim$8000 rebuild passes. \textbf{This argues against a
large, dominant ownership-fairness pathology at this (fine, converged)
cadence.} A subtler, cadence-coupled mechanism remains plausible but is
\textbf{not yet isolated}: within one pass the 4 co-located stars are
processed sequentially (not concurrently), sharing the same cell and
the same \code{interaction\_limit}, but each one's \emph{own} Phase~2
gather must search past every earlier star's already-claimed inner
shell within the same fixed \code{Stars:HII\_max\_ngbs}/retry budget --
a later-processed star is specifically more exposed to the reach
ceiling than an equivalent single source would be. This would worsen
exactly where the reach ceiling is already known to bind (coarse
cadence), consistent with the observed trend (31.75\% at $0.5\,$Myr
vs.\ 17.34\% at $0.001\,$Myr) -- but is not yet separated from the
already-understood single-source reach-ceiling effect
(Section~\ref{sec:starbench-current} has no such effect to compare
against, being single-source) as a distinct, quantified contributor.

\subsubsection{Absolute residual resolved: reference-curve systematics
  (2026-07-27)}
\label{sec:husmith-resolution}

The converged $\sim$17--18\% median against Raga-II is \textbf{not a
code deficiency}: it is dominated by the unmodeled R-type onset plus
the band where the two published reference conventions themselves
disagree. Every code-side suspect was eliminated by direct measurement
on the $0.01\,$Myr-cadence snapshots:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Tag churn (the lapse/reclaim $N_H$ re-charge sink)}: 59
  lapse events over the entire $8\,$Myr run, a negligible fraction of
  the emitted photons. The Phase-C1 neutral-content pricing item in the
  fix plan therefore will \emph{not} move this target; it stands on
  production-regime grounds only.
\item \emph{Front granularity}: the radial gap between the outermost
  tagged particle and the next untagged candidate is $0.00$--$0.07\,$pc
  at all times -- the dense swept-up shell crowds particles at the
  front, so the measured radius is not resolution-quantized.
\item \emph{Bookkeeping sanity}: the final tagged count (340 particles,
  $\simeq 6800\,M_\odot$) matches the Spitzer rarefied-interior ionized
  mass for a $24\,$pc region at this density.
\item \emph{Ownership fairness}: Section~\ref{sec:husmith-ownership}.
\item \emph{Backward-Euler growth transient}: vanishes as $\Delta t
  \to 0$, yet the converged cadences share the residual -- excluded.
\end{itemize}

With those ruled out, the checker was re-run against every reference
convention it implements ($0.01\,$Myr cadence, $T_i=10^4\,$K):

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
Reference & median & $t_{\rm end}$ error & within 10\% tol \\
\hline
Raga-I (Eqn~8)          &  6.60\% & 21.21\% & 56/82 \\
Raga-II (Eqn~11)        & 18.44\% &  7.09\% & 12/82 \\
Bisbas+15 blend (28--29) &  7.12\% & 21.20\% & 53/82 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The simulated $r_{\rm HII}(t)$ overshoots all references during the
R-type onset (up to $\sim$39\%, as the papers' own simulations do),
crosses Raga-II at $\sim$0.8\,Myr, tracks the Bisbas blend to 3--6\%
through the mid-window -- exactly where the ``sag'' against Raga-II
sits, and where the two conventions disagree by 15--25\% -- then rises
toward Raga-II late, ending 7.1\% below it at $8\,$Myr. The simulation
is \textbf{bracketed by the published references over the whole
post-onset run}, i.e.\ it agrees with them to within their own
systematic spread. This also explains the single-source asymmetry:
STARBENCH (Section~\ref{sec:starbench-current}) passes at 2.51\%
because the blend \emph{is} the reference calibrated for exactly that
configuration, whereas its time-dependent weight does not transfer to
this 4-source case and no exact published answer exists between
$\sim$20 and $\sim$26\,pc.

\textbf{Verdict: pass-with-caveat; no code fix indicated by this
target.} A sharper criterion would require comparing against the
papers' own multi-code simulation band (Smith et al.\ 2021, Fig.~1)
rather than an ODE. As of commit \code{d9a371dd6},
\code{hu\_smith\_analytic\_check.py} reports median, $t_{\rm end}$
error, and within-tolerance count against all three references every
run (the verdict still follows \code{--reference}, default Raga-II),
so a single-median FAIL can no longer hide the bracketing.

\subsubsection{Phase 4b update (2026-07-28): fixed cadence at the shipped default}

\code{HII\_rebuild\_time\_Myr=0.01} (matching the converged end of the

\hline
Raga-I (Eqn~8)          &  8.19\% & 10.84\% & 56/82 \\

honest statement is: resolution alone does not degrade this target
materially, and the adaptive-to-fixed question for
\code{StromgrenSphereHuSmith2017} specifically was never empirically
isolated.

\subsection{Smith et al.\ (2021) clump}
\label{sec:clump-current}

\textbf{PASS as of 2026-07-22.} \textbf{Not yet re-checked} against the
2026-07-27 count-budget redesign (Section~\ref{sec:husmith-current}) --
every run below predates it. \code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021}
(four co-located $19.2\,M_\odot$ sources, $10\,$pc-radius $10^4\,{\rm
cm^{-3}}$ clump $20\,$pc from the sources in a $100\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$
background), built with
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}.
The paper reports no single quantitative erosion number to match, so
validation here rests on two criteria instead: visual agreement with
the qualitative morphology in the paper's own Figure~2 (ionized bubble
stopping at the clump, near/far asymmetry), and -- the criterion that
resolves this target -- cross-scheme consistency. The same
\code{gas\_mass=20} configuration was independently reproduced with
three different hydro solvers (SPH, Gizmo MFM, Gizmo MFV); MFM and MFV
agree with each other on every metric to within $1$--$2$ percentage
points (tables below), which is the result being validated. SPH's own
number disagrees with the other two (compression instead of
dilution), but that disagreement is itself understood: a known
SPH-specific under-mixing artifact detailed below, not a failure of
the photoionization/budget-splitting algorithm being tested.

\textbf{Confirmed}: the ionized core's measured gas temperature sits at
$1.4$--$2.2\times10^4\,$K out to $r\approx15\,$pc, dropping sharply to
$<50\,$K beyond $r\approx16\,$pc -- matching the algorithm's own
$r_{\rm HII}=16.44\,$pc for this configuration almost exactly, and
confirming the run is genuinely in the $10^4\,$K-order regime the
build flag targets.

as leaving it "barely disturbed"/"relatively undisturbed". Replotting
our own \code{gas\_mass=20} snapshot in their exact convention

partially-heated halo, distinct from both the ionized bubble and the
cold clump) draping around the clump, most pronounced in the spherical
panel -- this shell is essentially absent from our own result in both
schemes, which shows a sharp, direct transition from ionized bubble to
cold clump instead. This is consistent with a known SPH-vs-moving-mesh
difference: \citet{Smith2021} use AREPO (moving-mesh Godunov), which
handles mixing at contact discontinuities more physically than SPH,
which is prone to under-mixing (an artificial surface-tension-like
effect) at sharp density contrasts like this clump's $100\times$ jump.
This is a plausible explanation for the qualitatively smaller
disruption signal measured here relative to the paper's description,
and would not be a bug in the photoionization/budget-splitting
algorithm itself -- tested by rerunning \code{gas\_mass=20} with
\swift's Gizmo MFM solver instead of SPH (same IC, same radiation
parameters; \code{hydro\_set\_v\_sig\_based\_on\_velocity\_kick()}
backported from \code{darwin/gear\_mechanical\_feedback} to make the
GEAR mechanical-feedback code path build against Gizmo).

resolution.

appear in any of the three hydro schemes tested, are not pursued
further -- the paper gives no quantitative number for either, so there
is no concrete target left to chase. The cross-scheme consistency
above is the validation criterion for this target, and it is met.

\textbf{PASS, as a self-consistency check.} Neither Hu et al.\ (2017)
nor Smith et al.\ (2021) tests rate-coupling -- both use the same
instant flag-and-floor scheme this model defaults to -- so
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate} (T5) is checked
instead against basic photoionization-equilibrium physics, using
\code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021} as the testbed. A caching bug that
left \code{RT\_heating\_rate} always exactly zero was found and fixed
first (Section~\ref{sec:t5-heating-rate-zero}); the results below are
all post-fix.

budget-splitting $\times$ \code{gas\_mass}$=\{20,95\}$, T5 on/off in
each case; the uniform \code{gas\_mass=95} pair was skipped, since the

the resolution contrast under angular):

\textbf{Interpretation}: T5 consistently reaches a somewhat
\emph{larger} $r_{\rm HII}$ than flag-and-floor ($+1$--$1.6\,$pc,
5--8\%, every row above), while holding less total ionized mass
($\sim$50--75\%) and running hotter ($\sim$2000--4300\,K above). The
mass/temperature difference has a single cause: flag-and-floor forces
every tagged particle to \code{HII\_frac=1} at a fixed
$T\approx10^4\,$K by construction, while T5 lets Grackle's chemistry
network solve for whatever partial-ionization ($\sim$0.7--0.8),
higher-temperature balance the locally-computed $\Gamma_{\rm HI}$ and
photoheating rate (which includes the star's excess photon energy
above $13.6\,$eV) actually support -- the expected signature of real
photoionization microphysics, not a discrepancy. Why $r_{\rm HII}$
itself comes out larger under T5 is not yet explained (a plausible
link is the slower recombination of the residually-hot gas noted in
Figure~\ref{fig:t5-temperature-slices}, not yet checked).

\textbf{T5 and $T_i$}: \code{RT\_heating\_rate} is built purely from
the star's own blackbody excess-photon energy
(\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_mean\_excess\_photon\_energy\_HI}),
never from \code{radiation\_get\_T\_collisional\_K} ($T_i$) -- Grackle
never sees $T_i$ under T5, and gas heats to whatever equilibrium its
chemistry network finds from the real photoheating rate (measured
here: $11\,000$--$13\,500\,$K), regardless of whether $T_i$ is the
natural, metallicity-dependent value ($\sim\!47\,500\,$K at $Z=0$) or
the $10^4\,$K debug override used for every run above. $T_i$ still
enters the tagging/budget bookkeeping shared with flag-and-floor (the
case-B recombination coefficient in
\code{radiation\_get\_part\_rate\_to\_fully\_ionize} is evaluated at
$T_i$), so it still affects how far a pass can tag -- not yet checked
at the natural $T_i$.

T5 remains off by default (\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate=0}),
so none of this affects the three PASS verdicts above.
