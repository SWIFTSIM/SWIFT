\section{Validation}
\label{sec:validation}

This project validates against three published targets, listed in
Table~\ref{tab:validation-targets}. STARBENCH \citep{Bisbas2015} is a
single-source D-type expansion test where twelve independent,
differently-implemented radiation-hydrodynamics codes agree with each
other to $\sim$1--2\% -- a direct test of whether this code reproduces
the actual physics of D-type expansion, not just one paper's own
subgrid scheme. Hu et al.\ (2017)/Smith et al.\ (2021) 4-star and the
Smith et al.\ (2021) clump target instead reproduce those papers' own
specific multi-source tests and reported numbers.

\subsection{Metallicity and the ionized-gas temperature floor}
\label{sec:metallicity}

Both reference papers are effectively zero-metallicity (\citet{Hu2017}
state their D-type test medium is ``composed of pure hydrogen''; neither
paper's recombination rate or temperature floor has a metallicity term
at all) and clamp ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K. This code's own
temperature floor (Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}) is metallicity-\emph{dependent}:
at $Z=0$ the applied temperature is $\approx$47,500\,K, not $10^4\,$K,
which raises $r_{\rm HII}$ through two independent channels -- a lower
case-B recombination coefficient (Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature})
and a higher D-type driving sound speed ($c_s\propto\sqrt T$).

Both channels are neutralized by construction at the metallicity where
$T_{\rm collisional}=10^4\,$K exactly (Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}):
\begin{equation}
  \frac{0.86}{1+0.22\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)} = 1
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Z/Z_\odot \approx 0.231.
  \label{eq:z0231}
\end{equation}
All three validation targets instead use
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}
(forces $T_i$ directly, no metallicity side effects on cooling), which
matches the papers' own flat convention exactly. Each example's own
README documents the flag.

\subsection{STARBENCH (Bisbas et al.\ 2015)}
\label{sec:starbench-current}

\textbf{PASS, 6.12\% error.} Validated configuration: committed
\code{StromgrenSphereStarbench/run.sh}/\code{params.yml}
($26.75\,M_\odot$ star, $Q_H=10^{49}\,{\rm s^{-1}}$,
$n_H=3346\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$, $10\,$pc box, purely hydrodynamical) built
with \code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}.
Checked against Bisbas et al.\ (2015) Eqns 8/11/28/29
(\code{starbench\_analytic\_check.py}, 10\% tolerance):
$r_{\rm sim}=2.384\,$pc vs.\ $R_{\rm SB}=2.247\,$pc at $t=3\,$Myr,
\textbf{6.12\% error}, for the full run (never exceeds the $5\,$pc box
half-width or the $4.9\,$pc search-radius ceiling).

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{Figures/starbench_restart_ti1e4_2026-07-21_check}
  \caption{STARBENCH check: committed defaults plus $T_i=10^4\,$K
    forced at compile time. PASS, 6.12\% error at $t=3\,$Myr.}
  \label{fig:starbench-restart-ti1e4}
\end{figure}

\textbf{Caveat}: the plain default build (no compile flag) gives this
code's natural, metallicity-dependent $T_i\approx47{,}500\,$K instead
of STARBENCH's flat $10^4\,$K convention (Section~\ref{sec:metallicity})
and FAILs at 22.76\% -- the compile flag is required to reproduce this
result, not optional.

\textbf{Search-radius mechanism verified not binding}:
\code{HIIRegionRadii}$(t)$ grows smoothly to its natural equilibrium at
$2.46\,$pc by $t\approx0.93\,$Myr, with per-step growth factors
decelerating monotonically (never hitting
\code{Stars:HII\_max\_radius\_expansion\_tries} or
\code{Stars:HII\_max\_retry\_full\_buffer}), then holds flat for the
remaining $\sim2\,$Myr -- well short of the $4.9\,$pc ceiling.

\subsection{Hu et al.\ (2017)/Smith et al.\ (2021), 4-star}
\label{sec:husmith-current}

\textbf{PASS, median 6.89\% error.} Validated configuration: committed
\code{StromgrenSphereHuSmith2017/run.sh}/\code{params.yml} (4 co-located
$19.2\,M_\odot$ sources, $\dot N_{\rm ion}\approx2.5\times10^{48}\,{\rm
s^{-1}}$ each, uniform $n_H\approx107\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$ background,
$617{,}851$ particles at the default \code{gas\_mass=4}) built with
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}.

Hu et al.\ (2017, Fig.~A1) and Smith et al.\ (2021, Fig.~1) both plot a
curve labeled ``STARBENCH'' for this exact configuration -- this is
Hosokawa \& Inutsuka (2006)'s shell-inertia equation (``Raga-II'', Eqn
11), not the full Bisbas et al.\ (2015) blend (Eqns 28--29): the
papers' own curve reaches $\sim\!25\,$pc by $t=8\,$Myr, matching
Raga-II directly, not the blend's $\sim\!20\,$pc for the same inputs.
Checked against Raga-II: \textbf{median 6.89\% error} (min 3.69\%,
70/82 snapshots within the 10\% tolerance).

\begin{figure}[h]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.8\linewidth]{Figures/husmith_restart_ti1e4_2026-07-21_check}
  \caption{Hu/Smith 4-star check: committed defaults plus $T_i=10^4\,$K
    forced at compile time, checked against Raga-II. PASS, median
    6.89\%.}
  \label{fig:husmith-restart-ti1e4}
\end{figure}

\code{hu\_smith\_analytic\_check.py} defaults to \code{--reference
raga2}; Raga-I and the Bisbas et al.\ blend remain available via
\code{--reference \{raga1,starbench\}} for comparison, but are not
labeled ``STARBENCH'' in the figure.

\textbf{Search-radius mechanism verified not binding}: each star's
\code{HIIRegionRadii}$(t)$ grows steadily to $\sim\!27.7\,$pc by
$t\approx7.8\,$Myr, still rising, comfortably below the $40\,$pc
ceiling.

\textbf{Resolution}: \code{gas\_mass=4} (default, $617{,}851$ particles)
is the validated resolution. A coarser \code{gas\_mass=20} run
($123{,}571$ particles) still passes by aggregate median (5.58\%), but
its late-time error climbs past the 10\% tolerance from
$t\approx7.4\,$Myr onward and does not recover, unlike the finer
default run -- the expected convergence direction (finer resolution
tracks Raga-II more faithfully at late times).

\subsection{Smith et al.\ (2021) clump}
\label{sec:clump-current}

\textbf{NOT YET VALIDATED.} Investigation ongoing on
\code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021}
(four co-located $19.2\,M_\odot$ sources, $10\,$pc-radius $10^4\,{\rm
cm^{-3}}$ clump $20\,$pc from the sources in a $100\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$
background), built with
\code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_DEBUG\_FIXED\_IONIZED\_TEMPERATURE\_K=1e4}.

\textbf{Confirmed}: the ionized core's measured gas temperature sits at
$1.4$--$2.2\times10^4\,$K out to $r\approx15\,$pc, dropping sharply to
$<50\,$K beyond $r\approx16\,$pc -- matching the algorithm's own
$r_{\rm HII}=16.44\,$pc for this configuration almost exactly, and
confirming the run is genuinely in the $10^4\,$K-order regime the
build flag targets.

\textbf{Clump erosion} (ID-tracked, near/far hemisphere split against
the star$\to$clump axis, \code{clump\_hemisphere\_erosion.py}/
\code{clump\_hemisphere\_density\_change.py}): the near (star-facing)
side shows negligible mass loss below the clump's own IC density
threshold ($<1\%$ across \code{gas\_mass}$=\{20,95\}$ and both
uniform/angular schemes), but its median density actually
\emph{increases} to 125--141\% of its $t=0$ value -- compression, not
dilution, which a density-threshold metric alone cannot detect. The
far (star-shielded) side is essentially unaffected in every
configuration tested.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
\hline
& near, still-dense & near, $n_H$ ratio & far, $n_H$ ratio \\
\hline
\code{gas\_mass=20}, uniform & 99.3\% & 134.3\% [133.4--135.0] & 95.5\% \\
\code{gas\_mass=20}, angular & 99.9\% & 125.0\% [124.6--125.6] & 95.7\% \\
\code{gas\_mass=95}, uniform & 99.8\% & 140.2\% [138.9--141.4] & 104.9\% \\
\code{gas\_mass=95}, angular & 99.9\% & 141.3\% [139.8--142.6] & 105.1\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
``Still-dense'' is the \code{clump\_hemisphere\_erosion.py} threshold
metric (\% of $t=0$ mass remaining above $n_H=1000\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$);
``$n_H$ ratio'' is the continuous median-density metric (final/$t=0$,
\% with bootstrap 68\% confidence interval, $10^4$ resamples).

\textbf{Resolution-dependent scheme differentiation}: uniform and
angular differ significantly in near-side compression at
\code{gas\_mass=20} ($134.3\%$ vs.\ $125.0\%$, non-overlapping
confidence intervals above), but converge to statistically
indistinguishable values at the coarser \code{gas\_mass=95} ($140.2\%$
vs.\ $141.3\%$, overlapping intervals) -- consistent with the angular
scheme's per-pixel budget-splitting needing enough particles per
pixel's solid angle to manifest a distinct effect.

\textbf{Direct comparison against Smith et al.\ (2021)'s own Figure 2}:
the paper reports no quantitative erosion number at all -- their own
validation is qualitative, describing the spherical scheme as
disrupting "a significant portion of the clump" and the HealPix scheme
as leaving it "barely disturbed"/"relatively undisturbed". Replotting
our own \code{gas\_mass=20} snapshot in their exact convention
($\log_{10}(T/\mu\,[{\rm K}])$, $\mu=1$, colorbar 1.0--4.5, green
circle at the no-clump Raga-II prediction, $26.7\,$pc at $t=8\,$Myr)
for a direct visual comparison:

\begin{figure}
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.55\linewidth]{Figures/clump_smith2021_style_comparison}
  \caption{Our \texttt{gas\_mass=20} result in Smith et al.\ (2021)'s own
    Figure 2 convention: spherical (top) vs.\ HealPix/angular (bottom),
    $t=8\,$Myr, green circle at the no-clump Raga-II prediction
    ($26.7\,$pc).}
  \label{fig:clump-smith2021-style}
\end{figure}

Their Figure 2 shows a visible intermediate-temperature shell (a
partially-heated halo, distinct from both the ionized bubble and the
cold clump) draping around the clump, most pronounced in the spherical
panel -- this shell is essentially absent from our own result in both
schemes, which shows a sharp, direct transition from ionized bubble to
cold clump instead. This is consistent with a known SPH-vs-moving-mesh
difference: \citet{Smith2021} use AREPO (moving-mesh Godunov), which
handles mixing at contact discontinuities more physically than SPH,
which is prone to under-mixing (an artificial surface-tension-like
effect) at sharp density contrasts like this clump's $100\times$ jump.
This is a plausible explanation for the qualitatively smaller
disruption signal measured here relative to the paper's description,
and would not be a bug in the photoionization/budget-splitting
algorithm itself -- testable by rerunning with \swift's Gizmo MFV/MFM
solver instead of SPH and comparing directly, not yet attempted.

\textbf{Open}: whether the compression signal measured here (near-side
density increase) is the same effect the paper calls erosion/disruption,
or a distinct effect the missing mixing shell would otherwise mask;
the AREPO-vs-SPH mixing comparison above, if pursued.
