  \item[$\square$] \textbf{T8} -- e10 (above, Section~\ref{sec:e10})
    found the simulated $r_{\rm HII}(t)$ growth curve's \emph{shape}
    does not match the analytic D-type solution: it takes
    $\approx$3.8\,Myr to first reach $R_{\rm st}$, where the analytic
    solution reaches it almost immediately. The parameter-grounded
    hypothesis is that per-rebuild search-radius growth is capped at
    $\text{HII\_radius\_expansion\_factor}^{\text{max\_HII\_radius\_expansion\_tries}}$
    ($1.1^5\approx1.61\times$ at this example's defaults) per cycle,
    decoupled from the rebuild cadence itself -- so even a much faster
    \code{HII\_region\_rebuild\_time\_Myr} would shrink but not
    eliminate the delay. Not confirmed by direct instrumentation (which
    retry branch -- buffer-full vs.\ radius-expansion -- fires on each
    rebuild cycle, and why growth stalls for several cycles at a time,
    e.g.\ the 24\,pc plateau in Figure~\ref{fig:e10-dtype}). If this
    matters for production runs (an HII region reaching its true size
    several Myr later than the physics would predict), the fix
    direction is likely either raising
    \code{max\_HII\_radius\_expansion\_tries}/\code{HII\_radius\_expansion\_factor}
    so a single rebuild can catch up to the star's actual budget-implied
    reach, or seeding the post-rebuild search radius from something
    better-informed than the previous cycle's achieved $h_{\rm hii}$
    (e.g.\ an analytic $R_{\rm st}$ estimate). Not attempted this
    session. \textbf{Update (2026-07-18):} T11's adaptive rebuild
    cadence narrows this delay (time to first reach $R_{\rm
    st}$: $3.32\,$Myr fixed $\to$ $1.76\,$Myr adaptive, same setup,
    Section~\ref{sec:e10}) but does not close it -- confirming the
    hypothesis above that the per-cycle search-radius cap, not the
    rebuild cadence, is the structural bottleneck. The fix directions
    listed above remain the way to actually resolve this. \textbf{Update
    (2026-07-18):} two further, independent findings this pass. (1) A
    direct radial-velocity check on tagged gas confirms momentum
    coupling itself is not the bottleneck -- tagged particles show mean
    $v_r$ several times that of untagged gas and accelerating, so the
    pressure-to-momentum conversion already works; moving the
    ionization-heating application from the cooling task to
    \code{feedback\_update\_part()} (drift-time, same-step force-loop

    \code{HIIRegionRadii} freezes at tag time and never updates as

    bottleneck -- but both narrow how much of the mismatch was
    measurement artifact vs.\ real. \textbf{Update (2026-07-19),
    verdict refined:} directly tested the cap hypothesis by loosening
    it far beyond anything previously tried
    (\code{HII\_radius\_expansion\_factor}=1.5,
    \code{HII\_max\_radius\_expansion\_tries}=20, i.e.\
