  \item[$\square$] \textbf{T10} -- This model assumes $n_e \approx n_H$
    throughout (Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}), i.e.\ it does not track
    helium's ionization state at all. For a standard cosmic He mass
    fraction ($\sim$24--28\%, $\sim$8--10\% by number relative to H),
    singly-ionized He alone (needs $>24.6\,$eV, present in any O-star
    field that ionizes H at all) underestimates $n_e$ by $\sim$8--10\%
    if ignored; doubly-ionized He ($>54.4\,$eV, only the hottest O
    stars) up to $\sim$16--20\%. This affects the recombination-cost
    calculation, cooling, and (T11 below) the adaptive rebuild cadence's
    recombination timescale. Not attempted: would need a new
    per-particle He ionization state (not just the binary
    ionized/not-ionized tag this model has today), separate
    photon-count integrals above 24.6\,eV/54.4\,eV in the $\dot N_{\rm
    ion}$ fit, and a self-consistent H/He budget-sharing scheme -- a
    standalone physics feature at least as large as the angular-pixel-
    splitting work, not a small addition. GEAR\_10 chemistry already
    tracks He \emph{mass fraction} per particle (one of its 10
    elements) -- the gap is specifically ionization \emph{state}, not
    composition data. Worth checking whether \citet{Hu2017}/
    \citet{Smith2021} themselves track He ionization before investing
    here, since matching their convention matters more for this
    model's validation purpose than matching real ISM physics in
    isolation.
