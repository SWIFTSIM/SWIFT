\section{ISRF channel: equations and discretisation}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc}

This section is a reference for the equations the GEAR interstellar-radiation-field
(ISRF) channel actually solves, and for the discretisation that solves them. Each
subsection first states the equations, the discretisation and the numerics. A
paragraph or subsection titled ``Implementation'' at its end then names the file,
function and line that compute each piece. The section contains no validation
status and no run results.

\subsection{Scope and where each piece runs}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-scope}

The transported field is an M1 (first-moment, variable-Eddington-tensor) system,
two bands, three moments, one band-edge coupling between the two bands, and two
couplings out to the gas through Grackle. The scheme is \textbf{M1}, not a P1
relaxation.

\paragraph{Order within one SWIFT step, per gas particle.} The discretisation of
Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-disc} is staggered across four task-graph stages, so
this ordering is needed to read it:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{drift.} Snapshot $u \to u_{\rm prev}$ and
    $\rho \to \rho_{\rm prev}$, set $\kappa$, set $c_{\rm hyp}$ for scheme
    2, and draw the dose reservoir down; then the illumination-tag
    expiry; then rebuild the closure cache.
  \item \textbf{every density $h$-iteration.} Zero the kernel-mean dissipation
    reference and reset the neighbour-bin maximum.
  \item \textbf{density loop.} Accumulate the dissipation reference and
    \code{max\_ngb\_time\_bin}, in the symmetric and the non-symmetric pair
    interaction.
  \item \textbf{density ghost.} Set $c_{\rm hyp}$ for the \code{kernel\_local}
    scheme and rebuild the closure cache. A particle whose $h$-iteration gives
    up with no neighbours takes a separate path
    (Section~\ref{sec:isrfd-reservoir}).
  \item \textbf{gradient loop.} Accumulate the M1 pressure-tensor divergence.
  \item \textbf{extra ghost.} Advance $\boldsymbol{F}$, apply the M1
    limiter, and set both dissipation coefficients.
  \item \textbf{force loop.} Accumulate
    $\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho\boldsymbol{F}^{n+1})$ and the dissipation source.
  \item \textbf{end-force.} Advance $u$.
\end{enumerate}

Star injection runs in the star feedback-apply loop.

\paragraph{The transport is off by default.}
\code{GEARFeedback:ISRF\_propagation} defaults to 0, i.e.\ the default ISRF run is
injection plus receiver-side extinction only, with no transport. In that mode the
three ghosts do nothing and both $\kappa$ are forced to zero, so nothing in
Sections~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-continuum}, \ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-disc} and
\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-transfer} is consumed; the pairwise density and gradient
loops nevertheless still run and still fill their accumulators.

\subsection{State and bands}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-state}

\subsubsection{Moments and operators}

There are three \textbf{moments}:
\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_PE} $=0$, \code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW} $=1$,
\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} $=2$, with
\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_COUNT} $=3$; and two \textbf{operators},
\code{ISRF\_OPERATOR\_PE} $=0$ and
\code{ISRF\_OPERATOR\_LW} $=1$. The two maps are

\begin{align}
  o(m) &= \code{radiation\_isrf\_moment\_to\_operator}[m] = (\mathrm{PE},\,\mathrm{LW},\,\mathrm{LW}),
  \label{eq:isrfd-moment-to-op} \\
  m(o) &= \code{radiation\_isrf\_operator\_owner}[o] = (\mathrm{PE},\,\mathrm{LW}),
  \label{eq:isrfd-op-owner}
\end{align}

each with exactly one entry per moment or operator.
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-moment-to-op}) means
\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} shares LW's opacity, Eddington tensor and
dissipation coefficients; Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-op-owner}) means every write to
per-operator state reads its owning moment's $(u,\boldsymbol{F})$.

\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} is a photon-\emph{number} moment carried in
energy-equivalent units: its source luminosity is a bare copy of the LW entry,
for the single-star path and for the IMF-population path, and at first init the
two are forced equal whenever exactly one is zero, in either direction. It is
\textbf{not} read by any coupling out to the gas
(Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-couplings}); its only live role is the diagnostic
$d\ln(U/N)/dt$ identity, which needs its own band-edge weight
$\lambda_N(\mathrm{LW})$.

\paragraph{Implementation.} The moments are the enum at
\code{feedback\_struct.h:45-50} and the operators the enum at
\code{feedback\_struct.h:56-60}. The two maps of
Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-moment-to-op}) and (\ref{eq:isrfd-op-owner}) are at
\code{feedback\_struct.h:67-68} and \code{:85-86}, both declared unsized with a
\code{\_Static\_assert} on their length (\code{feedback\_struct.h:70-74},
\code{:88-92}). The LW luminosity is copied into the photon moment at
\code{stellar\_evolution.c:1403-1405} for the single-star path and
\code{:1677-1679} for the IMF-population path. The first-init equalisation is
in \code{radiation\_first\_init\_part} (\code{radiation\_isrf.c:60}, lines
81-93). The per-moment weight array that holds $\lambda_N(\mathrm{LW})$ is at
\code{radiation\_isrf.c:566-569}.

\subsubsection{Implementation: per-moment, per-operator and per-particle state}

Per moment, \code{struct feedback\_isrf\_moment\_data}
(\code{feedback\_struct.h:97-147}): $u$ (\textbf{double},
\code{feedback\_struct.h:102}), $u_{\rm prev}$ (\textbf{double}, \code{:105}),
\code{specific\_flux[3]} (float, \code{:108}), \code{div\_specific\_flux} (float,
\code{:111}), \code{dissipation\_u} (float, \code{:114}), \code{grad\_u[3]}
(float, \code{:118}), \code{u\_dose\_reservoir} (float, \code{:121}),
\code{u\_source\_rate} (float, \code{:124}), plus the debug-only
\code{u\_min\_since\_snapshot} (\code{:137}), \code{cumulative\_injected}
(\code{:141}) and \code{cumulative\_absorbed} (\code{:145}).

The double width of $u$ and $u_{\rm prev}$ is load-bearing, as its comment explains
(\code{feedback\_struct.h:99-101}): the per-step relaxation depth
$\mathcal{A}$ can be a few $10^{-8}$, and $1-\mathcal{A}$ at that scale is not
distinguishable in a 24-bit mantissa. Every other accumulator stays float because
none carries a multi-step decay at that depth.

\textbf{The energy ledger (debug builds only).} \code{cumulative\_injected}
sums the raw dose handed to $u$, $\Delta t\,\mathcal{R}\,S_m$ with
$\mathcal{R} = c_{\rm hyp}/c$: rescaled but not relaxed.
\code{cumulative\_absorbed} sums, every step and before $u$ is overwritten,
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Abs} \mathrel{+}=
    \big(u^{n} + \Delta t\,\varphi\,D\big)\big(1 - e^{-\mathcal{A}}\big)
    + \big(\mathcal{R}\,S_m - \code{div\_specific\_flux}\big)\,\Delta t\,(1-\varphi),
\end{equation}
with $D$ the artificial-dissipation source (\code{dissipation\_u}) and
$\varphi$, $\mathcal{A}$ as in Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-depth-u}). It is
\textbf{not} the dust-extinction loss alone: the transport divergence and the
dissipation source are folded in too, because the exact-relaxation update mixes
all three under one $\varphi$. At a snapshot, $E + \mathrm{Abs} - \mathrm{Inj}$,
with $E = \sum m\,u$ (plus the pending amounts below), isolates the part of the update that this closed-form split
attributes neither to injection nor to the surviving field: the transport and
dissipation residual, which the SPH divergence's kernel-sum identity and the
dissipation's pairwise antisymmetry drive to about zero when summed over all
particles. Both totals are never reset, so the identity holds at every snapshot.
The PE moment's share of the LW-to-PE transfer is booked into PE's injected total,
since LW's absorbed total already contains it. Energy that a pair across time
bins still owes a coarser particle sits in its pending amounts
(Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-pairwise}), so
$E = \sum m\,\big(u + c_{\rm hyp}(P^{\rm tr} + P^{\rm d})\big)$, and the
pending add books its absorbed share. Across time bins the identity is exact
in its $c_{\rm hyp}$-weighted form. The ledger is updated at
\code{radiation\_isrf.c:619-629} and, for the pending add, at
\code{radiation\_isrf.c:504-505}.

The ledger residual is
\begin{equation}
  R = \frac{E - E_0 + (\mathrm{Abs} - \mathrm{Abs}_0) - (\mathrm{Inj} - \mathrm{Inj}_0)}
           {\mathrm{Inj} - \mathrm{Inj}_0},
  \label{eq:isrfd-ledger-residual}
\end{equation}
formed at each snapshot after the first, with every sum taken over particles
and the subscript 0 marking the first snapshot. It needs one propagation speed
in the whole box and a build with debugging checks, which write the two
cumulative totals. With the pending amounts in $E$, the pair operator is exact,
so $R$ is float32 rounding only. Each particle updates $u$ and its two
accumulators at most once per step, hence
$|R| \le n_{\rm steps}\,2^{-23}$, with $n_{\rm steps}$ the number of global
steps taken. This is the bar of the check, not a proof: float32 rounding scales
with $E$, so $R$ can exceed it when $E_0$ is much larger than the injected
energy. The bar does not depend on the number of ranks or threads. An $R$ above
it points to lost or doubled energy, not rounding.

\textbf{\code{grad\_u} is not the gradient of $u$.} The code accumulates the
anisotropic M1 pressure-tensor divergence
$\rho^{-1}\nabla\!\cdot\!(\mathsf{D}(\hat f)\,\rho\, u)$ into it
(\code{radiation\_propagation\_iact.h:307-328}, header comment \code{:274-275}).
The field name, and the ``$\nabla u$'' wording at
\code{radiation\_isrf.c:812 and :817}, are a misnomer; the code is the truth. It is
written $\boldsymbol{G}_m$ here.

For every scheme, \code{specific\_flux} stores the \textbf{reduced} flux
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} = \boldsymbol{F}/c_{\rm hyp}$, not the true flux
(\code{radiation\_isrf.c:875-876}, \code{radiation\_propagation\_iact.h:27-33},
\code{feedback\_struct.h:107-108}). The snapshot getters multiply it by the
particle's current $c_{\rm hyp}$ before writing it, so the output fields hold the
true flux.

Per operator, \code{struct feedback\_isrf\_operator\_data}
(\code{feedback\_struct.h:152-173}): $\kappa$ (float, \code{:157}),
\code{m1\_closure\_D[3][3]} (float, \code{:160}), \code{ngb\_mean\_abs\_u\_V}
(float, \code{:164}), \code{dissipation\_alpha\_trigger} (float, \code{:168}) and
\code{dissipation\_alpha\_floor} (float, \code{:172}).

Per particle, in \code{struct feedback\_part\_data}: \code{max\_ngb\_time\_bin}
(\code{feedback\_struct.h:192}), the largest \code{time\_bin} seen in this
$h$-iteration's kernel; \code{rho\_prev} (float, \code{:250}), a
\textbf{comoving} density snapshot seeded to $1.0$ at first init and never left
at zero (\code{radiation\_isrf.c:113}, \code{:168}); \code{c\_hyp} (float,
\code{feedback\_struct.h:257}), physical; \code{dt\_prev} (float,
\code{feedback\_struct.h:261}), this particle's own
\textbf{physical} timestep, cached raw and unfloored
(\code{radiation\_isrf.c:223}); and the four tag fields
\code{ISRF\_last\_touch\_ti}, \code{is\_illuminated\_ISRF},
\code{ISRF\_illumination\_end\_ti}, \code{ISRF\_reservoir\_end\_ti}
(\code{feedback\_struct.h:273}, \code{:278}, \code{:288}, \code{:293}).

On the star side, \code{struct feedback\_spart\_data.radiation} carries
\code{L\_band[ISRF\_MOMENT\_COUNT]} as \textbf{double} physical power in internal
units (\code{feedback\_struct.h:573}) and \code{Delta\_t} as float proper time
(\code{:584}).

\subsubsection{Band definitions and cross sections}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-bands}

The photoelectric (PE) band is $6.0$--$11.2$\,eV and the Lyman-Werner (LW) band
is $11.2$--$13.6$\,eV. Only the lower edges enter the code as numbers:
$E_{\rm lo}(\mathrm{PE}) = 6.0$\,eV and
$E_{\rm lo}(\mathrm{LW}) = 11.2$\,eV; the $13.6$\,eV
upper edge appears only in comments and is never used. The cgs forms are hard-coded
literals and never unit-converted:

\begin{equation}
  E_{\rm lo}(\mathrm{PE}) = 9.6130598\times10^{-12}\,{\rm erg}, \qquad
  E_{\rm lo}(\mathrm{LW}) = 1.7944378\times10^{-11}\,{\rm erg}.
  \label{eq:isrfd-band-edges}
\end{equation}

Dust cross sections per hydrogen nucleon, attributed
to \citet{Kim2023} and Weingartner \& Draine (2001):

\begin{equation}
  \sigma_d(\mathrm{PE}) = 9\times10^{-22}\,{\rm cm^2}, \qquad
  \sigma_d(\mathrm{LW}) = 1.5\times10^{-21}\,{\rm cm^2}.
  \label{eq:isrfd-sigma-d}
\end{equation}

The remaining constants are: $\mu_H = 1.4$, the mean mass per hydrogen nucleon in
proton masses, deliberately \emph{not} the run's own
hydrogen fraction; Grackle's solar metal fraction
$Z_\odot = 0.01295$; the default dust-to-gas ratio
$f_{\rm gr} = 0.009387$; and the Habing normalisation
$F_{\rm Habing} = 1.6\times10^{-3}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}}$.

For the H$_2$ photodissociation rate, the \textbf{primary} calibration constant is
the quotient

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\sigma_{\rm H_2}}{E_{\rm LW}} = 1.2847106348798106\times10^{-7}\,{\rm cm^2\,erg^{-1}},
  \label{eq:isrfd-sigma-over-e}
\end{equation}

anchored on \citet{Sternberg2014}. The pair
$\sigma_{\rm H_2} = 2.5111667\times10^{-18}\,{\rm cm^2}$
and $E_{\rm LW} = 12.2$\,eV is
derived, quoted only for provenance, and is \emph{not} read by the rate.

\code{radiation.h}. The lower edges are at \code{radiation.h:118} (PE) and

The dust cross sections of Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-sigma-d}) are at

$f_{\rm gr}$ at \code{radiation.h:83} and $F_{\rm Habing}$ at
\code{radiation.h:86}. The quotient of

\subsubsection{Band-edge weights $\lambda_E(\mathrm{PE})$, $\lambda_E(\mathrm{LW})$, $\lambda_N(\mathrm{LW})$}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-lambda}

Each $\lambda_m$ multiplies the dilated Hubble rate inside its own moment's
relaxation depth (Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-depth-u}) and
(\ref{eq:isrfd-depth-F})). Its meaning: the number of e-folds-worth of band
content lost per e-fold of expansion, because the band edges are fixed in
\emph{physical} energy while photons redshift downward through them. That
meaning holds for $u_m$. On $\boldsymbol{F}_m$ the same weight is an
assumption: it holds if the flux has the same spectral shape at the band edge
as its own moment, and the flux receives no LW-to-PE transfer. Its effect is
bounded in Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lagrangian}.

The grey (comoving-edge) value \textbf{differs per moment}:

\begin{align}
  \lambda_E(b) &= 1 + \frac{\Lambda_b\, E_{\rm lo}(b)}{\langle E\rangle_b},
  \qquad b \in \{\mathrm{PE}, \mathrm{LW}\},
  \label{eq:isrfd-lambda-E} \\
  \lambda_N(\mathrm{LW}) &= \Lambda_{\rm LW}
  \qquad \text{(no $+1$)},
  \label{eq:isrfd-lambda-N}
\end{align}

The $+1$ in
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-lambda-E}) is the redshift loss of photon \emph{energy},
which survives when the edges are comoving, so the grey value of $\lambda_E$ is
$1$. Photon \emph{number} does not redshift, so the grey value of
$\lambda_N(\mathrm{LW})$ is $0$, not $1$. The three shipped fallbacks are mutually
consistent on this: $(6.508 - 1)\times 12.2/11.2 = 6.0$.

The three scalars are computed once at start-up, over the whole IMF
$[\code{imf.mass\_min}, \code{imf.mass\_max}]$, at one reference metallicity
$Z_{\rm ref} = 0.014$ for a 2D table:

\begin{align}
  \lambda_E(\mathrm{PE}) &= 1 + \frac{l_{\rm edge}(\mathrm{PE})}{l_{\rm PE}},
  \label{eq:isrfd-lambda-pe-table} \\
  \lambda_E(\mathrm{LW}) &= 1 + \frac{l_{\rm edge}(\mathrm{LW})}{l_{\rm LW}},
  \label{eq:isrfd-lambda-lw-table} \\
  \lambda_N(\mathrm{LW}) &= \big(\lambda_E(\mathrm{LW}) - 1\big)
    \frac{\langle E\rangle_{\rm LW}}{E_{\rm lo}(\mathrm{LW})},
  \label{eq:isrfd-lambda-n-table}
\end{align}

Here $l_{\rm edge}(b)$ is the
IMF-integrated \code{Integrated\_SpectralPhotonRateAt<b>Edge} differenced over
the mass interval, \emph{already} multiplied by $E_{\rm lo}(b)^2$ and converted to
internal power at read time (the read divides the cgs value by

\subsection{The continuum M1 system, per band}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-continuum}

The code never writes the PDE; the system below is the continuum problem whose
one-step solution is implemented in Sections~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-u-update} and
\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-F-update}. Per moment $m$, with operator $o = o(m)$:

\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial u_m}{\partial t} &=
    -\big(c_{\rm hyp}\kappa_o + \lambda_m H_{\rm dil}\big)\, u_m
    + \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c}\, S_m
    - \frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\!\cdot\!\big(\rho \boldsymbol{F}_m\big)
    + \Psi_m,
  \label{eq:isrfd-continuum-u} \\
  \frac{\partial \boldsymbol{F}_m}{\partial t} &=
    -\big(c_{\rm hyp}\kappa_o + \lambda_m H_{\rm dil}\big)\, \boldsymbol{F}_m
    - c_{\rm hyp}^2\, \frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\!\cdot\!
      \big(\mathsf{D}_o(\hat f)\, \rho\, u_m\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-continuum-F}
\end{align}

with

\begin{equation}
  H_{\rm dil} = \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c}\, H .
  \label{eq:isrfd-Hdil}
\end{equation}

$H_{\rm dil}$ is evaluated in double precision for the energy update and in float
for the flux update.

The code does not store $\boldsymbol{F}_m$ but the reduced flux
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m = \boldsymbol{F}_m/c_{\rm hyp}$, for every
$c_{\rm hyp}$ scheme. In that variable the flux equation reads

\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m}{\partial t} =
    -\big(c_{\rm hyp}\kappa_o + \lambda_m H_{\rm dil}\big)\, \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m
    - c_{\rm hyp}\, \frac{1}{\rho}\nabla\!\cdot\!
      \big(\mathsf{D}_o(\hat f)\, \rho\, u_m\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-continuum-Ft}
\end{equation}

with $\hat f = |\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m|/u_m$ and the divergence of the true
flux in Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-continuum-u}) written $c_{\rm hyp}\,
\rho^{-1}\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m)$. For a uniform
$c_{\rm hyp}$ the two forms are the same equation divided by one constant; for
a per-particle $c_{\rm hyp,i}$ the change of variable is what makes every
operator at particle $i$ the $c_{\rm hyp,i}/c$ multiple of the true-speed one
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-variable-c-operators}). Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-continuum-u}) and
(\ref{eq:isrfd-continuum-F}) are written in the true flux because that is the
physical statement; the discretisation below is in the reduced one.

Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-continuum-u}) and (\ref{eq:isrfd-continuum-F}) are
not written anywhere in the source; they are implemented by the one-step updates
of Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-u-update}) and (\ref{eq:isrfd-F-update}), with the
depths of Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-depth-u}) and (\ref{eq:isrfd-depth-F}).

The one-step solution is \emph{exact} with $S_m$ and
$\nabla\!\cdot\!(\rho\boldsymbol{F}_m)$ frozen, but \textbf{not} including the
artificial dissipation $\Psi_m$, which is decayed together with $u_{\rm prev}$
rather than integrated as a frozen source.

\paragraph{Closure.} The M1 closure is

\begin{align}
  \hat f &= \min\!\left(1, \frac{|\boldsymbol{F}|}{c_M u}\right)
    \ \ \text{for } c_M u > 0, \quad \hat f = 0 \text{ otherwise},
  \label{eq:isrfd-fhat} \\
  \chi &= \frac{3 + 4\hat f^{\,2}}{5 + 2\sqrt{4 - 3\hat f^{\,2}}},
  \label{eq:isrfd-chi} \\
  \mathsf{D} &= \frac{1-\chi}{2}\,\mathsf{I}
    + \frac{3\chi - 1}{2}\, \boldsymbol{n}\otimes\boldsymbol{n},
    \qquad \boldsymbol{n} = \frac{\boldsymbol{F}}{|\boldsymbol{F}|},
    \ \ \boldsymbol{n} = 0 \text{ at } \boldsymbol{F} = 0 .
  \label{eq:isrfd-eddington}
\end{align}

Here $c_M = 1$, because the stored
flux is already reduced, so $\hat f = |\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}|/u$.

\paragraph{Absorption.} Pure dust absorption: no scattering, and no emission term
other than the star source. $\kappa_o$ is the band mass opacity times the local
physical density, with no column.
Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-extinction}'s extinction is a separate,
independently computed quantity and the two are not
interchangeable.

\paragraph{Cosmological terms.} One power of $H$ appears in each equation of a moment, multiplied by its band-edge weight $\lambda_m$. For the energy of a grey band the power is exactly \emph{one}, in both
equations. The reason: $u$ is
\emph{mass}-specific, so the volume dilution is already carried by the physical
gas density it is measured against, and the specific flux loses the same single
power. $H$ is \textbf{dilated} by the same $c_{\rm hyp}/c$ factor as the
absorption term, in double precision in the energy update and in float in the
flux update. The single power holds for the energy of a grey band;
for photon number the grey power is zero, and $\lambda_N$ is the band-edge term alone. The band-edge
weight $\lambda_m$ is derived for $u_m$ and assumed for $\boldsymbol{F}_m$
(Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-lambda}). Unlike $\kappa$, $H$ does not pick
that factor up for free. Leaving it bare suppresses the true-speed fixed point of
the homogeneous equation and stops $c_{\rm hyp}$ cancelling out of it. A
consequence, not a defect: with $\kappa = 0$ the pure-expansion transient decays
at $\lambda_m(c_{\rm hyp}/c)H$, not at $\lambda_m H$. The term vanishes exactly in a
non-cosmological run, because SWIFT sets \code{cosmo->H} $=0$.

The Hubble term is folded \emph{into} the relaxation depth rather than subtracted
explicitly, so $e^{-\mathcal{A}}$ cannot drive $u$ negative at large $H\,dt$.

\paragraph{Comoving bookkeeping of the operators.} Inputs are comoving,
accumulators are physical, and each spatial operator carries one net inverse
length, so each closes with one factor $1/a$.

\paragraph{Implementation.} $H_{\rm dil}$ of Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-Hdil}) is
the variable \code{H\_dilated}, computed in double at \code{radiation\_isrf.c:554}
in \code{radiation\_end\_force\_propagation} and in float at
\code{radiation\_isrf.c:834} in \code{radiation\_end\_gradient\_propagation}. The

and the depths at \code{radiation\_isrf.c:611} and \code{:862}; the depth

is at \code{radiation\_isrf.c:632-633}. The closure tensor is built by
\code{radiation\_build\_m1\_closure\_tensor}
(\code{radiation\_propagation\_iact.h:219-229}), with coefficients from
\code{radiation\_get\_m1\_closure\_coefficients\_band}
(\code{radiation\_propagation\_iact.h:187-208}); $c_M = 1$ is passed by
\code{radiation\_cache\_m1\_closure\_part}
(\code{radiation\_propagation\_iact.h:260-271}). $\kappa_o$ is

\subsection{The discretisation}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-disc}

\subsubsection{Pairwise transport operators}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-pairwise}

Throughout, $\boldsymbol{dx} = \boldsymbol{x}_i - \boldsymbol{x}_j$ and
$r = |\boldsymbol{dx}|$ are \textbf{comoving}, $r^{-1} = 1/r$, every $\rho$ is the
\textbf{comoving} \code{rho\_prev} snapshot, and the kernel derivative the code
calls \code{wi\_dr} is written
\begin{equation}
  W'_i \equiv \frac{dW}{dq}\!\left(\frac{r}{h_i}\right) h_i^{-(d+1)},
  \qquad
  W'_j \equiv \frac{dW}{dq}\!\left(\frac{r}{h_j}\right) h_j^{-(d+1)},
  \label{eq:isrfd-kernel-deriv}
\end{equation}
with $h$ comoving and $d$ the dimension count.

\paragraph{Divergence (force loop).} The stored flux is the
\emph{reduced} flux $\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} = \boldsymbol{F}/c_{\rm hyp}$ for every
scheme (Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-continuum}), so the code's \code{F\_i} and
\code{F\_j} are $\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_i$ and $\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_j$; the
symbols below are written for them:

\begin{align}
  \Phi_{ij} &= \left(
    \frac{\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{dx}}{\rho_i}\,W'_i\,r^{-1}
    + \frac{\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_j\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{dx}}{\rho_j}\,W'_j\,r^{-1}
  \right)\frac{1}{a},
  \label{eq:isrfd-div-phi} \\
  X_i &\mathrel{+}= + c_i m_j \Phi_{ij}, \qquad
  X_j \mathrel{+}= - c_j m_i \Phi_{ij},
  \label{eq:isrfd-div-apply}
\end{align}

where $X$ denotes \code{div\_specific\_flux} and $c_i$ is the receiving particle's
own $c_{\rm hyp}$. $\Phi_{ij}$ is shared by the two sides and each side takes its
\emph{own} speed, so the accumulated $X$ is the divergence of the true flux
$c_{\rm hyp}\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}$ at the receiver.

\textbf{What is conserved exactly.} At the \emph{accumulator} level, per pair,
$m_i X_i / c_{{\rm hyp},i} + m_j X_j / c_{{\rm hyp},j} = 0$ identically,
because both sides share one $\Phi_{ij}$. At a uniform
$c_{\rm hyp}$ this is proportional to $m_i X_i + m_j X_j = 0$. The exactness is
a property of the accumulator, not of the applied update: $u$ is advanced by
$dt_i\,\varphi_i\,X_i$ per particle, so the
pairwise cancellation survives into $u$ only where $dt_i = dt_j$ and
$\varphi_i = \varphi_j$, i.e.\ same time bin and same relaxation depth. On one
rank, a pair across time bins is booked once instead; see the paragraph on
pairs across time bins below.

The divergence sits in the \emph{force} loop, not a type-1 loop, because it needs
both sides of every pair credited and only the type-2 dispatch fires both sides
whenever either kernel reaches.

\paragraph{Gradient (gradient loop).}

\begin{align}
  \boldsymbol{T}_i &= \big(\mathsf{D}_i\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{dx}\big)\,\rho_i u_i\, r^{-1},
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol{T}_j = \big(\mathsf{D}_j\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{dx}\big)\,\rho_j u_j\, r^{-1},
  \label{eq:isrfd-grad-temp} \\
  \mathcal{F}_i &= \frac{m_j}{\rho_i^2}\,W'_i\,\frac{1}{a},
  \qquad
  \mathcal{F}_j = \frac{m_i}{\rho_j^2}\,W'_j\,\frac{1}{a},
  \label{eq:isrfd-grad-fac} \\
  \boldsymbol{G}_i &\mathrel{+}= -\big(\boldsymbol{T}_i - \boldsymbol{T}_j\big)\mathcal{F}_i,
  \qquad
  \boldsymbol{G}_j \mathrel{+}= -\big(\boldsymbol{T}_i - \boldsymbol{T}_j\big)\mathcal{F}_j,
  \label{eq:isrfd-grad-apply}
\end{align}

Each particle uses its \emph{own} kernel derivative and its
\emph{own} closure tensor, with no shared average and no grad-$h$ \code{forcef}
factor. Both members take the same difference
$\boldsymbol{T}_i - \boldsymbol{T}_j$ with a factor of one sign
($W'_i, W'_j \le 0$), so the pair term is symmetric, not antisymmetric:
$m_i\,\delta\boldsymbol{G}_i + m_j\,\delta\boldsymbol{G}_j \ne 0$. The
estimator is consistent, $\boldsymbol{G} = 0$ for a uniform
$\rho u\,\mathsf{D}$, and it does not conserve the flux sum
$\sum_i m_i\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_i/c_{{\rm hyp},i}$; the derivation is
Eq.~(\ref{eq:fuv-grad-kernel-error}). Paired with the divergence's
shared-coefficient form it is skew-adjoint in the $m\rho$ inner product
only where the closure tensors of neighbours agree
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}).

Both operators read \code{rho\_prev}, never the live \code{p->rho}: during the
density loop \code{p->rho} is a partial sum, and the
skew-adjoint pairing needs both operators built from the same densities.

\paragraph{Dissipation reference (density loop).}

\begin{align}
  \code{ngb\_mean\_abs\_u\_V}_i &\mathrel{+}= \frac{m_j}{\rho_j}\, w_i\,
    \big|\rho_j\, u_{{\rm prev},j}\big|,
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-ref-i} \\
  \code{ngb\_mean\_abs\_u\_V}_j &\mathrel{+}= \frac{m_i}{\rho_i}\, w_j\,
    \big|\rho_i\, u_{{\rm prev},i}\big| .
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-ref-j}
\end{align}

There is deliberately no $1/a$ factor: its only consumer divides it into
$\rho_{\rm prev} u$, which carries the same $a^3$ weight.

\paragraph{Dissipation source (force loop).}

\begin{align}
  d_{ij} &= \rho_i u_i - \rho_j u_j,
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-d} \\
  \alpha_{ij} &= \max\big(\alpha^{\rm trig}_i,\ \alpha^{\rm trig}_j,\
    \alpha^{\rm floor}_i,\ \alpha^{\rm floor}_j\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-alpha} \\
  \overline{W}_{ij} &= \tfrac{1}{2}\big(W'_i + W'_j\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-wbar} \\
  \Upsilon_{ij} &= \frac{d_{ij}\,\overline{W}_{ij}}{\rho_i \rho_j}\,\frac{1}{a},
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-shape} \\
  \code{dissipation\_u}_i &\mathrel{+}= + m_j\, \alpha_{ij} c_i \Upsilon_{ij},
  \qquad
  \code{dissipation\_u}_j \mathrel{+}= - m_i\, \alpha_{ij} c_j \Upsilon_{ij},
  \label{eq:isrfd-diss-apply}
\end{align}

with two receiver-side speeds rather than one
shared minimum. The accumulator-level antisymmetry, and the caveat about the
applied update, are the same as for the divergence.

The dissipation is applied to a pair \emph{ungated}, with no mutual-reach test,
because the force loop fires both sides whenever either kernel reaches. The four
operators carry no
floating-point annotation: the compiler may reassociate them under
\code{-ffast-math}, so a rerun after a compiler or flag change can move the last
bits of the accumulators without changing the discrete scheme.

\paragraph{Why a pair across time bins needs a booking.} A scheme that
updates only the active particles meets the following problem at a pair of
unequal steps. The finer member $f$ is active and the coarser member $c$ is
not. If the pair is summed for $f$ alone, the energy that leaves $f$ is never
added to $c$, and the sum $\sum m\,u$ changes by that amount at every such
pair and step. The explicit scheme of the metal diffusion of
\citet[Appendix~C]{Correa2026} is of this kind. An inactive neighbour takes
part in the sum of the active particle, but its own value is not updated, and
the loss or gain of the conserved quantity is accepted as small. For two
active particles of unequal steps that scheme uses
$dt_{ij} = \min(dt_i, dt_j)$ on both sides, as the booking here does. It has no
pending amount, no conservation check with a bar and no treatment of MPI ranks.
The booking below removes the loss by construction: the amount that $f$ gives
up over $dt_f$ is held for $c$ and added at its next update. The pair then
conserves energy, and the ledger residual of
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-ledger-residual}) tests it.

\paragraph{Pairs across time bins.} A force pair whose members
have unequal steps is booked once, over the finer member's step $dt_f$. The
finer member $f$ takes its rate from the pair as above. The coarser member $c$
takes no rate from the pair. It accumulates two pending amounts per moment,
formed with $c_{\rm hyp} = 1$ on its side:
\begin{equation}
  P^{\rm tr}_c \mathrel{+}= -X^{(1)}_c\, dt_f,
  \qquad
  P^{\rm d}_c \mathrel{+}= \code{dissipation\_u}^{(1)}_c\, dt_f\, \varphi_f,
  \label{eq:isrfd-pending}
\end{equation}
where the superscript $(1)$ marks the pair's contribution at unit speed and
$\varphi_f = \varphi(c_{{\rm hyp},f}\,\kappa_f\, dt_f)$ is the finer member's
integrating factor. By the accumulator identity, this is the exact opposite of
the finer member's change of $u$ plus absorbed energy, in
$c_{\rm hyp}$-weighted units: transport enters with no $\varphi$, dissipation
with the finer member's $\varphi$. At its own next update, after
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-u-update}), the coarser member adds
\begin{equation}
  \Delta u_c = c_{{\rm hyp},c}\big(\mathcal{E}_c\, P^{\rm d}_c
    + \varphi_c\, P^{\rm tr}_c\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-pending-add}
\end{equation}
books the rest of $c_{{\rm hyp},c}(P^{\rm d}_c + P^{\rm tr}_c)$ as absorbed,
and zeroes both amounts. Each amount thus enters $u$ plus absorbed energy with
weight one, split by the coarser member's own depth as its own terms are. The
LW absorbed share of the pending amount feeds the band-edge transfer, as in the
update. With this booking the $c_{\rm hyp}$-weighted energy ledger is exact
across time bins, up to two terms. The first is the same-step term above:
members of one bin with unequal $\varphi$ or $c_{\rm hyp}$. The second is the
Hubble term, which $\varphi_f$ leaves out of the finer member's depth. It is
first order in $\lambda\,(c_{\rm hyp}/c)\,H\,dt$, and since
$c_{\rm hyp}\,dt \le C_{\rm hyp} h_{\rm phys}$ its relative size is at most
$\lambda\, C_{\rm hyp} h_{\rm phys} H / (2c)$, negligible for any $h$ much
smaller than the Hubble length.

The booking needs the extra ghost, which marks each member's active step. On
several MPI ranks, two gaps must be closed for the coarser member to receive
its amount. Each rank writes only its own particles, so the amount must be
booked by the rank that owns $c$.

\emph{First gap, the pair does not run.} A force pair runs on a rank only if
one of its cells is active there. If $c$ is inactive and local, and $f$ is
active and foreign, no cell is active on the rank of $c$, so the pair does not
run on that rank. The rank of $f$ then books into its copy of $c$, and nothing
sends the amount back. The force pair is therefore also run on the rank of
$c$. The macro \code{MPI\_SYMMETRIC\_FORCE\_INTERACTION} enables this. It also
makes each force task depend on the sorts of its cells. The default hydro scheme does not define the
macro, so \code{part.h} defines it when the build has MPI, the GEAR feedback,
the ISRF and the extra hydro loop. Without the ISRF the macro stays off.

\emph{Second gap, the pair loop skips half of the pairs.} Even when the pair
task runs, the pair loop of an inactive local particle visits an active foreign
one only for $h_f \le r < h_c$. The other part, $r < h_f$, belongs to a loop
that visits active particles only, so $c$ is not reached. The standard
interactions never need that part, because neither particle changes. The pair
loop of the force task therefore has two extra visit passes under MPI, one for
each orientation of the cells. They call the dissipation interaction only and
skip pairs where $c$ is active.

\subsubsection{The exact-exponential relaxation update of $u$ (end-force ghost)}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-u-update}

With $dt = \code{dt\_prev}$:

\begin{align}
  \mathcal{R} &= \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c},
  \qquad H_{\rm dil} = \mathcal{R}\, H,
  \label{eq:isrfd-rescale} \\
  \mathcal{A}_m &= \big(c_{\rm hyp}\kappa_o + \lambda_m H_{\rm dil}\big)\, dt,
  \label{eq:isrfd-depth-u} \\
  \mathcal{E}_m &= e^{-\mathcal{A}_m},
  \qquad
  \varphi_m = \frac{1 - e^{-\mathcal{A}_m}}{\mathcal{A}_m},
  \label{eq:isrfd-decay-u}
\end{align}

The update itself is

\begin{equation}
  u_m^{n+1} = \mathcal{E}_m\Big(u_{{\rm prev},m}
    + dt\,\varphi_m\,\code{dissipation\_u}_m\Big)
    + dt\,\varphi_m\Big(\mathcal{R}\,S_m - X_m\Big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-u-update}
\end{equation}

plus, for $m = \code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_PE}$
only, a raw add of the band-edge transfer of
Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-transfer},

\begin{equation}
  u_{\rm PE}^{n+1} \mathrel{+}= \mathcal{T},
  \label{eq:isrfd-u-transfer-add}
\end{equation}

kept as a separate statement so that
\code{-ffast-math} cannot merge it into a form that no longer vanishes exactly at
$H = 0$.

Both $dt$ and $c_{\rm hyp}$ are read from the particle and never recomputed: the
call site's own $dt$ uses a different step-begin convention, and using it would
desynchronise this update from the flux update, so the update takes no $dt$
argument at all.

The integrating factor is evaluated in float for the flux update and in double
for the energy update:

\begin{equation}
  \varphi(\mathcal{A}) =
  \begin{cases}
    1 - \dfrac{\mathcal{A}}{2} + \dfrac{\mathcal{A}^2}{6}, & \mathcal{A} < 10^{-6},\\[2ex]
    -\dfrac{\code{expm1}(-\mathcal{A})}{\mathcal{A}}, & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:isrfd-phi}
\end{equation}

As
$\mathcal{A}\to 0$, $\varphi \to 1$ and $\mathcal{E}\to 1$, so
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-u-update}) degenerates to plain forward Euler transport,
$u^{n+1} = u_{\rm prev} + dt(S - X + \Psi)$. As $\mathcal{A}\to\infty$,
$\varphi \to 1/\mathcal{A}$, i.e.\ exact relaxation to the local target each
step. The $10^{-6}$ Taylor branch exists to avoid the $0/0$ cancellation.

$u$ is advanced in \textbf{double} throughout; the double
$\varphi$ exists specifically because the $u$-path depth can be a few $10^{-8}$.
The update is \emph{idempotent}: $u$ is
rebuilt from $u_{\rm prev}$ plus this step's accumulators, never incremented.
The exceptions are the two debug ledger counters and the pending add of
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-pending-add}), which follows the rebuild. No flux limiter is applied
here: the extra ghost already limited this step's flux against $u^n$.

\subsubsection{The exact-exponential relaxation update of $\boldsymbol{F}$ (extra ghost)}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-F-update}

Everything here is \textbf{float},
including $H_{\rm dil}$ and the \code{expf}. The stored flux is the reduced flux
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m$, so the update carries one power of $c_{\rm hyp}$
less than the true-flux recurrence:

\begin{align}
  H_{\rm dil} &= \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c}\, H,
  \label{eq:isrfd-Hdil-float} \\
  \mathcal{A}_m &= \big(c_{\rm hyp}\kappa_o + \lambda_m H_{\rm dil}\big)\, dt,
  \qquad
  \mathcal{E}_m = e^{-\mathcal{A}_m},
  \qquad
  \varphi_m = \varphi(\mathcal{A}_m),
  \label{eq:isrfd-depth-F} \\
  \mathcal{C}_m &= c_{\rm hyp}\, dt\, \varphi_m,
  \label{eq:isrfd-coeff-F} \\
  \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m^{n+1} &= \mathcal{E}_m \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}_m
    - \mathcal{C}_m\, \boldsymbol{G}_m,
  \label{eq:isrfd-F-update}
\end{align}

Substituting
$\boldsymbol{F} = c_{\rm hyp}\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}$ into the true-flux recurrence
$\boldsymbol{F}^{n+1} = \mathcal{E}\boldsymbol{F} - c_{\rm hyp}^2\,dt\,\varphi\,
\boldsymbol{G}$ and dividing by the step-constant $c_{\rm hyp}$ gives
Eq.~(\ref{eq:isrfd-F-update}).

\textbf{Precision asymmetry}, recorded as a property of the scheme. The same
moment's $u$ and $\boldsymbol{F}$ relax with depths computed at different
precision, double for $u$ and float for $\boldsymbol{F}$, from the same inputs.
At the few-$10^{-8}$ depths
that the $u$ path can reach, the float depth
is unresolvable, so the flux decay is effectively 1 there while the energy decay
is not. The flux path stays in float because $\boldsymbol{F}$ itself is float, so
a double $\varphi$ there would not change what gets stored. That sits in tension
with the requirement that $\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}$ and $u$ of one moment relax at
the \emph{same} rate, or the reduced flux
$\hat f = |\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}|/u$ that the closure reads is corrupted.

\paragraph{M1 flux limiter.} Decided once per \emph{operator} from its owning
moment and applied identically to every
moment sharing that operator:

\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} \longleftarrow
  \begin{cases}
    0, & u \le 0,\\
    \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} \ \text{(untouched, no multiply)}, & |\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}|^2 \le 0,\\
    \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}\,\min\!\left(1, \dfrac{u}{|\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}|}\right), & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:isrfd-limiter}
\end{equation}

$\boldsymbol{F}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{F}$ and the ratio are formed in
\textbf{double}, because in float32
$\boldsymbol{F}\!\cdot\!\boldsymbol{F}$ underflows once
$|\boldsymbol{F}| \lesssim \sqrt{\code{FLT\_MIN}} \approx 1.1\times10^{-19}$
internal, which would skip the limiter for a nonzero flux. The three outcomes
are a \code{switch}, not one scale value, so the skip case executes no multiply at
all under \code{-ffast-math}.

The limiter removes flux and returns it nowhere. It conserves $u$ and
not the flux sum; at one fixed $c_{\rm hyp}$ it is the largest flux leak
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-flux-conservation}).

\code{div\_specific\_flux} is zeroed here, not at the drift, so a snapshot keeps
the last step's value.

\subsubsection{Artificial dissipation: trigger, floor, gate}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-dissipation}

Defaults, all parsed unconditionally so a validation run can set them with
propagation off: \code{ISRF\_dissipation\_alpha\_max} $=0.5$,
\code{ISRF\_dissipation\_negativity\_threshold} $\epsilon_1 = 0.01$,
\code{ISRF\_dissipation\_alpha\_floor} $=0.5$,
\code{ISRF\_dissipation\_floor\_h\_over\_lambda} $\epsilon_\lambda = 0.5$,
\code{ISRF\_dissipation\_floor\_relaxation\_residual} $\epsilon_R = 0.40$
and \code{ISRF\_dissipation\_alpha\_pin\_for\_debugging}
$=0$. Elsewhere: \code{ISRF\_c\_hyp\_margin} $=0.5$,
with \code{ISRF\_c\_hyp\_fixed\_fraction\_of\_c} disabled
by default. Ranges are
enforced at parse time: $\alpha_{\max} \ge 0$,
$\epsilon_1 \in (0,1]$, $\alpha_{\rm floor} \ge 0$,
$\epsilon_\lambda > 0$,
$\epsilon_R \in [0,1]$.

\paragraph{Trigger.} Evaluated once per operator per step in the
extra ghost, with $u_V = \rho_{\rm prev}\, u$:

\begin{align}
  u_V^{\rm ref} &= \max\big(\code{ngb\_mean\_abs\_u\_V},\ -u_V\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-trig-ref} \\
  \epsilon &=
    \begin{cases}
      -u_V / u_V^{\rm ref}, & u_V < 0 \ \text{and}\ u_V^{\rm ref} > 0,\\
      0, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
  \label{eq:isrfd-trig-eps} \\
  x &= \min\!\left(\frac{\epsilon}{\epsilon_1},\, 1\right),
  \qquad
  \alpha^{\rm aim} = \alpha_{\max}\, x^2 (3 - 2x),
  \label{eq:isrfd-trig-aim} \\
  \alpha^{\rm trig} &=
    \begin{cases}
      \alpha^{\rm aim}, & \alpha^{\rm aim} \ge \alpha^{\rm trig}_{\rm prev},\\
      \alpha^{\rm aim} + \big(\alpha^{\rm trig}_{\rm prev} - \alpha^{\rm aim}\big)\mathcal{E}_\alpha,
        & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
  \label{eq:isrfd-trig-apply} \\
  \mathcal{E}_\alpha &= \exp\!\left(
    -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}\, dt}{5.0\, h_{\rm phys}} - c_{\rm hyp}\kappa\, dt\right),
  \label{eq:isrfd-trig-decay}
\end{align}

with the decay length $5.0$ a compile-time constant. Instant rise, exponential
decay. The decay memory is the
trigger's \emph{own} previous value, not the combined coefficient, so a high floor
cannot hold up the trigger's decay tail. The trigger responds to
\textbf{negativity only} and is exactly zero on a positive front. The test
$u_V < 0 \wedge u_V^{\rm ref} > 0$ in
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-trig-eps}) is exact rather than assumed, because
$\rho_{\rm prev}u$ can narrow to $-0$ for a faint band while the unnarrowed
double still reads negative, which would make the division $0/0$.

\paragraph{Floor.}

\begin{equation}
  x = \frac{h_{\rm phys}\,\kappa}{\epsilon_\lambda},
  \qquad
  \alpha^{\rm floor} = \frac{\alpha_{\rm floor}}{1 + x^4}.
  \label{eq:isrfd-floor}
\end{equation}

The roll-off is quartic, not quadratic, so an optically thick region
($h\kappa \sim 6$--$10$) gets about $10^{-5}$ of the floor rather than a
percent-level residue. The floor enters a
pair unconditionally through Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-diss-alpha}); this roll-off
and the gate below are its only gating.

\paragraph{Relaxation-residual gate.} The gate multiplies the floor's aim. Let
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}$ be the
\emph{pre-update} reduced flux, snapshotted per operator before the moment loop
overwrites it:

\begin{align}
  &\epsilon_R \le 0 \ \Rightarrow\ s = 1; \qquad c_{\rm hyp} \le 0 \ \Rightarrow\ s = 1,
  \label{eq:isrfd-gate-early} \\
  w &= \kappa + \frac{\lambda\, H}{c} \quad \text{(the \textbf{true} $c$, not $c_{\rm hyp}$)},
  \qquad w \le 0 \ \Rightarrow\ s = 1,
  \label{eq:isrfd-gate-w} \\
  N_R &= \big| w\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} + \boldsymbol{G} \big|,
  \qquad
  D_R = w\,|\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}| + |\boldsymbol{G}|,
  \qquad D_R \le 0 \ \Rightarrow\ s = 0,
  \label{eq:isrfd-gate-num-den} \\
  R &= \frac{N_R}{D_R},
  \qquad
  s = \min\!\left(1, \left(\frac{R}{\epsilon_R}\right)^{\!2}\right),
  \label{eq:isrfd-gate-s}
\end{align}

Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-gate-num-den}) is the discrete fixed point of the
unlimited recurrence
$\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}^{n+1} = \mathcal{E}\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} - \mathcal{C}\boldsymbol{G}$.
With $1 - \mathcal{E} = \mathcal{A}\varphi$ and
$\mathcal{A} = c_{\rm hyp} w\, dt$, the factor $\mathcal{C} = c_{\rm hyp}\,dt\,\varphi$
cancels against $\mathcal{A}\varphi$ and the fixed point is

\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} = -\frac{1}{w}\boldsymbol{G}
  \ \Longleftrightarrow\ w\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}} + \boldsymbol{G} = 0,
  \label{eq:isrfd-gate-fp-reduced}
\end{equation}

independent of $c_{\rm hyp}$. $c_{\rm hyp}$ enters the gate only through
the $c_{\rm hyp} \le 0$ guard. $N_R$, $D_R$, $R$ and $R^2$ are all formed in
\textbf{double}, because the squared denominator underflows float32 under this
build's folding of the ratio and its square into one division; $D_R \le 0$ is
branched rather than epsilon-guarded for the same reason.

\subsubsection{The $c_{\rm hyp}$ schemes}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-chyp}

There are two schemes. Each fixes the speed
rule; the pairwise operators are the same for both, and both store the reduced
flux $\tilde{\boldsymbol{F}}$. Any other value stops the run: at parse time, at
the drift and density-ghost dispatch, and on a restart, where the restored value
is checked separately because the flat restart read bypasses the parser; a
restart written with a removed scheme cannot be resumed. A restart that stores
the true flux
cannot be resumed either, because this code would read it as the reduced flux,
wrong by a factor $c_{\rm hyp}$. The restart stream therefore starts with a
marker block naming the stored particle layout, and a file without it, or with
another value, stops the run. The current layout is 3: the reduced flux plus
the pending amounts of pairs across time bins, with $\varphi$-weighted
dissipation (Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-pairwise}). Layout 1 (reduced flux,
no pending amounts) and layout 2 stop the run. A restart file
written by an older code, which stores the true flux and has no marker, stops
the run, so such a run is rerun, never resumed.

With $h_{\rm phys} = a\, h$ (note: \emph{not} $\gamma_K h$) and $dt_{\max}(i)$
the physical step at \code{max\_ngb\_time\_bin}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{p{4.6cm}p{9.1cm}}
\hline
scheme & speed formula \\
\hline
2 \code{fixed\_fraction} & $c_{\rm hyp} = f_c\, c$ \\
4 \code{kernel\_local\_reduced\_flux} & $c_{\rm hyp} = \min(C_{\rm hyp} h_{\rm phys}/dt_{\max}(i),\, c)$ \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Scheme 4 is the \textbf{default}.
Here $f_c = \code{ISRF\_c\_hyp\_fixed\_fraction\_of\_c}$ and
$C_{\rm hyp} = \code{ISRF\_c\_hyp\_margin}$, default $0.5$.

The \code{fixed\_fraction} scheme decides $c_{\rm hyp}$ at the drift; the
\code{kernel\_local} scheme defers it to the density ghost,
once the $h$-iteration has converged, and the
drift only caches $dt_i$ for it.
\code{max\_ngb\_time\_bin} is accumulated unconditionally, one byte compare per
pair, and reset to
the particle's own bin each $h$-iteration.

A degenerate $dt_{\max} \le 0$ returns $c$ \emph{directly} rather than relying on
$\min(+\infty, c)$, which \code{-ffast-math} does not guarantee; this guard
exists only in the density ghost.
$dt_{\max}(i)$ reuses \code{dt\_prev} in the same-bin case rather than
recomputing. At parse time $f_c > 0$ and the \code{fixed\_fraction} scheme are
forced to agree. A fixed propagation speed $v$ is
the \code{fixed\_fraction} scheme with $f_c = v/c$. SWIFT
ignores a parameter file key that no code reads. A stale key therefore has no
effect, and the user must remove it from the parameter file by hand.

\subsubsection{The timestep each scheme imposes}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-timestep}

Only the \code{fixed\_fraction} scheme imposes a radiation timestep:

\begin{equation}
  dt_{\rm rad} = \frac{C_{\rm hyp}\, h_{\rm phys}}{f_c\, c}.
  \label{eq:isrfd-dt-rad}
\end{equation}

The bound is \code{FLT\_MAX}, i.e.\ no
constraint, if $f_c \le 0$, if
\code{ISRF\_propagation} is off, if the debug off-switch is set,
if the particle is not near-field, or if
$h_{\rm phys} \le 0$ (an explicit branch rather than a clamp,
because a clamp does not shield a NaN under \code{-ffast-math}). The near-field
set is: \code{is\_illuminated\_ISRF}, or
$u_{\rm PE} \ne 0$, or $u_{\rm LW} \ne 0$, or either operator's
\code{ngb\_mean\_abs\_u\_V} $>0$. \code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} is deliberately
omitted, because first init forces it nonzero if and only if $u_{\rm LW}$ is.
The run additionally stops if the \code{fixed\_fraction} bound falls below
\code{TimeIntegration:dt\_min}, and the error names the parameter.

Scheme 4 imposes \textbf{no} radiation timestep at all: its $c_{\rm hyp}$ is
already derived from a timestep. It builds
$c_{\rm hyp}$ from $dt_{\max}(i)$, so the receiver bound
$c_i\, dt_j \le C_{\rm hyp} h_i$ holds for every neighbour the density loop
reached, i.e.\ every $j$ inside $\mathcal{H}_i$,
including a neighbour on a coarser bin.
This does not cover the $\mathcal{H}_i \le r < \mathcal{H}_j$ pair class
(Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-limits}, item~\ref{it:isrfd-uncovered}).

Absorption imposes no timestep limit by construction: the exponential relaxation
is exact at any $\kappa\, dt$.

\subsubsection{The dose reservoir and its drawdown}
\label{sec:isrfd-reservoir}

Every star's touch only adds to the reservoir, so any number of stars on any
time bins superpose without losing or double-counting emission.
Drawdown happens at the drift, for active particles only. Let
$t_{\rm begin}$ be the start of the particle's current step and $t_{\rm end}$
the time at which the reservoir must be empty, both on SWIFT's integer time
line; let $\tau_{\rm base}$ be the length of one integer time unit, and
$\Delta t_{\rm prop}(t_1, t_2)$ the proper time elapsed between two integer
times $t_1$ and $t_2$. Then:

\begin{align}
  t_{\rm rem} &=
    \begin{cases}
      0, & t_{\rm end} \le t_{\rm begin},\\
      \Delta t_{\rm prop}(t_{\rm begin}, t_{\rm end}), & \text{with cosmology},\\
      (t_{\rm end} - t_{\rm begin})\,\tau_{\rm base}, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
  \label{eq:isrfd-trem} \\
  f_{\rm draw} &=
    \begin{cases}
      1, & t_{\rm rem} \le dt_{\rm phys},\\
      dt_{\rm phys}/t_{\rm rem}, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
  \label{eq:isrfd-fdraw} \\
  S_m &= \frac{f_{\rm draw}\, \code{u\_dose\_reservoir}_m}{dt_{\rm phys}},
  \qquad
  \code{u\_dose\_reservoir}_m \mathrel{-}= f_{\rm draw}\, \code{u\_dose\_reservoir}_m,
  \label{eq:isrfd-drawdown}
\end{align}

Using the current time
instead of $t_{\rm begin}$ would over-drain and empty
the reservoir one sub-step early, and
$dt_{\rm phys} > 0$ is required so the refund below is exact. A particle whose
$h$-iteration gives up with
no neighbours is refunded exactly the amount subtracted:
$\code{u\_dose\_reservoir} \mathrel{+}= S_m\, \code{dt\_prev}$ with
$S_m \leftarrow 0$.

\paragraph{Implementation.} The drawdown is in
\code{radiation\_snapshot\_part\_propagation} (\code{radiation\_isrf.c:151}),
lines 225-251. Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-trem}), (\ref{eq:isrfd-fdraw}) and
(\ref{eq:isrfd-drawdown}) are at \code{radiation\_isrf.c:232-240},
\code{:241-242} and \code{:245-246}. In Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-trem}),
$t_{\rm begin}$ is \code{ti\_begin} $=$
\code{get\_integer\_time\_begin}(\code{ti\_current}, \code{time\_bin})
(\code{radiation\_isrf.c:200-201}), $t_{\rm end}$ is the field
\code{ISRF\_reservoir\_end\_ti}, $\Delta t_{\rm prop}$ is
\code{cosmology\_get\_delta\_time} (\code{radiation\_isrf.c:236-237}) and
$\tau_{\rm base}$ is \code{time\_base}. The choice of \code{ti\_begin} is

\subsection{Injection}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-injection}

Injection runs in the star feedback-apply loop. It acts only when
$L_{\rm band}(\mathrm{PE}) \ne 0$ or $L_{\rm band}(\mathrm{LW}) \ne 0$.

\subsubsection{Kernel weighting and normalisation}

\begin{align}
  r &= \sqrt{\max\big(r^2,\ 10^{-6} h_i^2\big)},
  \label{eq:isrfd-inj-r} \\
  w_i &= W(r/h_i)\, h_i^{-d},
  \label{eq:isrfd-inj-w} \\
  \omega_{ij} &= \frac{m_j\, w_i}{\code{enrichment\_weight}_i},
  \label{eq:isrfd-inj-weight}
\end{align}

with
$r$ and $h_i$ \textbf{comoving} and $d$ the dimension count. Here
\code{enrichment\_weight} is $\sum_j m_j W(r/h_i)$ accumulated in the star
density loop and multiplied by $h_i^{-d}$ once that loop is complete, i.e.\ it
\emph{is} the
SPH density estimate at the star; a value of 0 gives
$\omega_{ij} = 0$ rather than a division. The injected energy per pair, per
moment, is

\begin{equation}
  \Delta U_m = \Delta t_\star\, \omega_{ij}\, L_{\rm band}(m)\,
    \mathcal{X}_{o(m)},
  \label{eq:isrfd-inj-energy}
\end{equation}

with $\mathcal{X}$ the extinction factor of
Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-extinction}. $\Delta t_\star$ is the star's own
cached feedback step in proper time, asserted against a stale step boundary under
\code{SWIFT\_DEBUG\_CHECKS}.

The deposit depends on the mode:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{ISRF\_propagation} \textbf{on}: pure accumulation into the reservoir,
    $\code{u\_dose\_reservoir}_m \mathrel{+}= \Delta U_m / m_j$ in float, plus
    $\code{ISRF\_reservoir\_end\_ti} = \max(\text{old},\
    \code{ti\_current} + \code{ti\_step\_star})$. There is no reset and no
    first-touch logic, so any number of stars on any time bins superpose.
  \item \code{ISRF\_propagation} \textbf{off}: $u$ is an \emph{instantaneous}
    strength. On the first touch of the step every band's $u$ is zeroed,
    then
    $u_m \mathrel{+}= \Delta U_m/m_j$ in double, so
    \begin{equation}
      u_m = \frac{1}{m_j}\sum_{\star} \Delta t_\star\, \omega_{\star j}\,
        L_{\rm band}(m)\, \mathcal{X}_{o(m)},
      \label{eq:isrfd-inj-instantaneous}
    \end{equation}
    which scales linearly with the
    illuminating star's own timestep, and so does $G_0$.
\end{itemize}

The illumination window is renewed on every touch,
$\code{ISRF\_illumination\_end\_ti} = \code{ti\_current} + 2\,\code{ti\_step\_star}$,
and a time-step synchronisation of the particle is requested on the first
touch only.

\subsubsection{Dust extinction factor}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-extinction}

\begin{align}
  \frac{\mathcal{D}(Z)}{\mathcal{D}_{\rm MW}} &=
    \frac{\max(Z, 0)}{0.01295}\cdot\frac{f_{\rm gr}}{0.009387},
  \label{eq:isrfd-dust-ratio} \\
  \kappa_{\rm eff} &= \frac{\sigma_d(b)\,\mathcal{D}(Z)/\mathcal{D}_{\rm MW}}
    {\mu_H\, m_p\, \code{units\_cgs}(\mathrm{AREA})},
  \label{eq:isrfd-kappa-eff} \\
  \mathcal{X} &= \exp\big(-\kappa_{\rm eff}\, \Sigma_{\rm gas}\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-extinction} \\
  \Sigma_{\rm gas} &= \ell_{\rm ext}\, \rho\, a^{-2},
  \label{eq:isrfd-column}
\end{align}

In Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-column}) the
$\rho$ is the receiver's \textbf{comoving} \code{p->rho} and $\ell_{\rm ext}$ is
comoving, so the $a^{-2}$ makes $\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ a \textbf{physical} column, in
mass per area.

must be kept distinct from the per-particle $\kappa$ of

(1/length) equal to $\kappa_{\rm eff}\,\rho_{\rm phys}$. The two are computed
independently and are not interchangeable.
$f_{\rm gr}$ is the \emph{resolved}
\code{chemistry\_data.local\_dust\_to\_gas\_ratio}, never the $-1$ sentinel. The
factor is band-specific and computed per operator.

\subsubsection{Extinction path: three mechanisms}

The extinction path uses
$h_{\rm gas} = \gamma_K\, h$. The default
mechanism is \code{pair\_separation}.

\begin{align}
  \text{\code{constant\_kernel\_path}:}\quad
    \ell_{\rm ext} &= R_{\rm ext}\, \gamma_K\, h_j,
  \label{eq:isrfd-path-constant} \\
  \text{\code{pair\_separation} (default):}\quad
    \ell_{\rm ext} &= r,
  \label{eq:isrfd-path-pair} \\
  \text{\code{temperature\_capped\_jeans}:}\quad
    \ell_{\rm ext} &= \min\!\left(\lambda_{\rm J}\, a^{-1},\ \gamma_K h_j\right),
  \label{eq:isrfd-path-jeans}
\end{align}

with
$R_{\rm ext}$ the parameter \code{ISRF\_extinction\_path\_in\_kernel\_radii},
default 1.0, and the Jeans length built
from a temperature-capped sound speed:

\begin{equation}
  \theta = \min\!\left(1, \frac{T_{\rm cap}}{T}\right),
  \qquad
  c_s^2\big|_{\rm capped} = c_s^2\, \theta,
  \qquad
  \lambda_{\rm J} = \sqrt{\frac{\pi\, c_s^2\big|_{\rm capped}}{G\, \rho_{\rm phys}}},
  \label{eq:isrfd-jeans}
\end{equation}

with
$T_{\rm cap} = 40$\,K by default.

Guards: $T \le 0$ or $\rho_{\rm phys} \le 0$ returns $h_{\rm gas}$ outright,
rather than clamping a division, because a guarded denominator is not safe under
\code{-ffast-math}. An unrecognised
mechanism string is fatal, and an
unknown enum value reaching the switch is fatal. Each mechanism's magnitude key
is parsed
only by the mechanism that reads it, so \code{used\_parameters.yml} records a
number only where it was used. The
value $R_{\rm ext} = 5/12$ is exact in the uniform optically thin limit, as
stated for \code{pair\_separation} above.

\subsubsection{The exact identity at zero metallicity}
\label{sec:isrfd-zero-Z}

At $Z = 0$, Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-dust-ratio}) gives
$\mathcal{D}(Z)/\mathcal{D}_{\rm MW} = 0$,
hence $\kappa_{\rm eff} = 0$ and
$\mathcal{X} = e^{0} = 1$ exactly for both bands. Summing the deposited
\emph{energy} over the star's neighbours then gives

\begin{equation}
  \sum_j m_j\,\frac{\Delta U_m}{m_j}
  = \Delta t_\star\, L_{\rm band}(m) \sum_j \frac{m_j w_i}{\rho_\star}
  = \Delta t_\star\, L_{\rm band}(m),
  \label{eq:isrfd-inj-identity}
\end{equation}

because $\rho_\star = \sum_j m_j w_i$ is the same kernel sum. So the injection
conserves the star's band energy over the step exactly, with three documented
exceptions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item the apply loop floors $r$ at $10^{-6}h_i^2$
    while the density loop that built
    \code{enrichment\_weight} does not, so a
    coincident pair breaks the identity;
  \item the reservoir accumulator is \textbf{float}, unlike $u$ itself;
  \item the identity holds for the \emph{deposited dose}. What reaches $u$ is
    $(c_{\rm hyp}/c)\times\text{dose}$ plus the relaxation factors:
    injection deposits the raw, unrescaled dose
    and the rescale is applied exclusively in the end-force update.
\end{itemize}

The injection applies no $\varphi$.

with \code{feedback.c:638}. The floored apply loop is

\subsection{The band-edge transfer, LW into PE}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-transfer}

Computed before the moment loop, from LW's own depth and frozen inputs, so the
result cannot depend on moment ordering. The whole block is gated on
$H_{\rm dil} \ne 0$:

\begin{align}
  \mathcal{A}_{\rm LW} &= \big(c_{\rm hyp}\kappa_{\rm LW}
    + \lambda_E(\mathrm{LW})\, H_{\rm dil}\big)\, dt,
  \label{eq:isrfd-transfer-depth} \\
  1 - \mathcal{E}_{\rm LW} &= -\code{expm1}(-\mathcal{A}_{\rm LW}),
  \qquad
  \varphi_{\rm LW} = \varphi(\mathcal{A}_{\rm LW}) \ \text{[double]},
  \label{eq:isrfd-transfer-decay} \\
  \mathcal{L}_{\rm LW} &=
    \Big(u_{{\rm prev},{\rm LW}} + dt\,\varphi_{\rm LW}\,
      \code{dissipation\_u}_{\rm LW}\Big)\big(1 - \mathcal{E}_{\rm LW}\big)
    \nonumber\\
    &\qquad + \Big(\mathcal{R}\, S_{\rm LW} - X_{\rm LW}\Big)\,
      dt\,\big(1 - \varphi_{\rm LW}\big),
  \label{eq:isrfd-transfer-absorbed} \\
  f_{\rm edge} &= \frac{\big(\lambda_E(\mathrm{LW}) - 1\big)\, H_{\rm dil}\, dt}
    {\mathcal{A}_{\rm LW}}
    \qquad \text{if } \mathcal{A}_{\rm LW} > 0,
  \label{eq:isrfd-transfer-fedge} \\
  \mathcal{T} &= f_{\rm edge}\, \mathcal{L}_{\rm LW},
  \label{eq:isrfd-transfer}
\end{align}

The result is added to PE by
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-u-transfer-add}), with no
$\varphi$ and no PE absorption.

\paragraph{What it conserves.} $\mathcal{L}_{\rm LW}$ is the total amount that
LW's relaxation depth removed this step, summed over all competing sinks in that
depth, i.e.\ dust absorption plus the band-edge loss. $f_{\rm edge}$ is exactly
the band-edge loss's share of $\mathcal{A}_{\rm LW}$, since
$\mathcal{A}_{\rm LW} = (c_{\rm hyp}\kappa + \lambda_E(\mathrm{LW})H_{\rm dil})dt$
and the band-edge part of it is
$(\lambda_E(\mathrm{LW}) - 1) H_{\rm dil} dt$. The transfer is therefore an
\textbf{exact proportional split} of one combined exponential sink, and LW's loss
at its $11.2$\,eV lower edge equals PE's gain at its $11.2$\,eV upper edge
exactly, because the two bands are contiguous there. $f_{\rm edge}$ is derived
from the
\emph{same} $\mathcal{A}_{\rm LW}$ the moment loop uses, not from a separately
written algebraically-equal expression, because under \code{-ffast-math} two such
expressions can disagree at extreme $\lambda$ and let the loss apply while the
gain silently does not. Grey LW,
$\lambda_E(\mathrm{LW}) = 1$, gives $\mathcal{T} = 0$ exactly.

\paragraph{Deliberate asymmetries and limits.}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The transfer is added raw to PE's already-relaxed $u$, so it is not
    subject to PE's own absorption during its arrival step.
  \item $\mathcal{T}$ is kept \textbf{signed}, not clamped at zero: clamping
    would break the exact $11.2$\,eV cancellation and the per-band ledger. A
    negative transfer (LW undershot) reduces PE's $u$ on the same step and can
    drive it negative, which then zeroes PE's flux on the next step's limiter.
  \item \code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} loses at its own lower edge, with
    $\lambda_N(\mathrm{LW})$, but has no photon-number PE moment to transfer
    into: that loss is uncompensated.
  \item The PE band's own $6$\,eV lower-edge loss is kept, not compensated, by
    design.

\subsection{Couplings out to the gas}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-couplings}

\subsubsection{Habing $G_0$, the sum of both bands}

\begin{align}
  u_\Sigma &= \big[u_{\rm PE}\big]_+ + \big[u_{\rm LW}\big]_+,
  \label{eq:isrfd-usum} \\
  \mathcal{J} &= c\, \rho_{\rm phys}\, u_\Sigma,
  \label{eq:isrfd-habing-flux} \\
  G_0 &= \left[\frac{\mathcal{J}\,\code{units\_cgs}(\text{energy flux per unit surface})}
    {1.6\times10^{-3}}\right]_+,
  \label{eq:isrfd-G0}
\end{align}

where $[\cdot]_+$ is the non-negativity clamp.

$G_0$ is the \textbf{sum of both bands}, because the Habing band is $6$ to
$13.6$\,eV, which is exactly PE plus LW.
\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} does not enter. The $c$ in
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-habing-flux}) is the \textbf{true} speed of light, not
$c_{\rm hyp}$, and $\rho$ is physical. Each band is clamped non-negative
\emph{before} the sum, so an undershot band reads as zero
illumination instead of cancelling part of the other band's real signal, and the
summed result passes the same clamp again. The clamp is read-only on the
particle: the stored $u$ keeps its negative value.

$G_0$ reaches Grackle's per-particle \code{isrf\_habing} only when
\code{chemistry\_data.use\_isrf\_field} is set, which
\code{with\_interstellar\_radiation\_field} forces on internally together with
\code{dust\_chemistry} and \code{photoelectric\_heating}. This coupling carries
\textbf{no} Grackle-mode gate: it gives 0 only if the ISRF is off.

\paragraph{Implementation.} \code{radiation\_get\_part\_isrf\_habing}
(\code{radiation\_gas.c:762-781}). Equations~(\ref{eq:isrfd-usum}),
(\ref{eq:isrfd-habing-flux}) and (\ref{eq:isrfd-G0}) are at

\subsubsection{H$_2$ Lyman-Werner photodissociation rate}

\begin{align}
  \mathcal{J}_{\rm LW} &= c\, \rho_{\rm phys}\, \big[u_{\rm LW}\big]_+,
  \label{eq:isrfd-lw-flux} \\
  k_{\rm diss} &= \left[\frac{\dfrac{\sigma_{\rm H_2}}{E_{\rm LW}}\,
    \mathcal{J}_{\rm LW}\,\code{units\_cgs}(\text{energy flux per unit surface})}
    {\code{units\_cgs}(\mathrm{INV\_TIME})}\right]_+,
  \label{eq:isrfd-kdiss}
\end{align}

with $\sigma_{\rm H_2}/E_{\rm LW}$ the single constant of
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-sigma-over-e}). The cross section and the photon energy
never appear separately, and \textbf{no} table value enters the rate: the table's
mean LW photon energy is a reported diagnostic only.
\code{ISRF\_MOMENT\_LW\_PHOTON} does not enter either.

\paragraph{The \texttt{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE > 1} gate.} The rate of
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-kdiss}) reaches Grackle only in a build with
\code{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE > 1}, and is $0$ otherwise. So at
\code{grackle\_0} and \code{grackle\_1} the rate is \textbf{identically zero},
with no start-up warning. The LW band is still transported and still written to
snapshots, and its \emph{energy} still feeds $G_0$ through
Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-usum}), so what is inert below mode 2 is only the band's
own dedicated dissociation effect.

Two further facts at the Grackle interface. The per-particle rate takes priority
over the global YAML scalar only when it is nonzero, so at mode 2 or above a
particle with
$k_{\rm diss} = 0$ (unilluminated, or clamped) receives the global
\code{GrackleCooling:RT\_H2\_dissociation\_rate\_cgs} instead of zero; setting
both is only a warning. And the per-particle rate
reaches Grackle \emph{unshielded} unless
\code{GrackleCooling:H2\_self\_shielding} selects a column-length mode; the code
warns that at $N_{\rm H_2} = 10^{20}\,{\rm cm^{-2}}$ the
Wolcott-Green \& Haiman (2019) factor is of order $10^{-5}$, so H$_2$ cannot survive
an illuminated cloud without it.

\subsection{Approximations and known limitations}
\label{sec:isrf-eqdisc-limits}

\subsubsection{Physical approximations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Reduced speed of light.} $c_{\rm hyp} \le c$ replaces $c$ in
    every transport and absorption rate, and the source is rescaled by
    $c_{\rm hyp}/c$. The steady state is
    preserved by the rescale, but every \emph{transient} is slowed by
    $c_{\rm hyp}/c$, including the pure-expansion transient at $\kappa = 0$,
    which decays at $(c_{\rm hyp}/c)H$ instead of $H$. Where the light-speed
    clamp is not
    active, $c_{\rm hyp}\, dt = C_{\rm hyp}\, h_{\rm phys}$ exactly, so the
    kernel light-crossing time is pinned to a fixed fraction of a timestep
    regardless of the true light travel time.
  \item \textbf{Grey within each band.} One dust cross section per band
    and one spectrum-averaged
    $\sigma_{\rm H_2}/E_{\rm LW}$, both compile-time
    constants rather than per-run parameters.
  \item \textbf{Run-wide scalar band-edge weights}, evaluated once over the
    whole IMF at one reference metallicity $Z = 0.014$. The code states that
    the bias of the
    shipped scalar against a population average is \emph{not sized}, and that
    an aged population's
    $\Lambda_{\rm LW}$ reaches about 18.3, roughly three times the shipped
    young-population value.
  \item $\mu_H = 1.4$ and $Z_\odot = 0.01295$ are calibration partners of the
    grain model, not the run's own composition.
  \item \textbf{Receiver-side extinction column with no resolved density
    gradient.} The column is the receiver's own local density times a path
    length, and every path mechanism
    assumes the intervening medium sits at the receiver's density (a model
    assumption, not checked by the code).
  \item The transported \code{LW\_PHOTON} moment is energy-equivalent at a fixed
    reference photon energy, so a spectrum's
    own $E_{\rm LW}$ cannot change the dissociation rate.
  \item \textbf{No $v/c$ terms anywhere.} $u$ and $\boldsymbol{F}$ are
    mass-specific quantities on Lagrangian particles, so the
    comoving-frame/lab-frame distinction is not carried. This is an observation
    from reading the equations, not a code comment.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Numerical and scheme limitations the code itself flags}

\begin{enumerate}
  \setcounter{enumi}{16}
  \item \label{it:isrfd-uncovered} \textbf{The uncovered pair class
    $\mathcal{H}_i \le r < \mathcal{H}_j$.}
    The force loop fires a side whenever either kernel reaches, and the shared
    $\Phi_{ij}$ of Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-div-phi}) carries a nonzero
    $W'_j$ into particle $i$ even where $W'_i = 0$; but the
    density loop never saw $j$, so $dt_j$ never entered $dt_{\max}(i)$. The
    overshoot factor is $dt_j/dt_{\max}(i)$, unbounded while $j$ stays
    inactive; once $j$ recomputes its own step the
    $\code{min\_ngb\_bin} + 2$ cap bounds it at 4. Closing the gap needs
    $dt_{\max}(i)$ built over the force loop's neighbour set.
  \item \textbf{Stability bound with hard-coded kernel constants}:
    \begin{equation}
      6.2\,\alpha\, C_{\rm hyp} + 0.70\, C_{\rm hyp}^2 \le 2,
      \label{eq:isrfd-stability}
    \end{equation}
    with $6.2 = 2 I_W$ and $0.70 = \nu_{\max}^2/2$ for Wendland-C2
    ($I_W = 3.10$, $\nu_{\max} = 1.18$). It is \emph{not} re-derived for any
    other kernel. At $\alpha = 0$ it gives the absolute ceiling
    $C_{\rm hyp} \le \sqrt{2/0.70} \approx 1.6903$; at the shipped
    $\alpha_{\rm floor} = 0.5$ it caps $C_{\rm hyp}$ at about $0.571$
    (solve the bound at $\alpha = 0.5$).
  \item The staggered order is forced: $\boldsymbol{F}$ is advanced first, then
    $u$ from the new flux. The order is forced by conservation, not by
    stability: only the force loop visits both sides of every pair, and it runs
    after the flux update.
  \item The negativity trigger is one step late by construction: it reads
    $u^n$, so an undershoot created by this step's update is corrected by the
    \emph{next} step, and cooling reads it uncorrected for one step through its
    own clamp.
  \item The dissipation, built from $u^n$, is decayed together with
    $u_{\rm prev}$ rather than added undamped, for the larger stability margin.
  \item $c_{\rm hyp}$ uses $a\, h$, not $\gamma_K h$, while the
    extinction path uses $\gamma_K h$.
  \item Energy transport conservation is exact per pair at the \emph{accumulator}
    level. In the applied update it holds between members of one time bin with
    equal depth, and across time bins up to the same-step and
    Hubble terms; see Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-pairwise}. The flux
    sum is not conserved: the gradient pair term is symmetric, the flux
    limiter removes flux, and no flux is booked across time bins
    (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-flux-conservation}). The cross-bin
    booking does not preserve positivity, and neither does the per-owner one.
    The metric counts the particle-snapshots whose lowest value at the end of
    an update, since the previous snapshot, is negative, summed over the PE
    and LW bands (debug builds only). On \code{ISRFMultiBinDissipation} (level 5, scheme 4, one
    thread), per-owner against cross-bin: 538 / 547 at $Z = 1$ and margin 0.5,
    58 / 64 at $Z = 0$ and margin 0.5, 99 / 101 at $Z = 1$ and margin 0.25,
    and 28 / 26 at $Z = 1$ and margin 0.125. Values negative at the snapshot
    instants, $Z = 1$ and margin 0.5: 54 / 67. On
    \code{ISRFDissipationExtreme} (scheme 4, 6 threads), one particle holds
    $-2.07$ at a snapshot against $-0.067$ with the per-owner booking, after its coarse step
    receives a drain larger than its $u$. The mechanism of the excess is not
    established. No limiter is applied, because a limiter would break the
    ledger.
  \item The $u$-path and $\boldsymbol{F}$-path relax at different precision; see
    Section~\ref{sec:isrf-eqdisc-F-update}.
  \item MPI and restart: operator and moment state, the pending amounts
    included, lives on \code{struct part}, so it rides the
    normal exchange and dump; the HII owner-computed payload does not. A
    pending amount is booked on its particle's own rank and never sent.
    The flux
    read order is safe across ranks:
    the gradient loop reads the old flux (before this cell's extra ghost), the
    force loop the new one, every force task runs after the extra ghosts of both
    cells it pairs, and a foreign particle is received after its own extra ghost.
  \item Not independently verified here, and reported as the code's own
    claims: the $6.2$ and $0.70$ lattice constants of
    Equation~(\ref{eq:isrfd-stability}). The derivation behind them is in
    the FUV chapter of this document (\code{02\_fuv\_isrf.tex}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Invariants each change must keep}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The $c_{\rm hyp}$-weighted energy.} Every force-loop pair
    term of $u$ is one shared coefficient times the receiver's own
    $c_{\rm hyp}$, so that
    $m_i X_i/c_{{\rm hyp},i} + m_j X_j/c_{{\rm hyp},j} = 0$
    (Eq.~(\ref{eq:isrfd-div-apply})). A new term on $u$ that does not have
    this form breaks the energy ledger. A term on $u$ added to the force
    loop must also be routed through \code{radiation\_cross\_bin\_pair\_band}
    for pairs of unequal steps, or it leaks across time bins.
  \item \textbf{Antisymmetry and pair coverage.} The divergence and the
    dissipation source are applied on both sides of every pair whenever
    either kernel reaches, which only the force loop guarantees. A term
    moved to the density or gradient loop loses the pairs with
    $\mathcal{H}_i \le r < \mathcal{H}_j$. The gradient is deliberately not
    antisymmetric (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}); making it so trades
    consistency in a uniform field for conservation of the flux sum.
  \item \textbf{Positivity.} The transport does not keep $u \ge 0$. The
    dissipation trigger and floor reduce negative values, the flux limiter
    keeps $|\tilde{\boldsymbol F}| \le u$, and the Grackle clamp hides any
    remaining negative value from the chemistry while the snapshot keeps
    it. The cross-bin booking does not preserve positivity, and a limiter
    on it would break the ledger.
  \item \textbf{The time-bin pending booking.} A pending amount is formed
    at unit speed, with the finer member's step and $\varphi$, and is
    added by the coarser member at its own next update, after which both
    amounts are zero. In a debugging build, a particle that adds pending
    amounts without a marked active step stops the run. The amounts live
    on the particle, so a change of their
    layout changes the restart layout marker.
  \item \textbf{MPI foreign particles.} A rank writes only its own
    particles. A pending amount is booked by the rank that owns the
    coarser member and is never sent. A foreign particle is received after
    its own extra ghost, so the force loop reads its new flux and the
    gradient loop its old one. Without
    \code{MPI\_SYMMETRIC\_FORCE\_INTERACTION} the booking is switched off
    on several ranks.
  \item \textbf{Float range and NaN prevention.} The closure, the limiter
    and the floor gate form the flux norm and its square in double. The
    energy depth and its $\varphi$ are in double. A ratio whose
    denominator can reach zero returns a documented value on
    \code{den <= 0} before it divides.
    Under \code{-ffast-math} a NaN cannot be caught by \code{isnan} or
    absorbed by \code{min} and \code{max}, so it must not be produced.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Known blind spots}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The production regime, many stars at once with a nearly isotropic
    field, is not validated directly, and the energy change that a spread
    of $c_{\rm hyp}$ makes in production is not measured.
  \item The flux sum is not conserved
    (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-flux-conservation}).
  \item A uniform flux seeded in a dust-free expanding box grows, and the
    cause is not located (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-limits}).
  \item A varying speed under expansion at a strong density contrast has
    never run.
  \item The multi-bin box at a bin gap of $5$ stops with non-finite
    positions near step 770, also on one rank; the cause is not located.
  \item The flux Hubble term check needs a flux in the initial conditions,
    which the shipped initial-condition reader does not read.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Review checklist}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item For every new float expression that squares or multiplies
    quantities in internal units, check its range in float32 under
    \code{-ffast-math}, and build the boundary configuration rather than
    trusting the algebra.
  \item Prevent a NaN at its source; do not rely on a later clamp to
    absorb it. A check of a computed value must test finiteness by the bit
    pattern before it compares, because a NaN compares false against every
    bar.
  \item Derive each bar from the mechanism or the float model, never from
    the runs at hand. Name the regime the bar holds in.
  \item Show each check red, then green: construct the input that must
    fail (a pending amount dropped, a band weight off by one, a build
    without the extra visits), and record both outcomes.
  \item For a change to a force-loop term of $u$, run the energy ledger
    on several time bins and, for an MPI path, on several ranks.
  \item For a change to the flux, report the net and the gross flux beside
    $L$, and compare on a physical measure, not on the factor over the
    exactness bound.
\end{enumerate}
