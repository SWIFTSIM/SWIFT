\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Ionizing photon rate: blackbody spectrum fit}
\label{sec:blackbody}

Every active star's ionizing photon rate $\dot N_{\rm ion}$ (the budget
spent in Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget} below) comes from
\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_ionizing\_photon\_emission\_
rate\_fit} (\code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation.c}), used both directly
for individual (non-SSP) star particles and to build the IMF-integrated
lookup table population stars read from. Unlike a simple empirical
$\dot N_{\rm ion}(M)$ fit, this derives $\dot N_{\rm ion}$ from the
star's own blackbody spectrum, via a mass $\to$ radius $\to$
luminosity $\to$ effective temperature chain:
\begin{align}
  R(M) &= R_\odot \times
  \begin{cases}
    (M/M_\odot)^{0.8} & M < 1\,M_\odot \\
    (M/M_\odot)^{0.57} & 1 \le M/M_\odot < 8 \\
    (M/M_\odot)^{0.5} & M/M_\odot \ge 8
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:mass-radius} \\
  L(M) &= L_\odot \times
  \begin{cases}
    0.185\,(M/M_\odot)^{2} & M/M_\odot < 0.43 \\
    (M/M_\odot)^{4} & 0.43 \le M/M_\odot < 2 \\
    1.5\,(M/M_\odot)^{3.5} & 2 \le M/M_\odot < 54 \\
    32000\,(M/M_\odot) & M/M_\odot \ge 54
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:mass-luminosity} \\
  T_{\rm eff} &= 5780\,{\rm K} \times
  \left(\frac{L/L_\odot}{(R/R_\odot)^2}\right)^{1/4},
  \label{eq:stefan-boltzmann}
\end{align}
i.e.\ a piecewise power-law mass--radius and mass--luminosity relation
(\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_radius},
\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_luminosity}), then $T_{\rm eff}$
from the Stefan--Boltzmann law applied to $L$ and $R$ directly (not a
separate mass--temperature fit: see the note at the end of this
subsection). $\dot N_{\rm ion}$ is then the photon-number flux of a
Planck spectrum of temperature $T_{\rm eff}$ above the H-ionizing edge
$h\nu_0=13.6\,$eV:
\begin{equation}
  \dot N_{\rm ion} = \frac{L}{k_B T_{\rm eff}} \times
  \frac{15}{\pi^4} \int_{x_0}^{\infty} \frac{x^2}{e^x-1}\,dx, \qquad
  x_0 = \frac{h\nu_0}{k_B T_{\rm eff}},
  \label{eq:planck-photon-rate}
\end{equation}
the standard Planck photon-number integral normalized so the
$15/\pi^4$ prefactor makes the \emph{bolometric} photon rate exact in
the $x_0\to0$ limit. The incomplete integral in
Eq.~\ref{eq:planck-photon-rate} has no closed form, so the code
evaluates it via the standard series expansion
\begin{equation}
  \int_{x_0}^\infty \frac{x^2}{e^x-1}\,dx =
  \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{e^{-nx_0}}{n}
  \left(x_0^2 + \frac{2x_0}{n} + \frac{2}{n^2}\right),
  \label{eq:planck-series}
\end{equation}
truncated at $n=5$ terms (or earlier once $e^{-nx_0}<10^{-10}$),
sufficient given $x_0 \gtrsim 3$ for any star hot enough to ionize
hydrogen at all, so the series converges geometrically fast. If
$x_0 > 45$ (i.e.\ $e^{-x_0}<10^{-20}$, negligible ionizing output, as
happens for any star with $T_{\rm eff}\lesssim3500\,$K), $\dot N_{\rm
ion}=0$ is returned directly rather than evaluating the series.

\textbf{Note: two independent mass--temperature relations coexist,
confirmed to disagree (investigated 2026-07-20) (TODO: Remove it once in pychem).}
\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_temperature} is a separate,
piecewise mass--temperature fit ($3500\,{\rm K}\,(M/M_\odot)^{0.5}$
below $1\,M_\odot$, $5800\,{\rm K}\,(M/M_\odot)^{0.5}$ up to
$8\,M_\odot$, $25000\,{\rm K}\,(M/M_\odot/20)^{0.1}$ above), \emph{not}
used by Eq.~\ref{eq:stefan-boltzmann} above, which derives $T_{\rm
eff}$ from $L$ and $R$ via Stefan--Boltzmann instead. A grep confirms
\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_temperature} has zero call
sites anywhere in \code{src/}: genuinely dead code, not merely
redundant. Evaluated both relations (plus the $R(M)$/$L(M)$ fits the
Stefan--Boltzmann path depends on) across $0.1$--$150\,M_\odot$: they
agree only near the two piecewise-boundary anchors ($\approx1\,M_\odot$,
$\approx8\,M_\odot$, almost certainly tuned to known reference points
like the Sun's $T_{\rm eff}$) and diverge by a factor
$\sim1.5$--$2.7\times$ everywhere else, worst in the OB-star range that
dominates ionizing photon production. The Stefan--Boltzmann path (the
one actually used) also saturates at a fixed $\approx77{,}300\,$K for
every mass above $\approx54\,M_\odot$: a structural artifact of the
mass--luminosity fit turning linear ($L\propto M$) at exactly the mass
the mass--radius fit is already $R\propto M^{0.5}$, making $L/R^2$ (and
hence $T_{\rm eff}$) mass-independent there by construction, not
physical intent. \textbf{Not worth reconciling in place}: both fits are
single-metallicity piecewise power laws with no $(M,Z)$ dependence at
all, which is the actual limitation regardless of which temperature
relation is used. This entire empirical-fit layer (radius, luminosity,
temperature) is intended to be replaced by extending \texttt{pychem}
(\texttt{\textasciitilde/programs/pychem/}, already the source of this
project's GEAR chemical-yield tables) to emit proper
$(M,Z)$-dependent stellar tables: see the open items (item~T7 in the
GEAR radiation logbook's Open Items section) for that standalone
follow-up project. When it
lands, the unused \code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_temperature}
should simply be deleted.

\subsection{Photon budget and recombination cost}
\label{sec:photon-budget}

The whole algorithm (i.e. one-shot, distance-sorted photon-budget spending
on the nearest candidate gas particles, closest-first, until exhausted)
is the FIRE-2 subgrid photoionization scheme
\citep[Appendix~E]{Hopkins2018}; Hu et al.\ and Smith et al.\ (and this
branch) build directly on it. Each active star with ionizing photon
rate $\dot N_{\rm ion}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:planck-photon-rate},
Section~\ref{sec:blackbody}) gathers candidate gas particles within a
search radius, sorted by distance, and spends its budget on them
closest-first. For a candidate particle $b$ of mass $m_b$, hydrogen
mass fraction $X_H$, and physical density $\rho_b$, the number of
hydrogen atoms and the recombination rate needed to keep it ionized
are
\begin{align}
  N_{H,b} &= \frac{X_H\, m_b}{m_p}, \\
  n_{e,b} &= \frac{X_H\, \rho_b}{m_p}, \\
  \Delta \dot N_b &= N_{H,b}\, \beta\, n_{e,b},
  \label{eq:recomb-cost}
\end{align}
where $m_p$ is the proton mass and $\beta$ is the case-B recombination
coefficient, a \emph{fixed} constant evaluated at the conventional
$T=10^4\,$K (\code{phys\_const->const\_caseb\_recomb}), matching the
convention both reference papers use. Equation~\ref{eq:recomb-cost} is
identical in form to Hu et al.'s $N_H\beta n_H$ (their $N_H$ is called
``the number of hydrogen nuclei'', an unambiguous atom count) and to
Smith et al.'s $R_{\rm rec}=\beta m \rho (X_H/m_p)^2$. 

\textbf{Claim cost vs.\ maintenance rate.} Equation~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}
is a \emph{rate}: what it costs, per unit time, to keep a particle's
recombinations replaced once it is ionized. The one-off cost actually
charged to \emph{claim} a fresh candidate (Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting})
integrates that rate over the interval since the star's previous pass
and adds a one-off stripping cost,
\begin{equation}
  {\rm cost}_b = N_{HI,b} + \Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta \dot N_b,
  \label{eq:claim-cost}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_{\rm back}$ is the elapsed interval this pass covers
and $N_{HI,b}$ is the particle's \emph{neutral}-hydrogen atom count
(\code{radiation\_get\_part\_number\_neutral\_hydrogen\_atoms}), not
the total $N_{H,b}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}. At
\code{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE}$\,\ge 1$, where species fractions are
tracked, a candidate that is already partly or fully ionized: gas
re-tagged after its previous tag lapsed on a marginal budget shortfall,
or pre-ionized by an external UV background: does not need to pay
again to strip electrons it has already lost; at mode~0, with no
species tracked, $N_{HI,b}$ falls back to the conservative $N_{H,b}$.
The maintenance charge levied on gas the star already holds ionized
(Section~\ref{sec:persistence}) uses only the rate term,
$\Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta \dot N_b$: those electrons are already
stripped, so there is no neutral-hydrogen term to pay again. The
$\Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta \dot N_b$ reserve in
Eq.~\ref{eq:claim-cost} is also what makes newly-claimed region growth
implicit rather than explicit: the growth equation
$dS/dt=(Q-S)/t_{\rm rec}$ integrates, with this reserve included, as
$\Delta S = (Q-S)\,\Delta t_{\rm back}/(t_{\rm rec}+\Delta t_{\rm back})$,
unconditionally stable for any $\Delta t_{\rm back}$; dropping the
reserve would give explicit Euler instead, which overshoots and churns
whenever $\Delta t_{\rm back} \gg t_{\rm rec}$.

\textbf{Known approximation: $n_e \approx n_H$.} Equation~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}
(and every other electron-density calculation in this model, including
the adaptive rebuild cadence's recombination timescale,
Section~\ref{sec:adaptive-cadence}) assumes one free electron
per hydrogen nucleus: it does not track helium's ionization state at all.
For a standard cosmic He mass fraction ($\sim$24--28\%, $\sim$8--10\%
by number relative to H), singly-ionized He alone (needs $>24.6\,$eV,
present in any O-star spectrum energetic enough to ionize H at all)
underestimates $n_e$ by $\sim$8--10\% if ignored; doubly-ionized He
($>54.4\,$eV, only the hottest O stars) up to $\sim$16--20\%. This is a
structural simplification of the whole model (this branch does not add
or remove it), not something any single feature here fixes: any
future recombination-timescale or electron-density calculation added
to this model inherits the same approximation.

\subsection{Temperature dependence of $\beta$}
\label{sec:beta-temperature}

\citet{HuiGnedin1997}, Appendix A, give a fit to the case-B H\,{\sc
ii} recombination coefficient (from \citealt{Ferland1992}'s data,
accurate to 0.7\% from 1\,K to $10^9\,$K: effectively exact for our
purposes):
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_B(T) = 2.753\times10^{-14}\,
  \frac{\lambda^{1.500}}{\left[1+(\lambda/2.740)^{0.407}\right]^{2.242}}
  \ {\rm cm^3\,s^{-1}}, \qquad
  \lambda = \frac{315614\,{\rm K}}{T}.
  \label{eq:alphaB-huignedin}
\end{equation}
This was already implemented correctly elsewhere in \swift:
identically in \code{src/rt/SPHM1RT/rt\_cooling\_rates.h}, and inside
grackle's own \code{k2\_rate()} (which GEAR-RT delegates to for its own
case-B recombination, via the \code{CaseBRecombination} flag: GEAR-RT
has no case-B formula of its own), and \textbf{is now also
implemented here (2026-07-16)}: $\beta$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost} is
Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin} evaluated at $T$, not a fixed constant.
$T$ is the particle's own applied ionized temperature: the same
$u_{\rm new}$ from Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}, converted to a temperature via
its own $\mu$, so the recombination cost charged against a star's
budget is always consistent with the temperature that particle is
actually being held at
(\code{radiation\_get\_part\_ionized\_internal\_energy}, shared between
\code{radiation\_get\_part\_rate\_to\_fully\_ionize} and
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid} to guarantee the two never
disagree; \code{radiation\_get\_case\_b\_recombination\_\allowbreak
coefficient\_cgs} implements Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin} itself).

For reference, evaluating Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin} across the
range this model's own temperature floor
(Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}--\ref{eq:tcollisional}) applies
($\approx$8,600\,K at $Z=Z_\odot$ up to $\approx$47,500\,K at $Z=0$)
against the value $\beta$ used to take everywhere before this fix
($2.6\times10^{-13}\,{\rm cm^3\,s^{-1}}$, $\alpha_B(10^4\,{\rm K})$ to
+0.3\%):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrr}
  \hline
  $T$ [K] & $\alpha_B(T)$ [cm$^3$ s$^{-1}$] & vs.\ the old fixed value \\
  \hline
  8,600 ($Z=Z_\odot$)  & $2.94\times10^{-13}$ & $+13\%$ \\
  10,000 (old fixed value's own $T$) & $2.59\times10^{-13}$ & $-0.3\%$ \\
  20,000               & $1.43\times10^{-13}$ & $-45\%$ \\
  47,500 ($Z=0$)        & $6.42\times10^{-14}$ & $-75\%$ \\
  \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Since $\alpha_B(T)$ falls with rising $T$, the old fixed value used to
\emph{overestimate} the recombination cost at low metallicity by a
factor of $\sim$4 at this example's $Z=0$ default: the star's budget
was spent roughly 4$\times$ faster than physically necessary, which
artificially shrank the achieved $r_{\rm HII}$ in exactly the direction
that could explain part of the residual gap to
\citet{Hu2017}/\citet{Smith2021}'s reference numbers (the GEAR
radiation logbook's Metallicity and the ionized-gas temperature floor
section). At $Z=Z_\odot$ the effect was smaller
and opposite in sign ($+13\%$, i.e.\ a mild \emph{under}estimate). See
the GEAR radiation logbook's Validation section for the measured
effect of this fix on the validation matrix.

A particle is a candidate for ionization only if it is not already
tagged, is denser than \code{HII\_region\_min\_density\_Hpcm3}, and
is not already above $\approx 10^4\,$K
(\code{feedback\_part\_can\_be\_ionized}). Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}
covers how a star spends its budget on the resulting candidates,
including what happens once a pixel can no longer afford the next one.

\subsection{Per-cone photon budget (angular splitting)}
\label{sec:angular-splitting}

To avoid a dense clump near a source absorbing a disproportionate
share of the photon budget regardless of the small solid angle it
subtends (``mass-biasing''), \code{HII\_angular\_nside} splits the
budget into $N_{\rm pix} = 12\,{\rm nside}^2$ equal shares
($\dot N_{\rm ion}/N_{\rm pix}$ each) and assigns each candidate
particle to exactly one pixel (cone) by its direction from the star,
hard assignment (no fractional overlap, matching both papers' own
choice). \code{nside=0} degenerates to $N_{\rm pix}=1$, i.e.\ today's
plain spherical behaviour. \code{nside} can be any non-negative
integer: the HEALPix RING scheme, unlike NEST, has no power-of-2
restriction; but the practical ceiling is memory, not geometry: each
star carries a fixed-size per-pixel array sized at configure time by
\code{--with-number-of-hii-angular-pixels} (default 12, i.e.\
$\text{nside}\le1$); requesting more pixels than the build supports
errors clearly at start-up rather than overflowing the array.

\textbf{Opening a pass's budget: what carries over.} At the start of
each rebuild pass, every pixel's photon count is (re)opened from
\begin{equation}
  N_{{\rm ion},p}^{\rm open} =
  \min\!\left(N_{{\rm ion},p}^{\rm prev},\,0\right) +
  \dot N_{{\rm ion},p}\,\Delta t_{\rm back},
  \label{eq:budget-open}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_{\rm back}$ is the time elapsed since this star's
previous pass and $N_{{\rm ion},p}^{\rm prev}$ is whatever the pixel's
count stood at when that pass ended. Unspent \emph{positive} budget is
not carried forward: those photons reached no gas and escaped;
but a \emph{negative} balance is: a pass can overdraw a pixel by up to
one particle's cost (the boundary-particle rule below), and that debt
is repaid out of the next pass's income before any new spending
happens, rather than forgiven. Forgiving it every pass instead would
over-issue photons in proportion to how many passes elapse per unit
time, i.e.\ as $1/\Delta t_{\rm back}$: a cadence-dependent bias,
which this model is built to avoid.

\textbf{Sequential spend within a pass.} A pass gathers its candidates
into a single buffer sorted by distance from the star, each entry
tagged with the pixel its direction falls in. Scanning that buffer in
order and skipping (not stopping at) entries whose pixel is already
exhausted visits every pixel's own candidates in ascending-distance
order: a sorted sequence's subsequence is still sorted, so each
cone spends closest-first exactly like the spherical ($N_{\rm
pix}=1$) case, just interleaved with every other cone's spending in
the same traversal.

\textbf{The boundary particle.} A pixel's boundary particle for the
pass is the first candidate, in that pixel's own distance order, whose
claim cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:claim-cost}) it can no longer afford outright:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{HII\_deterministic\_boundary\_ionization=1}: ionize it
    anyway, overdrawing the pixel (bounded by Eq.~\ref{eq:budget-open}'s
    debt carry to at most this one particle's cost);
  \item \code{HII\_deterministic\_boundary\_ionization=0} (the
    default): draw a single random number, keyed on the star id, the
    pixel, and the integer time at the start of the step
    (\code{random\_number\_HII\_regions}): \emph{not} on the
    candidate particle, and ionize the boundary particle iff the
    draw is at most the affordable fraction
    $\text{budget}_p/{\rm cost}_b$.
\end{itemize}
Because the draw is fixed for the whole pixel this pass, at most one
particle per pixel can win it: a win drives the pixel's budget
negative (never enough to also afford the next, farther candidate),
so every later comparison in the same pixel this pass evaluates a
non-positive affordable fraction and always loses. This is what gives
expectation-level photon conservation,
$\mathbb{E}[\text{spent}] = P_{\rm win}\cdot{\rm cost}_b +
(1-P_{\rm win})\cdot 0 = \text{budget}_p$, and specifically requires
the single fixed draw: rolling independently per candidate in a loop
that stops at the first win would instead claim a boundary particle
almost surely, spending its full cost rather than the pixel's actual
remaining budget. The probabilistic default is the better choice in
practice too: deterministic mode over-ionizes, mildly for the
spherical scheme and severely for the angular one (each pixel's own
small budget makes its one guaranteed over-spend a proportionally
larger error), with no compensating benefit.

\subsection{Temperature floor}

Once a particle is tagged, its internal energy is set (and reset every
step while tagged, in the cooling task, via
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid}) to
\begin{equation}
  u_{\rm new} = \min\left(\Delta u_{\rm ionized},\ u_{\rm collisional}\right),
  \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta u_{\rm ionized} = N_H E_{\rm ion}/m_b$ is the specific
energy needed to fully ionize the particle ($E_{\rm ion}=13.6\,$eV per
H atom, metallicity-independent), and $u_{\rm collisional}$ comes from
a metallicity-dependent equilibrium temperature. Unlike the rest of
Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional} is not from
FIRE-2: FIRE-2 clamps ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K, but from
its successor, \citet[][the metallicity-dependent photoionization
temperature fit]{Hopkins2023}:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\rm collisional} = 10^4\,{\rm K} \times
  \begin{cases}
    \min\left(6.62,\ \dfrac{0.86}{1+0.22\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)}\right) & Z \ge 10^{-3}Z_\odot \\[2mm]
    6.62 & Z < 10^{-3}Z_\odot.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:tcollisional}
\end{equation}
Neither Hu et al.\ nor Smith et al.\ have a metallicity-dependent floor
at all: both simply clamp ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K. See the
GEAR radiation logbook's Metallicity and the ionized-gas temperature
floor section for the numeric consequence of
Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}--\ref{eq:tcollisional} at $Z=0$.

\textbf{Applying the floor: energy accounting.} $u_{\rm new}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}) is never written to the particle directly.
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid} (\code{cooling\_gear\_subgrid.h})
returns it to its caller, \code{cooling\_new\_energy}, which returns
immediately on a tagged particle without ever calling Grackle's own
chemistry/cooling solve (\code{local\_solve\_chemistry}): the
untagged path always calls it. Skipping that call is what makes the
floor hold for the step: Grackle's ODE integrator has no notion of the
external photoionizing forcing this model represents and would
otherwise relax the just-floored energy back toward its own
equilibrium within the same step it was applied. $u_{\rm new}$ then
re-enters \code{cooling\_cool\_part} exactly like any other cooling
function's return value: floored again at
\code{hydro\_props->minimal\_internal\_energy} if needed, and is
turned into a rate,
\begin{equation}
  \left.\D{u}{t}\right|_{\rm cool} =
  \frac{u_{\rm new} - u_{\rm ad,\,before}}{dt_{\rm therm}},
  \label{eq:cool-dudt}
\end{equation}
which is added to hydro's own $du/dt$ and handed to the kick: the
ionization floor is applied exactly once per step, through the same
integration path as ordinary cooling, subject to the same floors. It
is also subject to the same suppression as ordinary cooling when
\code{cooling->thermal\_time} delayed-cooling turn-off is active for
this particle (\code{time - time\_last\_event < thermal\_time}): while
that window is open, \code{cooling\_new\_energy}: and with it
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid}: is not called at all, so a
tagged particle is not re-floored until the window closes.

The bookkeeping this produces is exact, not merely consistent:
\code{cooling\_cool\_part} records, every step,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta E_{\rm radiated} = -m_b\left(u_{\rm new} - u_{\rm ad,\,before}\right)
  = m_b\left(u_{\rm ad,\,before} - u_{\rm new}\right)
\end{equation}
into \code{xp->cooling\_data.radiated\_energy}, from the identical
$u_{\rm new}$ Eq.~\ref{eq:cool-dudt} used. There is no separate
accounting path for the ionization floor: the recorded
radiated energy (negative, i.e.\ recorded as heating, whenever the
floor raises the particle's energy) always equals the energy
correction actually applied to the particle that step.

\textbf{Bug found and fixed (2026-07-18):} Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional} is
$\log_{10}$, but \code{radiation.c} computed it with C's \code{log()}
(natural log) until this fix. This silently applied the wrong base
everywhere $Z \ge 10^{-3}Z_\odot$, including at this project's own
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ reference point (the GEAR radiation logbook's
Metallicity and the ionized-gas temperature floor section),
chosen specifically because $\log_{10}$ makes
$T_{\rm collisional}=10^4\,$K there. With the natural-log bug, the code
actually applied $T_{\rm collisional}\approx12{,}700\,$K at that same
point: not the intended, papers-matching $10^4\,$K, which also
shifted the case-B $\beta$ (Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature}, evaluated
at this same temperature whenever $u_{\rm collisional} < \Delta
u_{\rm ionized}$). Every deprecated result in the GEAR radiation
logbook's deprecated-results section computed at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ (the pre-restart clump matrices and the e10
check) was therefore run at the wrong reference temperature;
see the GEAR radiation logbook's ``$T_{\rm collisional}$ used natural
log'' bug entry (2026-07-18) for the fix and its measured effect.

\subsection{Persistence and maintenance}
\label{sec:persistence}

A tagged particle stays flagged (and re-floored, per
Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}, every step) until
\texttt{end\_time = tag\_time\allowbreak{} + HII\_region\_rebuild\_time},
at which
point it is unflagged and becomes eligible to compete for budget again
on the star's next rebuild pass. Each star's rebuild is scheduled
independently off its own age and its own last-rebuild time
(\code{feedback\_common.c}); there is no cross-star coordination or
deliberate synchronous/asynchronous toggle like the one
\citet{Smith2021} implemented (they found no difference between the
two). For the co-located, simultaneously-born stars in the validation
example this produces synchronous rebuilds in practice, simply because
the stars are identical, not because the code enforces it.

\textbf{Maintenance: renewing gas already held ionized.} Before a pass
spends anything on new candidates, it first renews the gas the star
already holds. \code{runner\_dosub\_stars\_hii\_ionization\_feedback}
gathers every particle currently tagged with this star's own id,
via a traversal exempt from the ordinary candidate filter
(\code{feedback\_part\_can\_be\_ionized}), since held gas is neither
cold nor untagged: into a scratch buffer, then charges each one's
maintenance rate (Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget}) via
\code{feedback\_iact\_HII\_maintain\_ionized\_part}, which also renews
its \texttt{end\_time} so the tag survives to the following pass. Every
rebuild after the one that first claimed a particle therefore
re-charges its ongoing recombination cost, rather than charging it
once at claim time and never again.

\textbf{Maintenance order: a receding front recedes from the outside
in.} The scratch buffer is charged in ascending $(r^2,{\rm id})$
order: current squared distance from the star, with particle id as
the tiebreak for particles at coincident $r^2$: \emph{not} the order
cell traversal happens to visit them in. This order is what determines
which particles a budget shortfall lapses: charging nearest-first
means an exhausted pixel simply stops renewing its most distant held
particles first, which then reach their already-recorded
\texttt{end\_time} unrenewed and expire on their own next cooling
step. A receding region therefore shrinks from its outer shell inward,
rather than developing a hole wherever the traversal order happened to
run out of budget. Each held particle is re-priced against the same
pixel it was assigned to when claimed or last renewed
(\code{feedback\_iact\_HII\_maintain\_ionized\_part} passes the stored
pixel index straight through to
\code{radiation\_consume\_ionizing\_photons}), so upkeep tracks
ownership, not a particle's current direction from the star even if it
has since drifted into another cone.

The star's extent measure, $h_{\rm hii}$, is updated from this same
pass: it is set to the largest $r$ among particles the star still
actually holds after maintenance (tracked for every held particle
regardless of whether its renewal was affordable this pass, so a
shrinking region promptly shrinks $h_{\rm hii}$, and with it the
search radius and locking footprint, for a star doing correspondingly
less work), not the historical high-water mark of everything the star
has ever claimed: see Section~\ref{sec:front-radius} for the
distinct, historical-extent measure used to read out this model's
region from the outside.

Budget carryover between passes: unspent positive budget discarded,
negative debt carried forward and repaid first, is
Eq.~\ref{eq:budget-open} (Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}); the
same rule governs every pixel a star's maintenance and new-candidate
spending draw from.

\subsection{Front-radius measure}
\label{sec:front-radius}

Two on-particle quantities give two different readings of how far a
star's ionizing influence currently reaches, and they can diverge
whenever a region recedes (Section~\ref{sec:persistence}).

\textbf{Instantaneous front: \code{h\_hii} (\code{HIIRegionRadii}).}
The star's own $h_{\rm hii}$, updated every maintenance pass
(Section~\ref{sec:persistence}), is the distance out to which the
star's budget \emph{currently} keeps gas ionized. It shrinks as soon
as a shortfall stops renewing the outer shell, tracking the region's
present state exactly.

\textbf{Historical extent: the ownership stamp.} A gas particle's
ionizing-star id (\code{radiation\_tag\_part\_as\_ionized}'s
\code{star\_id}, exposed to snapshots as \texttt{HIIStarIDs}) is a
one-way stamp:
\code{radiation\_reset\_part\_ionized\_tag} clears the active
\emph{tag} once a particle's \texttt{end\_time} lapses, but never
clears which star last owned it. A particle the star ionized and later
stopped renewing therefore still carries that star's id and keeps
advecting with the flow, marking where the swept shell has been even
after the active front has receded past it. The largest current
distance among a star's ever-owned gas is a historical-extent measure,
distinct from, and always at least as large as, $h_{\rm hii}$.

For the default flag-and-floor scheme, the historical-extent measure
is the more useful proxy for ``how far did this star's ionization
front get'': the swept shell keeps its ownership stamp after tag
expiry, so it directly reads the front's high-water mark. For a
rate-coupled configuration (\code{HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate}),
where the tag instead drives Grackle's own non-equilibrium ionization
state, $h_{\rm hii}$ is the measure that coincides with the actual
$x_{\rm HII}$ front; the historical-extent measure would instead
freeze at the historical maximum once the region starts to recede.

\textbf{Robust historical extent: gap-aware outlier rejection.} A
single particle whose tag lapsed early and then advected outward for
several dynamical times can dominate a plain maximum over ever-owned
distances and inflate the reported extent by double digits percent,
without any genuine coverage problem. The validation checks
(\code{examples/SubgridTests/SubgridRadiation/\{StromgrenSphere,\allowbreak
StromgrenSphereHuSmith2017,\allowbreak StromgrenSphereStarbench\}/
*\_analytic\_check.py}, function \code{robust\_ever\_tagged\_radius})
read the historical extent robustly instead of taking this maximum
directly: sort ever-owned distances descending, estimate a local point
spacing from the median gap among the outermost few percent of
points, then walk inward from the largest distance and discard a
point only while the gap to its next-lower neighbour exceeds several
times that local spacing: an isolated advected straggler produces a
gap an order of magnitude or more above the front's own natural
surface roughness, so this stops at the first point that is not such
an outlier. The true (unrejected) maximum is still reported alongside
it as a secondary diagnostic.

This robustness is also what makes the search-radius expansion
artifact of Section~\ref{sec:runtime} (in the task-graph document)
harmless to the measurement: an isolated particle tagged by an
expansion probe beyond the coherent front is exactly the
gap-separated case this rejection removes.

\subsection{Adaptive rebuild cadence (opt-in 2026-07-18, removed Phase 4c
2026-07-28)}
\label{sec:adaptive-cadence}

\textbf{Removed (Phase 4c, 2026-07-28).} The count-budget redesign (the
GEAR radiation logbook's Hu et al./Smith et al.\ 4-star validation
section) made
rebuild cadence a purely numerical parameter: a six-cadence sweep found
the converged answer independent of it, so this mode's correctness
motivation was gone, leaving only its per-star timestep-pinning cost.
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence} and
\code{HII\_rebuild\_safety\_factor} no longer exist;
\code{HII\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr} is kept (it floors the photon-budget
interval in fixed-cadence mode too). The description below is kept as
a historical record of the mechanism that was removed.

The fixed \code{HII\_region\_rebuild\_time\_Myr} above is a single,
problem-specific value with no principled way to choose it: this
project has used both $0.01$ and $0.5\,$Myr across different examples.
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence=1} replaces it with a
physically-derived, per-star cadence,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta t_{\rm rebuild} = f_{\rm safety}\times
  \min(t_{\rm rec,\,pixel\text{-}min},\ t_{\rm cross}),
\end{equation}
where $t_{\rm rec}=1/(\alpha_B(T_i)\,n_H)$ (recombination timescale,
computed \emph{per active angular pixel} and combined via a minimum,
since a dense clump-facing pixel and a diffuse one are physically
different environments: diluting via a star-wide mean would
reintroduce a milder version of the mass-biasing problem angular
splitting exists to solve), $t_{\rm cross}=R_{\rm hii}/c_s(T_i)$
(sound-crossing timescale, star-wide, since $h_{\rm hii}$ is not
tracked per-pixel), and $f_{\rm safety}$ (default $0.2$) is a
dimensionless safety factor, the direct analogue of a CFL number. The
density sample for $t_{\rm rec}$ is drawn only from not-yet-tagged
front candidates already gathered by the ordinary search pass (not from
gas already tagged by this star in an earlier rebuild): simpler, no
changes needed to \code{feedback\_part\_can\_be\_ionized}/the gather
functions, and arguably the physically right population for
recombination risk anyway (front gas is the marginal population, not
the already-robustly-ionized interior). A backstop floor,
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_region\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr} (default
$10^{-4}\,$Myr), prevents a pathologically short computed interval;
this is a distinct quantity from \code{Stars:min\_star\_timestep\_Myr}
(the GEAR radiation logbook's ``star/HII timestep floor'' bug entry,
2026-07-20), the unrelated numerical
step-size safety floor.

Implementation: \code{radiation\_get\_T\_collisional\_K()} (extracted
helper) and
\code{radiation\_compute\_and\_cache\_HII\_rebuild\_interval()} in
\code{radiation.c}, dispatched through a thin wrapper in
\code{feedback\_common.\{h,c\}} so \code{runner\_radiation\_feedback.c}
: generic cell-traversal code: never includes GEAR-internal
\code{radiation.h} directly. The non-adaptive code path is untouched
(only new \code{if}/\code{else if} branches added ahead of it), so
every already-validated result stays reproducible with the feature off
(the default).

\textbf{Verification.} A genuine \code{dt=0} deadlock was found and
fixed during verification: the cached rebuild deadline is only
refreshed by an actual rebuild pass, and if that gate stays false for a
reason unrelated to timing (photon budget exhausted, aged past
\code{HII\_region\_max\_age}) the deadline goes stale and the timestep
constraint returned exactly $0.0$ forever: fixed by flooring the
fallback instead of returning a literal $0.0$ (the GEAR radiation
logbook's ``star/HII timestep floor'' bug entry, 2026-07-20,
generalized this floor further). With that fixed: \code{StromgrenSphere}
and all six \code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021} sweep configurations
completed cleanly with sane, bounded $r_{\rm HII}$; the e10 task-graph
diagnostic showed a real, quantified improvement (time to reach
$R_{\rm st}$ roughly $47\%$ faster) without fully closing that
diagnostic's own shape mismatch: consistent with the search-radius
cap being a separate, also-contributing factor there.

\textbf{Not yet done (2026-07-20):} the user has indicated this should
eventually become the default (replacing the fixed cadence), but wants
more testing first: it remains opt-in, off by default, until that
testing happens.
