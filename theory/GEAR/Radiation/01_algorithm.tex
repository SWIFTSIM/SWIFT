\section{Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Physical basis: FIRE-2's Strömgren approximation}
\label{sec:fire2-basis}

An H\,{\sc ii} region in photoionization equilibrium is set by a
balance: the star's ionizing photon rate is matched by the
recombination rate integrated over the ionized volume. A uniform
medium gives this balance a closed-form radius (the Strömgren
radius); the non-uniform, particle-sampled density field an SPH
simulation actually has does not, so \citet[Appendix~E]{Hopkins2018}'s
FIRE-2 scheme evaluates the balance directly, gas element by gas
element, rather than solving for a radius. Given a star's ionizing
photon rate $\dot N_{\rm ion}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:planck-photon-rate},
Appendix~\ref{sec:blackbody}), the algorithm proceeds as follows, once
per rebuild pass:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Gather the gas particles within the star's current search
    radius and sort them by distance.
  \item Walk that list closest-first. For each particle in turn, price
    the recombinations it would cost to keep it ionized
    (Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget}).
  \item If the star's remaining budget can still afford that cost,
    flag the particle as ionized and deduct the cost from the budget;
    otherwise stop spending in that direction.
\end{enumerate}
This simple algorithm grows the ionized region outward from the star by
spending its photon budget on the closest not-yet-ionized,
not-already-hot candidate first, until the budget runs out
(Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget} gives the per-particle cost;
Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting} the boundary treatment that keeps
the average ionized mass unbiased). This branch follows Hu et al.\ and
Smith et al.\ in building directly on that scheme, with three
deliberate departures from the printed FIRE-2 formula: the hydrogen
atom count omits FIRE-2's $\mu$ factor (the mean molecular weight FIRE-2
divides through by when converting a particle's mass to a hydrogen
atom count), following Hu et al.\ and Smith et al., who instead use the
bare $N_{H,b}=X_H m_b/m_p$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost} below (there is
no room for $\mu$ in the underlying $\alpha_B n_e n_H
V$ balance); $\beta$ is evaluated at the particle's own temperature
rather than fixed at the papers' common $10^4\,$K reference
(Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature}); and the region stops growing at
the search radius and the candidate gates of
Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget}, not FIRE-2's domain edge or
metagalactic-background cutoff. This branch also carries over Smith et
al.'s angular (HEALPix) extension to the base FIRE-2 scheme, absent
from Hu et al.'s purely spherical treatment: the budget can be split
into directional cones (Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}) so a
dense clump near the star cannot absorb photons earmarked for other
directions, avoiding the mass-biasing a single shared budget produces.

\subsection{Photon budget and recombination cost}
\label{sec:photon-budget}

Every active star's ionizing photon rate $\dot N_{\rm ion}$, used
throughout this section, comes from a blackbody spectrum fit to the
star's mass; see Appendix~\ref{sec:blackbody} for the fit itself
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:mass-radius}--\ref{eq:planck-photon-rate}) and its one
caveat (an unrelated, unused dead-code mass--temperature relation).

\subsubsection{What a Tag Is}

A gas particle a star has claimed carries three
pieces of state, all set by \code{radiation\_tag\_part\_as\_ionized}:
the owning star's id, an \code{end\_time} at which the claim lapses
(Section~\ref{sec:persistence}), and, until that time, a forced
thermodynamic state (Section~\ref{sec:temperature-floor} in the
default mode, or the Grackle source terms of
Section~\ref{sec:rate-coupled} in the rate-coupled one). Of these,
only the star id is one-way: \code{radiation\_reset\_part\_ionized\_
tag} clears the active claim once \code{end\_time} lapses, but never
clears which star last owned the particle, a distinction
Section~\ref{sec:front-radius} relies on. ``Tagged'' below always
means holding an active (not yet lapsed) claim.

\subsubsection{Candidate Gating and the Recombination Rate}

Each active star with ionizing photon rate $\dot N_{\rm ion}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:planck-photon-rate}, Appendix~\ref{sec:blackbody}) gathers
candidate gas particles within a search radius, sorted by distance,
and spends its budget on them closest-first. A particle is a candidate
for ionization only if

\begin{enumerate}
\item it is not already tagged,
\item is denser than \code{GEARFeedback:HII\_min\_density\_Hpcm3},
\item and is not already above $\approx 10^4\,$K (\code{feedback\_part\_can\_be\_ionized}).
\end{enumerate}

For a candidate particle $b$ of mass $m_b$, hydrogen mass fraction $X_H$,
and physical density $\rho_b$, the number of hydrogen atoms and the
recombination rate needed to keep it ionized are
\begin{align}
  N_{H,b} &= \frac{X_H\, m_b}{m_p}, \\
  n_{e,b} &= \frac{X_H\, \rho_b}{m_p}, \\
  \Delta \dot N_b &= N_{H,b}\, \beta\, n_{e,b},
  \label{eq:recomb-cost}
\end{align}
where $m_p$ is the proton mass and $\beta$ is the case-B recombination
coefficient, evaluated at the particle's own applied ionized
temperature rather than fixed at the papers' common $10^4\,$K
reference (\code{radiation\_get\_case\_b\_recombination\_\allowbreak
coefficient\_cgs}, Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature}).
Equation~\ref{eq:recomb-cost} is
identical in form to Hu et al.'s $N_H\beta n_H$ (their $N_H$ is called
``the number of hydrogen nuclei'', an unambiguous atom count) and to
Smith et al.'s $R_{\rm rec}=\beta m \rho (X_H/m_p)^2$. 

\subsubsection{Claim Cost vs.\ Maintenance Rate}

Hu et al.\ and Smith et al.\
test the photon budget as an instantaneous \emph{rate} comparison: at
each pass, is the star's ionizing photon rate $\dot N_{\rm ion}$ still
enough to cover the summed recombination rate,
$\sum_b \Delta \dot N_b$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}), of the gas it
currently holds ionized? \swift instead advances against an
\emph{integrated} budget: each pass, the star has
$\dot N_{\rm ion}\,\Delta t_{\rm back}$ photons to spend over the
interval $\Delta t_{\rm back}$ since its previous pass
(Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}), and every candidate it
considers is charged a number of atoms or electrons for that interval,
not a rate. For a fresh candidate, that one-off claim cost is
Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}'s rate integrated over $\Delta t_{\rm back}$,
plus a one-off cost to strip its still-neutral electrons:
\begin{equation}
  {\rm cost}_b = N_{HI,b} + \Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta \dot N_b,
  \label{eq:claim-cost}
\end{equation}
where $N_{HI,b}$ is the particle's \emph{neutral}-hydrogen atom count
(\code{radiation\_get\_part\_number\_neutral\_hydrogen\_atoms}), not
the total $N_{H,b}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}. At
\code{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE}$\,\ge 1$, where species fractions are
tracked, a candidate that is already partly or fully ionized: gas
re-tagged after its previous tag lapsed on a marginal budget shortfall,
or pre-ionized by an external UV background: does not need to pay
again to strip electrons it has already lost; at mode~0, with no
species tracked, $N_{HI,b}$ falls back to the conservative $N_{H,b}$.
The maintenance charge levied on gas the star already holds ionized
(Section~\ref{sec:persistence}) drops the $N_{HI,b}$ term and uses only
the integrated rate, $\Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta \dot N_b$: those
electrons are already stripped, so there is no neutral-hydrogen term to
pay again.

\subsubsection{Implicit Region Growth}

That same integrated-rate reserve is what keeps a newly-claimed
region's growth consistent with the physical growth of an H\,{\sc ii}
region, and Eq.~\ref{eq:implicit-growth} below follows
directly from the star's photon budget above. Write $S$ for the
number of atoms/electrons the star is currently paying to keep
ionized. Over one pass, the star's $\dot N_{\rm ion}\,\Delta t_{\rm
back}$ photons pay for exactly three things: stripping the $\Delta S$
atoms newly claimed this pass (the $N_{HI,b}$ term of
Eq.~\ref{eq:claim-cost}, summed over the newly-claimed particles,
totals $\Delta S$); the recombinations those same $\Delta S$ atoms
will need over the rest of the interval (the reserve term of
Eq.~\ref{eq:claim-cost}, since each atom's own recombination rate is
$1/t_{\rm rec}$, totals $\Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta S/t_{\rm rec}$);
and the recombinations of the $S$ atoms already held ionized from
previous passes (the maintenance charge above, $\Delta t_{\rm
back}\,S/t_{\rm rec}$). Spending the whole budget on exactly these
three terms,
\[
  \dot N_{\rm ion}\,\Delta t_{\rm back} =
  \Delta S + \frac{\Delta t_{\rm back}}{t_{\rm rec}}\,\Delta S
  + \frac{\Delta t_{\rm back}}{t_{\rm rec}}\,S,
\]
and solving for $\Delta S$ gives
\begin{equation}
  \Delta S = (S_{\rm eq}-S)\,\frac{\Delta t_{\rm back}}{t_{\rm rec}+\Delta t_{\rm back}},
  \qquad S_{\rm eq} \equiv \dot N_{\rm ion}\,t_{\rm rec},
  \label{eq:implicit-growth}
\end{equation}
where $t_{\rm rec} \equiv 1/(\alpha_B(T_i)\,n_H)$ is the
recombination timescale and $S_{\rm eq}$ is the
Strömgren atom count: the number of atoms whose recombinations exactly
balance the star's photon rate in equilibrium. This is Spitzer's
time-dependent Strömgren-sphere relation \citep{Spitzer1978},
$dS/dt=(S_{\rm eq}-S)/t_{\rm rec}$: photoionizations build the region
while recombinations drain it, and $S$ relaxes towards $S_{\rm eq}$
on the timescale $t_{\rm rec}$, with exact continuous-time solution
$S(t)=S_{\rm eq}\,(1-e^{-t/t_{\rm rec}})$, of which
Eq.~\ref{eq:implicit-growth} is the discrete-pass analogue: $\Delta S$
is $S_{\rm eq}-S$ scaled down by
$\Delta t_{\rm back}/(t_{\rm rec}+\Delta t_{\rm back})\in(0,1)$, so
$S$ can approach but never overshoot $S_{\rm eq}$ in a single pass,
whatever the length of $\Delta t_{\rm back}$.

Had the reserve term instead been dropped from
Eq.~\ref{eq:claim-cost}, i.e.\ a claim charged only the stripping cost
$N_{HI,b}$ and not its own future recombinations, the same budget
equation without the $\Delta t_{\rm back}\,\Delta S/t_{\rm rec}$ term
gives $\Delta S = (S_{\rm eq}-S)\,\Delta t_{\rm back}/t_{\rm rec}$
instead: the region's early-time, recombination-free growth rate
applied uniformly across the whole pass. That is correct only while
$\Delta t_{\rm back}\ll t_{\rm rec}$; once the pass interval is long
enough for the region to actually approach equilibrium, it can push
$S$ past $S_{\rm eq}$ and churn.

\subsubsection{Known Approximation: $n_e \approx n_H$}

Equation~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}
(and every other electron-density calculation in this model, including
the recombination timescale $t_{\rm rec}$ above) assumes one free
electron per hydrogen nucleus and does not track helium's ionization
state.
Ionized He contributes additional free electrons, so this
underestimates $n_e$. It is a structural simplification of the whole
model, not something any single feature here fixes: any future
recombination-timescale or electron-density calculation added to this
model inherits the same approximation.

\subsection{Temperature floor}
\label{sec:temperature-floor}

\subsubsection{The Floor Formula}

Once a particle is tagged, its internal energy is set (and reset every
step while tagged, in the cooling task, via
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid}) to
\begin{equation}
  u_{\rm new} = \min\left(\Delta u_{\rm ionized},\ u_{\rm collisional}\right),
  \label{eq:floor}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta u_{\rm ionized} = N_{H,b} E_{\rm ion}/m_b$ is the specific
energy needed to fully ionize the particle ($E_{\rm ion}=13.6\,$eV per
H atom, metallicity-independent), and $u_{\rm collisional}$ comes from
a metallicity-dependent equilibrium temperature, $T_{\rm collisional}$:
the temperature at which photoheating balances collisional (mostly
metal-line) cooling for gas of that metallicity, hence the name.
Unlike the rest of Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}
is not from FIRE-2: FIRE-2 clamps ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K, but from
its successor, \citet[][the metallicity-dependent photoionization
temperature fit]{Hopkins2023}:
\begin{equation}
  T_{\rm collisional} = 10^4\,{\rm K} \times
  \begin{cases}
    \min\left(6.62,\ \dfrac{0.86}{1+0.22\log_{10}(Z/Z_\odot)}\right) & Z \ge 10^{-3}Z_\odot \\[2mm]
    6.62 & Z < 10^{-3}Z_\odot.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:tcollisional}
\end{equation}
Neither Hu et al.\ nor Smith et al.\ have a metallicity-dependent floor
at all: both simply clamp ionized gas to a flat $10^4\,$K.

\subsubsection{Applying the Floor: Energy Accounting}

$u_{\rm new}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}) is never written to the particle directly.
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid} (\code{cooling\_gear\_subgrid.h})
returns it to its caller, \code{cooling\_new\_energy}, which returns
immediately on a tagged particle without ever calling Grackle's own
chemistry/cooling solve (\code{local\_solve\_chemistry}): the
untagged path always calls it. Skipping that call is what makes the
floor hold for the step: Grackle's ODE integrator has no notion of the
external photoionizing forcing this model represents and would
otherwise relax the just-floored energy back toward its own
equilibrium within the same step it was applied. $u_{\rm new}$ then
re-enters \code{cooling\_cool\_part} exactly like any other cooling
function's return value: floored again at
\code{hydro\_props->minimal\_internal\_energy} if needed, and is
turned into a rate,
\begin{equation}
  \left.\D{u}{t}\right|_{\rm cool} =
  \frac{u_{\rm new} - u_{\rm ad,\,before}}{dt_{\rm therm}},
  \label{eq:cool-dudt}
\end{equation}
which is added to hydro's own $du/dt$ and handed to the kick: the
ionization floor is applied exactly once per step, through the same
integration path as ordinary cooling, subject to the same floors. It
is also subject to the same suppression as ordinary cooling when
\code{cooling->thermal\_time} delayed-cooling turn-off is active for
this particle (\code{time - time\_last\_event < thermal\_time}): while
that window is open, \code{cooling\_new\_energy}: and with it
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid}: is not called at all, so a
tagged particle is not re-floored until the window closes.

The bookkeeping this produces is exact, not merely consistent:
\code{cooling\_cool\_part} records, every step,
\begin{equation}
  \Delta E_{\rm radiated} = -m_b\left(u_{\rm new} - u_{\rm ad,\,before}\right)
  = m_b\left(u_{\rm ad,\,before} - u_{\rm new}\right)
\end{equation}
into \code{xp->cooling\_data.radiated\_energy}, from the identical
$u_{\rm new}$ Eq.~\ref{eq:cool-dudt} used. There is no separate
accounting path for the ionization floor: the recorded
radiated energy (negative, i.e.\ recorded as heating, whenever the
floor raises the particle's energy) always equals the energy
correction actually applied to the particle that step.

\code{radiation.c} computes Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional}'s $\log_{10}$
with a base-10 logarithm throughout, for every $Z \ge
10^{-3}Z_\odot$. This project's reference metallicity point,
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$, is chosen specifically because that base
makes $T_{\rm collisional}=10^4\,$K there, matching
\citet{Hu2017}/\citet{Smith2021}'s own reference temperature (and,
through Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature} below, the case-B $\beta$
evaluated at it whenever $u_{\rm collisional} < \Delta u_{\rm
ionized}$).

\subsection{Temperature dependence of $\beta$}
\label{sec:beta-temperature}

\citet{HuiGnedin1997}, Appendix A, give a fit to the case-B H\,{\sc
ii} recombination coefficient (from \citealt{Ferland1992}'s data,
accurate to 0.7\% from 1\,K to $10^9\,$K: effectively exact for our
purposes):
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_B(T) = 2.753\times10^{-14}\,
  \frac{\lambda^{1.500}}{\left[1+(\lambda/2.740)^{0.407}\right]^{2.242}}
  \ {\rm cm^3\,s^{-1}}, \qquad
  \lambda = \frac{315614\,{\rm K}}{T}.
  \label{eq:alphaB-huignedin}
\end{equation}
The same fit is used elsewhere in \swift: identically in
\code{src/rt/SPHM1RT/rt\_cooling\_rates.h}, and inside grackle's own
\code{k2\_rate()} (which GEAR-RT delegates to for its own case-B
recombination, via the \code{CaseBRecombination} flag: GEAR-RT has no
case-B formula of its own). Here too, $\beta$ in
Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost} is Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin} evaluated at
$T$, not a fixed constant.
$T$ is the particle's own applied ionized temperature: the same
$u_{\rm new}$ from Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}, converted to a temperature via
its own $\mu$, so the recombination cost charged against a star's
budget is always consistent with the temperature that particle is
actually being held at
(\code{radiation\_get\_part\_ionized\_internal\_energy}, shared between
\code{radiation\_get\_part\_rate\_to\_fully\_ionize} and
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid} to guarantee the two never
disagree; \code{radiation\_get\_case\_b\_recombination\_\allowbreak
coefficient\_cgs} implements Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin} itself).

For reference, evaluating Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin} across the
range this model's own temperature floor
(Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}--\ref{eq:tcollisional}) applies
($\approx$8,600\,K at $Z=Z_\odot$ up to $\approx$47,500\,K at $Z=0$):
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lr}
  \hline
  $T$ [K] & $\alpha_B(T)$ [cm$^3$ s$^{-1}$] \\
  \hline
  8,600 ($Z=Z_\odot$)  & $2.94\times10^{-13}$ \\
  20,000               & $1.43\times10^{-13}$ \\
  47,500 ($Z=0$)        & $6.42\times10^{-14}$ \\
  \hline
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
$\alpha_B(T)$ falls by more than a factor of 4 across this range, so
evaluating it at the particle's own applied temperature
(Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin}) rather than any single reference value
materially changes the recombination cost charged against a star's
budget, most at low metallicity, where the floor temperature is
highest.

\subsection{Applying a tag: flag-and-floor vs.\ rate-coupled}
\label{sec:rate-coupled}

Everything from Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget} onward, closest-first
candidate selection, claim/maintenance pricing, and the temperature
floor, describes how a star decides \emph{which} gas to tag and for
how long. What happens to a tagged particle's thermodynamics is a
separate, swappable choice, controlled by
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate}
(\code{cooling\_gear\_subgrid.h}).

\subsubsection{Default: Flag-and-Floor}

With the flag off (the default),
Section~\ref{sec:temperature-floor} is the whole story:
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid} forces the particle's internal
energy to $u_{\rm new}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}) every step and skips
Grackle's own chemistry/cooling solve entirely while the tag is held.
The particle's ionization state is never actually tracked; it is
simply assumed fully ionized and held at the floor temperature for as
long as the tag lasts.

\subsubsection{Rate-Coupled: \texttt{HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate=1}}

Requires \code{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE}$\,\ge 1$ (species tracked); the
gate is compile-time (\code{\#if COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE > 0} in
\code{cooling\_gear\_subgrid.h}), so a \code{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE=0}
build never even evaluates the flag: setting it in the parameter file
at that mode is a silent no-op, with no warning at start-up, and the
run falls back to flag-and-floor unconditionally. Here the tag no
longer forces an energy: \code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_
subgrid} returns immediately without touching $u_{\rm new}$, and
Grackle's own non-equilibrium primordial-chemistry solver runs as
usual. What the tag supplies instead is a source term for that solver.
At the moment a particle is claimed or renewed, the model evaluates
the ionizing photon flux the tagging star delivers at that particle's
distance, $\dot N_{\rm ion}/(\Omega_{\rm pix}\, r^2)$ (the star's
remaining budget in that particle's own pixel,
Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}, diluted over the pixel's solid
angle $\Omega_{\rm pix}=4\pi/N_{\rm pix}$ and the physical distance
$r$), and converts it, via the standard hydrogen photoionization
cross-section $\sigma_{\rm HI}=6.3\times10^{-18}\,{\rm cm^2}$ (Osterbrock
\& Ferland 2006), into a photoionization rate coefficient
$\Gamma_{\rm HI}$
(\code{radiation\_get\_photoionization\_rate\_coefficient\_from\_flux\_HI}).
A companion quantity, the star's mean excess photon energy above the
$13.6\,$eV threshold (from the same blackbody spectrum as
Eq.~\ref{eq:planck-photon-rate},
\code{radiation\_get\_individual\_star\_mean\_excess\_photon\_energy\_HI}),
gives a heating rate $\Gamma_{\rm HI}\times E_{\rm excess}$. Both are
frozen on the particle at tag time and injected into Grackle every
step the tag is held
(\code{cooling\_get\_rate\_coupled\_RT\_fields\_subgrid}, feeding
Grackle's \code{RT\_HI\_ionization\_rate}/\code{RT\_heating\_rate}
fields), so Grackle's own solver, not a hand-set floor, determines the
resulting HII fraction $x_{\rm HII}$ and temperature self-consistently
with the gas's own cooling.

The candidate search, budget spend, and claim/maintenance pricing of
Sections~\ref{sec:photon-budget}--\ref{sec:beta-temperature} are
identical in both modes: $\beta$ is still evaluated at the temperature
$u_{\rm new}$ \emph{would} apply (Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature}),
purely as a pricing reference for the budget, whether or not that
temperature is ever written to the particle. Only the consequence of
holding a tag, forced energy vs.\ injected Grackle rates, differs.
This is also why the two modes read out a star's ionized region
differently; see Section~\ref{sec:front-radius}.

\subsection{Per-cone photon budget (angular splitting)}
\label{sec:angular-splitting}

To avoid a dense clump near a source absorbing a disproportionate
share of the photon budget regardless of the small solid angle it
subtends (``mass-biasing''), \code{HII\_angular\_nside} splits the
budget into $N_{\rm pix} = 12\,{\rm nside}^2$ equal shares
($\dot N_{\rm ion}/N_{\rm pix}$ each) and assigns each candidate
particle to exactly one pixel (cone) by its direction from the star,
hard assignment (no fractional overlap, matching both papers' own
choice). \code{nside=0} degenerates to $N_{\rm pix}=1$, i.e.\ today's
plain spherical behaviour. \code{nside} can be any non-negative
integer: the HEALPix RING scheme, unlike NEST, has no power-of-2
restriction; but the practical ceiling is memory, not geometry: each
star carries a fixed-size per-pixel array sized at configure time by
\code{--with-number-of-hii-angular-pixels} (default 12, i.e.\
$\text{nside}\le1$); requesting more pixels than the build supports
errors clearly at start-up rather than overflowing the array.

\subsubsection{Uniform vs.\ Per-Cone Spending}

Under the uniform
($\text{nside}=0$) scheme the star's whole budget is one shared pool,
spent by distance alone with no notion of direction: a dense structure
close to the star absorbs photons regardless of the solid angle it
subtends, at the expense of gas lying further out in
\emph{every} direction, including directions with no such structure at
all. The resulting ionized region is then shaped by wherever mass
happens to sit nearest the star, not by the star's own emission, which
is isotropic. Splitting the budget into pixels first, before spending
it by distance within each, removes this coupling between directions:
a clump can only ever draw on its own cone's fixed share
($\dot N_{\rm ion}/N_{\rm pix}$) and cannot absorb photons earmarked
for any other cone, so every direction grows to the extent its own
local density allows, independent of what the gas distribution looks
like elsewhere on the sphere. This is the only physical difference
between the two modes: the spending mechanics within a given angular
partition (closest-first, boundary-particle handling) are identical
either way, and $\text{nside}=0$ is simply the $N_{\rm pix}=1$ case of
the same scheme.

\subsubsection{Opening a Pass's Budget: What Carries Over}

At the start of
each rebuild pass, every pixel's photon count is (re)opened from
\begin{equation}
  N_{{\rm ion},p}^{\rm open} =
  \min\!\left(N_{{\rm ion},p}^{\rm prev},\,0\right) +
  \dot N_{{\rm ion},p}\,\Delta t_{\rm back},
  \label{eq:budget-open}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_{\rm back}$ is the time elapsed since this star's
previous pass and $N_{{\rm ion},p}^{\rm prev}$ is whatever the pixel's
count stood at when that pass ended. In
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_rebuild\_time\_Myr}$\,<0$ (``rebuild every
step'') mode, $\Delta t_{\rm back}$ tracks the star's own timestep,
which can be arbitrarily small; the nominal cadence used to bound how
large a single pass's $\Delta t_{\rm back}$ is trusted to be is
floored at \code{GEARFeedback:HII\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr}
(\code{feedback\_get\_star\_HII\_nominal\_interval}), the same
constant that also floors the star's own timestep in that mode so it
cannot collapse toward zero. Unspent \emph{positive} budget is
not carried forward: those photons reached no gas and escaped;
but a \emph{negative} balance is: a pass can overdraw a pixel by up to
one particle's cost (the boundary-particle rule below), and that debt
is repaid out of the next pass's income before any new spending
happens, rather than forgiven. Forgiving it every pass instead would
over-issue photons in proportion to how many passes elapse per unit
time, i.e.\ as $1/\Delta t_{\rm back}$: a cadence-dependent bias,
which this model is built to avoid.

\subsubsection{Sequential Spend Within a Pass}

A pass gathers its candidates
into a single buffer sorted by distance from the star, each entry
tagged with the pixel its direction falls in. Scanning that buffer in
order and skipping (not stopping at) entries whose pixel is already
exhausted visits every pixel's own candidates in ascending-distance
order: a sorted sequence's subsequence is still sorted, so each
cone spends closest-first exactly like the spherical ($N_{\rm
pix}=1$) case, just interleaved with every other cone's spending in
the same traversal.

\subsubsection{The Boundary Particle}

A pixel's boundary particle for the
pass is the first candidate, in that pixel's own distance order, whose
claim cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:claim-cost}) it can no longer afford outright:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{HII\_deterministic\_boundary\_ionization=1}: ionize it
    anyway, overdrawing the pixel (bounded by Eq.~\ref{eq:budget-open}'s
    debt carry to at most this one particle's cost);
  \item \code{HII\_deterministic\_boundary\_ionization=0} (the
    default): draw a single random number, keyed on the star id, the
    pixel, and the integer time at the start of the step
    (\code{random\_number\_HII\_regions}): \emph{not} on the
    candidate particle, and ionize the boundary particle iff the
    draw is at most the affordable fraction
    $\text{budget}_p/{\rm cost}_b$.
\end{itemize}
Because the draw is fixed for the whole pixel this pass, at most one
particle per pixel can win it: a win drives the pixel's budget
negative (never enough to also afford the next, farther candidate),
so every later comparison in the same pixel this pass evaluates a
non-positive affordable fraction and always loses. This is what gives
expectation-level photon conservation,
$\mathbb{E}[\text{spent}] = P_{\rm win}\cdot{\rm cost}_b +
(1-P_{\rm win})\cdot 0 = \text{budget}_p$, and specifically requires
the single fixed draw: rolling independently per candidate in a loop
that stops at the first win would instead claim a boundary particle
almost surely, spending its full cost rather than the pixel's actual
remaining budget. The probabilistic default is the better choice in
practice too: deterministic mode over-ionizes, mildly for the
spherical scheme and severely for the angular one (each pixel's own
small budget makes its one guaranteed over-spend a proportionally
larger error), with no compensating benefit.

\subsection{Persistence and maintenance}
\label{sec:persistence}

A tagged particle stays flagged (and re-floored, per
Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}, every step) until
\texttt{end\_time = tag\_time\allowbreak{} +
GEARFeedback:HII\_rebuild\_time\_Myr}, at which
point it is unflagged and becomes eligible to compete for budget again
on the star's next rebuild pass. Each star's rebuild is scheduled
independently off its own age and its own last-rebuild time
(\code{feedback\_common.c}); there is no cross-star coordination or
deliberate synchronous/asynchronous toggle like the one
\citet{Smith2021} implemented (they found no difference between the
two). For the co-located, simultaneously-born stars in the validation
example this produces synchronous rebuilds in practice, simply because
the stars are identical, not because the code enforces it.

\subsubsection{Maintenance: Renewing Gas Already Held Ionized}

Before a pass
spends anything on new candidates, it first renews the gas the star
already holds. \code{runner\_dosub\_stars\_hii\_ionization\_feedback}
gathers every particle currently tagged with this star's own id,
via a traversal exempt from the ordinary candidate filter
(\code{feedback\_part\_can\_be\_ionized}), since held gas is neither
cold nor untagged: into a scratch buffer, then charges each one's
maintenance rate (Section~\ref{sec:photon-budget}) via
\code{feedback\_iact\_HII\_maintain\_ionized\_part}, which also renews
its \texttt{end\_time} so the tag survives to the following pass. Every
rebuild after the one that first claimed a particle therefore
re-charges its ongoing recombination cost, rather than charging it
once at claim time and never again.

\subsubsection{Maintenance Order: A Receding Front Recedes from the
Outside In}

The scratch buffer is charged in ascending $(r^2,{\rm id})$
order: current squared distance from the star, with particle id as
the tiebreak for particles at coincident $r^2$: \emph{not} the order
cell traversal happens to visit them in. This order is what determines
which particles a budget shortfall lapses: charging nearest-first
means an exhausted pixel simply stops renewing its most distant held
particles first, which then reach their already-recorded
\texttt{end\_time} unrenewed and expire on their own next cooling
step. A receding region therefore shrinks from its outer shell inward,
rather than developing a hole wherever the traversal order happened to
run out of budget. Each held particle is re-priced against the same
pixel it was assigned to when claimed or last renewed
(\code{feedback\_iact\_HII\_maintain\_ionized\_part} passes the stored
pixel index straight through to
\code{radiation\_consume\_ionizing\_photons}), so upkeep tracks
ownership, not a particle's current direction from the star even if it
has since drifted into another cone.

The star's extent measure, $h_{\rm hii}$, is updated from this same
pass: it is set to the largest $r$ among particles the star still
actually holds after maintenance (tracked for every held particle
regardless of whether its renewal was affordable this pass, so a
shrinking region promptly shrinks $h_{\rm hii}$, and with it the
search radius and locking footprint, for a star doing correspondingly
less work), not the historical high-water mark of everything the star
has ever claimed: see Section~\ref{sec:front-radius} for the
distinct, historical-extent measure used to read out this model's
region from the outside.

Budget carryover between passes: unspent positive budget discarded,
negative debt carried forward and repaid first, is
Eq.~\ref{eq:budget-open} (Section~\ref{sec:angular-splitting}); the
same rule governs every pixel a star's maintenance and new-candidate
spending draw from.

\subsection{Front-radius measure}
\label{sec:front-radius}

Two on-particle quantities give two different readings of how far a
star's ionizing influence currently reaches, and they can diverge
whenever a region recedes (Section~\ref{sec:persistence}).

\subsubsection{Instantaneous Front: \texttt{h\_hii} (\texttt{HIIRegionRadii})}

The star's own $h_{\rm hii}$, updated every maintenance pass
(Section~\ref{sec:persistence}), is the distance out to which the
star's budget \emph{currently} keeps gas ionized. It shrinks as soon
as a shortfall stops renewing the outer shell, tracking the region's
present state exactly.

\subsubsection{Historical Extent: The Ownership Stamp}

A gas particle's
ionizing-star id (\code{radiation\_tag\_part\_as\_ionized}'s
\code{star\_id}, exposed to snapshots as \texttt{HIIStarIDs}) is a
one-way stamp:
\code{radiation\_reset\_part\_ionized\_tag} clears the active
\emph{tag} once a particle's \texttt{end\_time} lapses, but never
clears which star last owned it. A particle the star ionized and later
stopped renewing therefore still carries that star's id and keeps
advecting with the flow, marking where the swept shell has been even
after the active front has receded past it. The largest current
distance among a star's ever-owned gas is a historical-extent measure,
distinct from, and always at least as large as, $h_{\rm hii}$.

For the default flag-and-floor scheme, the historical-extent measure
is the more useful proxy for ``how far did this star's ionization
front get'': the swept shell keeps its ownership stamp after tag
expiry, so it directly reads the front's high-water mark. For the
rate-coupled scheme of Section~\ref{sec:rate-coupled}, where the tag
drives Grackle's own non-equilibrium ionization state $x_{\rm HII}$
rather than forcing a temperature, $h_{\rm hii}$ is instead the
measure that coincides with the actual $x_{\rm HII}$ front; the
historical-extent measure would instead freeze at the historical
maximum once the region starts to recede.

\subsubsection{Robust Historical Extent: Gap-Aware Outlier Rejection}

A
single particle whose tag lapsed early and then advected outward for
several dynamical times can dominate a plain maximum over ever-owned
distances and inflate the reported extent by double digits percent,
without any genuine coverage problem. The validation checks
(\code{examples/SubgridTests/SubgridRadiation/\{StromgrenSphere,\allowbreak
Hu2017,\allowbreak Starbench\}/
*\_analytic\_check.py}, function \code{robust\_ever\_tagged\_radius})
read the historical extent robustly instead of taking this maximum
directly: sort ever-owned distances descending, estimate a local point
spacing from the median gap among the outermost few percent of
points, then walk inward from the largest distance and discard a
point only while the gap to its next-lower neighbour exceeds several
times that local spacing: an isolated advected straggler produces a
gap an order of magnitude or more above the front's own natural
surface roughness, so this stops at the first point that is not such
an outlier. The true (unrejected) maximum is still reported alongside
it as a secondary diagnostic.

This robustness is also what makes the search-radius expansion
artifact of Section~\ref{sec:runtime} (in the task-graph document)
harmless to the measurement: an isolated particle tagged by an
expansion probe beyond the coherent front is exactly the
gap-separated case this rejection removes.
