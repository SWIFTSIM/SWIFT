\label{sec:bugs}

This section is append-only: entries stay even after being fixed, as

\subsection{2026-07-16: electron mass used instead of proton mass in the recombination cost}
\label{sec:mass-bug}

\code{radiation\_get\_part\_rate\_to\_fully\_ionize}
(\code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation.c}) computed the electron number
density as
\begin{equation}
  n_e = \frac{X_H \rho}{\mu\, m_e} \qquad (\text{wrong: } m_e = \text{electron mass}),
\end{equation}
instead of Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}'s $n_e = X_H\rho/m_p$. This
inflated every gas particle's recombination cost -- and hence the
budget a star was charged to maintain it as ionized -- by a factor of
$m_p/m_e \approx 1836$ (times a further $O(1$--$2\times)$ factor from
the spurious $\mu$ described below). At that inflation, a star's
entire photon budget could not fully afford even one gas particle's
cost, so every rebuild cycle tagged at most one particle (via the
deterministic boundary-particle override), then reported the budget
exhausted.

\textbf{How it was found:} the example
(\code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021}) was reported to show
essentially no HII region growth. A single-star, no-clump diagnostic
tracking \code{IsIonizedFlags} per particle over time showed exactly
one particle tagged per rebuild cycle, a different one each time
(hot-but-untagged and tagged-but-cold particles coexisting at every
snapshot -- flickering, not a growing core). Given the equilibrium
Str\"omgren volume at these parameters should sustain
$\dot N_{\rm ion}/\Delta\dot N_{\rm particle} \approx 20$ particles
simultaneously, a 20$\times$ shortfall pointed at the cost calculation
itself. Tracing \code{radiation\_get\_part\_rate\_to\_fully\_ionize}
found the $m_e$-for-$m_p$ swap.

\textbf{Fix:} \texttt{n\_e = (X\_H * rho) / m\_p}. Committed
\code{356ec0cda} (first pass, together with removing $\mu$; see
below for why $\mu$ went back and forth).

\textbf{Verification:} rebuilt, reran the same single-star diagnostic.
Tagged-particle count went from pinned at 1 to a smoothly growing
population (17$\to$59 over $t=0$--0.98\,Myr); $r_{\rm HII}$ grew
continuously with no plateau (0$\to$4.9\,pc over the same window,
still accelerating). \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck} and
\texttt{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass;
\code{StromgrenSphere} acceptance runs at \code{gas\_mass=0.1} and
\code{gas\_mass=0.01} both ran clean to completion with no
regression.

\subsection{2026-07-16: mean-molecular-weight factor -- kept, then removed, after checking the source}
\label{sec:mu-bug}

The first fix pass also removed a $\mu$ factor from both $N_H$ and
$n_e$, on the grounds that a hydrogen-atom count has no natural place
for a mean-particle-mass term. Darwin pointed out that
\citet{Hopkins2018}, Appendix E, states explicitly
\[
  \Delta\dot N_b = N(H)_b\,\beta\,n_{e,b}, \qquad
  N(H)_b = \frac{X_H m_b}{\mu\, m_p},
\]
i.e.\ FIRE-2's own formula \emph{does} include $\mu$. The fix was
reverted to keep $\mu$, pending a check.

Fetching the actual FIRE-2 PDF and re-deriving from the underlying
physics resolved it: the recombination rate within a gas element must
equal $\alpha_B\, n_e\, n_H\, V$, so $N_H \equiv n_H V$ is definitionally
the raw hydrogen atom count $X_H m/m_p$ -- there is no room for $\mu$
in that derivation regardless of what FIRE-2's text says. Both
\citet{Hu2017} (``the number of hydrogen \emph{nuclei}'', explicitly
$N_H = m_g X_H/m_p$) and \citet{Smith2021}
($R_{\rm rec}=\beta m\rho(X_H/m_p)^2$) use the $\mu$-free form, and
\code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021} exists specifically to match
those two papers' numbers. $\mu$ was removed again (final state,
Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost}), committed as part of \code{356ec0cda}.

\textbf{Method note:} this flip-flopped once before landing on the
derivation-based answer. When a paper's prose labels a quantity in a
way that doesn't match its role in the surrounding equation (FIRE-2
calls $N(H)_b$ ``the number of H atoms'' despite the $\mu$ factor
making it something else), trust the derivation over the label, and
check against multiple independent sources before committing.

\subsection{2026-07-16: case-B recombination coefficient made temperature-dependent}
\label{sec:caseB-fix}

$\beta$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:recomb-cost} was a fixed constant,
$\alpha_B(10^4\,{\rm K})$, matching both reference papers' own
convention but not this model's own metallicity-dependent temperature
floor (Eq.~\ref{eq:floor}--\ref{eq:tcollisional}), which holds ionized
gas well above $10^4\,$K at low metallicity ($\approx$47,500\,K at
$Z=0$). Section~\ref{sec:beta-temperature} quantified this as a
$\sim$75\% overestimate of the true recombination cost at this
example's $Z=0$ default.

\textbf{Fix:} \code{radiation\_get\_part\_rate\_to\_fully\_ionize}
(\code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation.c}) now evaluates
\citet{HuiGnedin1997}'s $\alpha_B(T)$ fit
(Eq.~\ref{eq:alphaB-huignedin}) at the particle's own applied ionized
temperature, instead of the fixed constant. That temperature
computation (previously inlined separately in
\code{cooling\_ionize\_part\_subgrid}) was factored out into
\code{radiation\_get\_part\_ionized\_internal\_energy}, shared by both
functions, so the temperature used to floor a particle and the
temperature used to price its recombination cost can never disagree.

\textbf{Verification:} clean build (\code{-Werror}, both \code{swift}
and \code{swift\_mpi}); \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck} and
\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass;
\code{StromgrenSphere} \code{gas\_mass=0.1} acceptance run completed to
\code{time\_end} with no crash/assertion, no regression. An early,
$Z=0$ test of this fix confirmed the predicted direction (a
temperature-dependent $\beta$ measurably grows $r_{\rm HII}$) but also
showed $Z=0$ runs can now approach
\code{Stars:max\_HII\_search\_radius} -- this is why the validation
matrix (Section~\ref{sec:validation}) runs at
$Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ instead, which avoids the issue entirely
(Section~\ref{sec:metallicity}).

\subsection{2026-07-19: radiation pressure momentum computed but never applied}
\label{sec:rp-hit-flag-bug}

\code{radiation\_iact\_nonsym\_feedback\_apply}
(\code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation\_iact.h}, the radiation-pressure
momentum kick, documented in the Radiation Pressure \& ISRF logbook,
this same directory) correctly computed
and accumulated $\Delta\vec p_j$ (that logbook's per-particle momentum
formula) into
\code{xpj->feedback\_data.radiation.delta\_p}, but never set
\code{xpj->feedback\_data.hit\_by\_radiation}. That flag gates two
things: \code{feedback\_update\_part\_radiation}
(\code{radiation\_iact.h}) only converts \code{delta\_p} into an actual
velocity kick if it is set, and
\code{feedback\_update\_part}'s (\code{feedback/GEAR\_thermal/feedback.c})
own top-of-function early return checks it alongside
\code{hit\_by\_SN}/\code{hit\_by\_winds}/tagged-as-ionized -- a
particle receiving \emph{only} a radiation-pressure kick (no SN, no
winds, not HII-tagged) would return before ever reaching the radiation
branch. Net effect: every radiation-pressure momentum kick was
computed, stored, and then silently reset to zero
(\code{radiation\_iact.h}'s own reset line) without ever reaching the
gas particle's velocity -- the whole channel was a structural no-op
regardless of \code{radiation\_pressure\_efficiency}.

\textbf{How it was found:} read while documenting the radiation-pressure
channel for the first time (now the Radiation Pressure \& ISRF
logbook); noticed
\code{hit\_by\_radiation} is read/reset in exactly two places in
\code{radiation\_iact.h} but grepping the whole \code{src/} tree found
no assignment to \code{1} anywhere. The sibling flags
\code{hit\_by\_SN}/\code{hit\_by\_winds}
(\code{feedback/GEAR\_thermal/feedback\_iact.h:274,311}) are both
correctly set at their own point of momentum deposit, confirming this
is an isolated omission in the radiation-pressure code path, not a
different intended design.

\textbf{Fix:} added \code{xpj->feedback\_data.hit\_by\_radiation = 1;}
immediately after the \code{delta\_p} accumulation in
\code{radiation\_iact\_nonsym\_feedback\_apply}, matching the
SN/winds pattern exactly. Committed \code{baf5b1ff5}.

\textbf{Verification:} clean build (both \code{swift} and
\code{swift\_mpi}); \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck} and
\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass. No dedicated
radiation-pressure validation example exists yet to check the kick's
magnitude against an analytic expectation -- see the open-items
section of the Radiation Pressure \& ISRF logbook.

\subsection{2026-07-17/19: radiation code did not compile under any
non-GEAR feedback model}
\label{sec:t7-buildfix}

Radiation is GEAR-specific functionality, but generic files
(\code{space\_regrid.c}, \code{space\_split.c},
\code{runner\_doiact\_functions\_stars.h}) hard-depended on it, and
\code{src/Makefile.am} listed \code{runner\_radiation\_feedback.\{c,h\}}
unconditionally instead of gating them behind \code{COMMON\_GEAR\_NEEDED}
like \code{feedback/GEAR/*.c}. \code{--with-stars=basic
--with-feedback=none} (and every other non-GEAR feedback model) failed
to build.

\textbf{How it was found:} direct build check against
\code{--with-stars=basic --with-feedback=none}, then systematically
against every \code{--with-subgrid} preset (none, EAGLE, EAGLE-XL,
SPIN\_JET\_EAGLE, FLAMINGO, SPIN\_JET\_FLAMINGO, GEAR, GEAR-G3, AGORA,
QLA-EAGLE, QLA).

\textbf{Fix, three parts (commit \code{8569d8006}):}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Moved \code{runner\_radiation\_feedback.\{c,h\}} into the
  \code{COMMON\_GEAR\_NEEDED} source list in \code{src/Makefile.am}.
\item Added an always-zero \code{h\_hii} stub field to
  \code{stars/\{Basic,EAGLE,None\}/stars\_part.h} (zero-initialized in
  \code{stars\_first\_init\_spart}), so the generic cell-sizing code can
  keep reading it unconditionally.
\item Extended \code{none}/\code{EAGLE\_thermal}/\code{EAGLE\_kinetic}/
  \code{AGORA}'s \code{runner\_iact\_nonsym\_feedback\_density()}/
  \code{\_apply()} signatures to accept (and ignore) the same 4 extra
  parameters (\code{hydro\_props}, \code{phys\_const}, \code{us},
  \code{cooling}) GEAR\_thermal's own signature had already grown --
  every model must implement an identical call-site signature, SWIFT's
  own established convention for this interaction.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{A second, related leak found and fixed the same pass (commit
\code{59d10f898}):} \code{star\_formation/GEAR/star\_formation.h} and
\code{sink/GEAR/sink.h} called
\code{radiation\_is\_part\_tagged\_as\_ionized()} directly, relying on
it being transitively pulled in via \code{cooling.h} only when
\code{feedback=GEAR \&\& cooling=grackle} together -- undefined under
\code{--with-subgrid=AGORA} (\code{star\_formation=GEAR},
\code{feedback=AGORA}), a real, selectable combination.
\code{stars/GEAR/stars\_io.h}'s HII-mass snapshot converter had the
identical problem. Fixed by adding
\code{feedback\_is\_part\_tagged\_as\_ionized()}/
\code{feedback\_get\_star\_HII\_mass()} to every feedback model's
dispatch surface (GEAR\_thermal implements them for real;
none/EAGLE\_thermal/EAGLE\_kinetic/AGORA return false/0) instead of a
scattered \code{\#ifdef} guard at each call site.

While in the same area, \code{runner\_radiation\_feedback.c}'s own two
direct touches of \code{sp->feedback\_data.radiation}
(\code{n\_HII\_pixels}, \code{HII\_region\_last\_rebuild}) were wrapped
behind \code{feedback\_get\_star\_HII\_pixel\_count()}/
\code{feedback\_set\_star\_HII\_last\_rebuild()} in
\code{feedback\_common.\{h,c\}}, per the user's explicit request to
reduce how much GEAR-internal detail that file exposes.

\textbf{Verification:} every \code{--with-subgrid} preset except
\code{QLA} builds \code{swift}+\code{swift\_mpi} clean (\code{QLA}
fails on a pre-existing, unrelated bug with zero diff from master, not
this branch's regression). \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck}/
\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass under GEAR.

\textbf{Known gap, not fixed:} the density/apply signature widening
(point 3 above) grows the shared interface every feedback model must
implement identically rather than narrowing GEAR's own footprint --
the inverse direction from hiding GEAR internals. Revisit together if
a dispatch-interface refactor is ever done.

\subsection{2026-07-18: $T_{\rm collisional}$ used natural log instead
of $\log_{10}$}
\label{sec:t12-log10-bug}

\code{radiation.c}'s $T_{\rm collisional}$ fit (Eq.~\ref{eq:tcollisional})
used C's \code{log()} (natural log) instead of \code{log10()}, silently
applying the wrong base for every $Z\ge10^{-3}Z_\odot$ case -- including
this project's own $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ reference point, chosen
specifically because $\log_{10}$ makes $T_{\rm collisional}=10^4\,$K
there. The buggy code instead applied $\approx$12,700\,K.

\textbf{Fix:} \code{log}$\to$\code{log10}. Build clean,
\code{testRadiationRebuildCheck}/\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5}
pass.

\textbf{Verification:} the corrected (lower) temperature raises $\beta$
and lowers $c_s$, both pushing $r_{\rm HII}$ down -- confirmed by
rerunning the $Z/Z_\odot\approx0.231$ matrix and the e10 diagnostic.
\code{gas20\_z0231\_nside1\_rebuild0p01}: 24.7--27.7\,pc pre-fix
$\to$ 21.1--23.6\,pc post-fix, still inside the papers' 20--25\,pc
band. Effect on e10 was marginal (the search-radius-cap/cadence
mechanism this bug is independent of remained the dominant driver of
that check's mismatch).

\textbf{Open question, not resolved:} \citet{Hopkins2023} (FIRE-3)
quotes $T_{\rm eqm}/10^4\,{\rm K}\approx\min(6.62,\,
0.86/(1+0.22\,\ln[Z/Z_\odot]))$ -- their own text uses $\ln$, not
$\log_{10}$, conflicting with this fix's premise. Not re-litigated:
Draine (2011), the primary source both this logbook and
\citet{Hopkins2023} cite, is the actual tie-breaker, not either paper's
possibly-loose notation. If the exact $Z/Z_\odot\to T_{\rm collisional}$
mapping ever becomes load-bearing again, check Draine (2011) directly
before trusting either reading.

\subsection{2026-07-16: no safety check against
\texttt{Stars:max\_HII\_search\_radius} exceeding the box}
\label{sec:t6-boxsize-warning}

\code{Stars:max\_HII\_search\_radius} was read as a plain user-set
float with no internal check against the periodic box size -- an
inconsistent setup (cap larger than the box half-width) fails silently:
the search radius just never expands that far, indistinguishable from
a genuinely converged result without an external comparison. Found
while diagnosing why a $Z=0$ run's HII region seemed to plateau early
(the true cause was the search cap, not convergence).

\textbf{Fix:} added a startup warning in \code{engine\_config()}
(right after \code{stars\_props\_print}) when
\code{Stars:max\_HII\_search\_radius} exceeds the periodic box's
half-width. Guarded behind \code{IONIZATION\_FEEDBACK\_LOOP}, fires
once on rank~0, only if periodic.

\textbf{Verification:} an isolated \code{StromgrenSpherePair} copy with
the cap deliberately set above the box half-width prints the warning at
startup; build clean, \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck}/
\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass.

\subsection{2026-07-20: star/HII timestep floor reused an unrelated
parameter, and \texttt{dt\_evolution} had no floor at all}
\label{sec:t16-timestep-floor}

The timestep-safety floor for \code{dt\_HII\_safe} (guarding against a
near-zero remainder as a star approaches a scheduled rebuild) reused
\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr} -- a parameter whose actual
purpose is flooring the \emph{adaptive-cadence rebuild interval itself}
(a physics/scheduling floor), not the numerical step-size safety floor.
Meanwhile \code{dt\_evolution} (the stellar-evolution-stage timestep
criterion) had no floor of any kind, and could in principle pass
through a near-zero value as \code{star\_age\_beg\_step -> 0+}.

\textbf{Fix:} new \code{Stars:min\_star\_timestep\_Myr} (default
$10^{-4}\,$Myr, matching \code{HII\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr}'s existing
value -- see the reasoning below), in \code{struct stars\_props}
(\code{src/stars/GEAR/stars\_part.h}/\code{stars\_io.h}), applied once
in \code{stars\_compute\_timestep()}
(\code{src/stars/GEAR/stars.h}) via
\code{max(min(dt\_age, dt\_feedback), min\_star\_timestep)} -- the same
model-agnostic layer the existing age-based bounds
(\code{max\_time\_step\_young}/\code{old}) already live at, rather
than inside any one feedback model's own timestep function.
\code{feedback\_compute\_spart\_timestep} now returns the raw,
unfloored \code{min3(dt, dt\_evolution, dt\_HII\_safe)};
\code{HII\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr} keeps its original, separate role
(flooring the adaptive rebuild interval in
\code{radiation\_compute\_and\_cache\_HII\_rebuild\_interval}).

\textbf{Default value check:} at $T_i=10^4\,$K, a dense clump's
recombination timescale is already of order a few hundred years
($\sim2$--$5\times10^{-4}\,$Myr); a $10\times$ larger floor
($10^{-3}\,$Myr) would routinely clip the adaptive cadence's
fine-grained resolution precisely where it matters, so the floor was
kept at $10^{-4}\,$Myr rather than raised.

\textbf{Verification:} build clean, \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck}/
\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass.

\subsection{2026-07-24: T5's \texttt{RT\_heating\_rate} was always exactly
zero under the default (fixed-cadence) configuration}
\label{sec:t5-heating-rate-zero}

T5 (\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate}) passed a build-clean,
crash-free sanity run of the cheapest validation target
(\code{StromgrenSphereClumpSmith2021}, 174{,}824 particles), but the
ionization state it produced was wrong: near-star gas ionized briefly then
decayed to a few percent instead of holding the near-100\% equilibrium
basic Str\"omgren physics predicts (confirmed both against a
Gamma\_HI/alpha\_B(T) hand calculation and against the actual measured
temperature of ``highly ionized'' particles, which sat at the pre-ionization
background temperature).

\textbf{Root cause}, confirmed by direct instrumentation (temporary
\code{message()} prints, reverted after use): each star's mean excess
photon energy above 13.6\,eV
(\code{sp->feedback\_data.radiation.mean\_excess\_photon\_energy\_HI}, the
factor \code{RT\_heating\_rate} is built from) was computed and cached in
exactly one place,
\code{radiation\_compute\_and\_cache\_HII\_rebuild\_interval()}, whose only
call site was gated behind
\code{if (feedback\_props->HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence \&\& ...)}. Since
\code{HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence} is off by default (T4, still not
enabled) and off in every validated example, that function never ran, so
the cached energy stayed at its zero-initialized default forever --
\code{RT\_heating\_rate = Gamma\_HI * E\_excess} was therefore always
exactly zero, while \code{RT\_HI\_ionization\_rate} (Gamma\_HI, threaded
through a separate, unconditional path) was genuinely nonzero. Grackle
received a real photoionization rate with zero photoheating: photoionized
gas never warmed up, so the (strongly temperature-dependent) case-B
recombination coefficient stayed at its low-temperature (large) value,
capping the achievable ionization equilibrium at a few percent.

\textbf{Fix:} split the excess-photon-energy caching out of
\code{radiation\_compute\_and\_cache\_HII\_rebuild\_interval()} into its own
function, \code{radiation\_cache\_mean\_excess\_photon\_energy\_HI()}
(\code{radiation.c}), called unconditionally once per star per HII rebuild
pass (gated only by \code{cooling->HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate}, not by
\code{HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence}) from
\code{runner\_radiation\_feedback.c}, mirroring how
\code{dot\_N\_ion\_pix\_initial} already stays correctly populated
regardless of cadence mode (via \code{radiation\_set\_ionizing\_photon\_rate}
in \code{stellar\_evolution.c}, an independent, unconditional path).

\textbf{Verification:} build clean, \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck}/
\code{test27cellsStars -n 3 -N 3 -r 5} pass. Rerunning the same
ClumpSmith2021 sanity check after the fix: near-star ionization now holds
at $\sim$0.9--1.0 for the entire 8\,Myr run (matching the $\sim$0.9998
analytic equilibrium) instead of decaying to a few percent, and the
rate-coupled particle count no longer collapses. Feature-off
(\code{HII\_couple\_ionization\_rate=0}) behaviour is unaffected by
construction: the moved code was reachable only through the same flag, so
removing it from the old function changes nothing when the flag is off
(confirmed by rerunning the ClumpSmith2021 feature-off regression check
with the fixed binary). Full re-validation of the three STARBENCH/Hu-Smith/
clump targets against this build not yet re-run.

\textbf{Angular-scheme and resolution check:} the sanity check above used
the spherical budget (\code{nside=0}). Rerun with the angular scheme
(\code{nside=1}, 12 HEALPix pixels) at both the default 20\,M$_\odot$
resolution and GEAR's 95\,M$_\odot$ production resolution (see
\code{project\_gear\_production\_resolution\_range.md}), T5 on and off
in each case (four runs total). All four complete cleanly. Comparing the
HII region's outer radius $r_{50}$ (the largest radius at which the
radially-binned mean HII fraction is still $\geq 0.5$) over time, T5 and
flag-and-floor track each other closely at both resolutions and reach
essentially the same final size ($r_{50}\approx13\,$pc at $t=8.2\,$Myr in
all four runs; the 95\,M$_\odot$ curves are visibly noisier, an expected
consequence of $\sim$5$\times$ fewer particles). The final-time
ionized-mass/temperature contrast seen with the spherical scheme persists
under the angular scheme at both resolutions:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lrrr}
 & $r_{50}$ [pc] & ionized mass [M$_\odot$] & mean $T$ (HII$>$0.5) [K] \\
\hline
20 M$_\odot$, T5 on  & 12.9 & 2902 & 10990 \\
20 M$_\odot$, T5 off & 12.5 & 3993 &  8756 \\
95 M$_\odot$, T5 on  & 12.9 & 2246 & 12352 \\
95 M$_\odot$, T5 off & 12.9 & 4480 &  8249 \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

i.e. T5 consistently reaches about the same physical size as flag-and-floor
but with lower total ionized mass (partial rather than forced-complete
ionization within the region) and a hotter ionized-gas temperature (Grackle's
own photoheating balance, including the star's excess photon energy above
13.6\,eV, settles above the flag-and-floor's fixed $\sim10^4\,$K target
here) -- the same qualitative pattern reported above, now shown to be
robust across both the angular/spherical budget-splitting choice and
resolution, not an artifact of the one configuration first checked. At
95\,M$_\odot$ the ionized-region particle count entering the temperature
average is small (34--47 particles), so those two temperature values
carry more sampling noise than the 20\,M$_\odot$ pair.

\subsection{2026-07-28: adaptive rebuild cadence removed (Phase 4c)}
\label{sec:phase4c-removal}

\code{GEARFeedback:HII\_adaptive\_rebuild\_cadence} and
\code{HII\_rebuild\_safety\_factor}, the \code{HII\_region\_next\_rebuild\_time}
spart field and its getter, and the deadline-computation half of
\code{radiation\_compute\_and\_cache\_HII\_rebuild\_interval()} (the
function referenced by several entries above) no longer exist in the
codebase. The count-budget redesign made rebuild cadence a purely
numerical parameter (Section~\ref{sec:husmith-current},
\code{04\_validation.tex}), removing the adaptive mode's correctness
motivation and leaving only its per-star timestep-pinning cost.
\code{HII\_rebuild\_floor\_Myr} is unaffected: it floors the
photon-budget interval in fixed-cadence mode too. Section~\ref{sec:adaptive-cadence}
of \code{01\_algorithm.tex} keeps the mechanism's description as a
historical record; the entries above describing bugs in the deleted
code are kept unchanged as a record of what was fixed at the time.

\textbf{Why the Phase 4b numbers (2.51\%$\to$0.52\% STARBENCH,
7.80\%/13.75\% HuSmith2017) still apply post-4c:} those were measured
with a \emph{pre}-Phase-4c binary, running the two examples with
