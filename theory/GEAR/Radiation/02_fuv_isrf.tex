\section{FUV / ISRF: photoelectric heating and H$_2$ photodissociation}
\label{sec:fuv-isrf}

This section documents two feedback channels beyond ionizing (HII)
radiation: \textbf{photoelectric heating} and \textbf{H$_2$
photodissociation} (Lyman-Werner), both driven by a star's non-ionizing
far-UV output, an interstellar radiation field (ISRF) estimated locally
rather than assumed as a fixed background value. The channel is
implemented in \code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation\_isrf.c} and related
files.

It covers, in order: what Grackle expects at each of the two coupling
points, and how this codebase configures it
(Sections~\ref{sec:fuv-lw-grackle}--\ref{sec:fuv-two-bands}); how a
star's two band luminosities are deposited onto its gas neighbours, and
what that injected field is and is not
(Sections~\ref{sec:fuv-units}--\ref{sec:fuv-propagation}); and the
hyperbolic (Cattaneo-type flux-relaxation, M1-closure) scheme that turns that per-star
deposit into a smooth, causally-bounded radiation field
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-propagation-scheme}), including its
discretization, its numerical stabilization, and the runtime parameters
it exposes.

Statements about Grackle below are checked against the frozen,
SWIFT-compatible checkout at
\code{\textasciitilde/programs/grackle-swift/}: against its own
source, not only its online documentation, which is thin on these two
coupling points.

\subsection{H$_2$ photodissociation (Lyman-Werner) in Grackle}
\label{sec:fuv-lw-grackle}

Grackle has its own rate-coupling field for this, parallel to the
H/He ionization-rate fields already used for photoionization (the HII
photoionization theory document's algorithm section):
\code{RT\_H2\_dissociation\_rate} (\code{grackle\_types.h:74}), threaded
through \code{solve\_chemistry.c} into the Fortran rate solver
(\code{solve\_rate\_cool\_g.F}) under the internal name
\code{kdissH2I}. Requirements, from \code{doc/source/Parameters.rst}
and \code{Integration.rst} (more explicit there than the field's own
one-line docstring):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{use\_radiative\_transfer=1};
  \item \code{primordial\_chemistry} (Grackle's own name for what this
    codebase calls \code{COOLING\_GRACKLE\_MODE}) set to \textbf{2 or
    3}: i.e.\ exactly this project's modes 2/3, since a
    dissociation rate for a species (H$_2$) only means something once
    that species is tracked;
  \item units \code{1/time\_units}, the same convention as
    \code{RT\_HI\_ionization\_rate}.
\end{itemize}
Reaction \textbf{k31} in Grackle's own network is
\code{H2I + photon -> 2 HI} (\code{initialize\_rates.c:62}): the
Lyman-Werner dissociation reaction itself. In the solver it is applied
additively on top of the UV-background table value, exactly like
\code{kphHI} for photoionization:
\begin{quote}
\code{k31shield(i) = k31 + kdissH2I(i,j,k)} \quad
(\code{solve\_rate\_cool\_g.F:1407})
\end{quote}
\textbf{Self-shielding is already handled for us}: both
\code{H2\_self\_shielding} and \code{H2\_custom\_shielding} explicitly
attenuate \emph{both} the background UVB rate and the supplied
\code{RT\_H2\_dissociation\_rate} by the same factor; dense,
self-shielded gas is protected from a local LW source exactly as it is
from the background, with no extra bookkeeping needed on this
codebase's side. Nothing needs building inside Grackle for this: the
coupling point already exists and is well-formed. Everything this
document describes is upstream of it: estimating the local LW flux that
populates the field.

\subsection{Photoelectric heating in Grackle}
\label{sec:fuv-pe-grackle}

A separate, older mechanism from the RT-coupled ionization/dissociation
fields above: not a radiative-transfer field at all, but its own
dust-physics option:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{photoelectric\_heating} (int, default 0): 0 = off; 1 = a
    spatially uniform rate set directly by
    \code{photoelectric\_heating\_rate} (Tasker \& Bryan 2008, no ISRF
    dependence); 2 = `Wolfire et al.\ (1995)`, Eq.~1, with a constant
    grain photoelectric efficiency $\epsilon=0.05$ (below $2\times10^4\,$K,
    0 above), scaled by the local ISRF strength; 3 = same but with
    $\epsilon$ computed directly from Wolfire et al.\ 1995 Eq.~2
    (electron-density-dependent, needs \code{primordial\_chemistry}$>0$
    to have a meaningful electron density).
  \item \code{dust\_chemistry=1} auto-enables
    \code{photoelectric\_heating=2} (if unset) \emph{and} dust
    recombination cooling (Wolfire et al.\ 1995 Eq.~9) \emph{and}
    H$_2$-formation-on-dust, all three scaled by the same ISRF value;
    i.e.\ this one flag is Grackle's intended entry point for "turn on
    dust physics with a given ISRF," not something to enable piecemeal.
  \item \textbf{The ISRF value itself can be per-particle}, not just a
    global scalar: \code{use\_isrf\_field=1} switches from the scalar
    \code{interstellar\_radiation\_field} parameter (Habing units,
    default 1.7: Grackle's own default happens to equal the standard
    Draine 1978 field normalization, Section~\ref{sec:fuv-two-bands})
    to a per-cell/per-particle field, \code{isrf\_habing}
    (\code{grackle\_types.h:79}), same units. This is the natural
    target for a local FUV estimate, more so than routing through
    \code{RT\_heating\_rate} (which Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lw-grackle}'s
    ionization-rate machinery is built for, and which does not know
    about Wolfire et al.\ 1995's dust-grain physics at all).
\end{itemize}
Traced into the actual solver (\code{cool1d\_multi\_g.F:709-997}): the
per-cell ISRF value (either the scalar or, if
\code{use\_isrf\_field=1}, \code{isrf\_habing(i,j,k)}) is read into a
local \code{myisrf(i)}, and the heating rate is
\begin{equation}
  \Gamma_{\rm PE} = \gamma_{ha}\,\epsilon\,G_0\,n_H, \qquad
  \gamma_{ha} = 10^{-24}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}}
  \label{eq:fuv-pe-heating}
\end{equation}
(\code{rate\_functions.c}'s \code{gammah\_rate}, applied as
\code{gammaha\_eff(i) = gammaha * 0.05 * myisrf(i)} for
\code{photoelectric\_heating=2}, \code{cool1d\_multi\_g.F:972}), added
directly into \code{edot}. As with H$_2$ dissociation, nothing needs
building inside Grackle: the per-particle \code{isrf\_habing} field
already exists, and the work is again upstream of it, estimating the
local $G_0$ that fills it.

\subsection{Grackle configuration for PE heating, dust, and a
per-particle ISRF}
\label{sec:fuv-pe-concrete}

The two sections above describe what Grackle \emph{can} do. This is the
configuration that ships, gated on
\code{GEARFeedback:with\_photoelectric\_heating}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \code{metal\_cooling=1}: prerequisite for the next flag,
    already on for GEAR chemistry runs.
  \item \code{dust\_chemistry=1}: Grackle's single intended entry
    point for "turn on dust physics with a given ISRF." It bundles
    three things at once, all scaled by the same ISRF value:
    photoelectric heating (auto-setting \code{photoelectric\_heating=2}
    if unset), dust recombination cooling (Wolfire et al.\ 1995
    Eq.~9), and H$_2$-formation-on-dust (since
    \code{primordial\_chemistry}$>1$ in modes 2/3).

    \textbf{The bundle does not double-count.} Every additive physics
    term (the H$_2$-on-dust rate in \code{solve\_rate\_cool\_g.F}, the
    Wolfire et al.\ 1995 Eq.~9 dust-recombination cooling in
    \code{cool1d\_multi\_g.F}) is gated by a single \code{if} on an OR
    of the contributing flags (\code{idust}/\code{idustall} for
    H$_2$-on-dust, \code{idustall}/\code{idustrec} for recombination
    cooling): enabling more than one gating flag never adds a term
    twice, it is the same single code path either way. The bundle is
    also safer than setting the component flags individually:
    \code{cool1d\_multi\_g.F}'s \code{anydust} condition, gating the
    gas/grain heat-transfer cooling (a distinct physical channel,
    lines~$\sim$726--753), is wider than
    \code{solve\_rate\_cool\_g.F}'s H$_2$-on-dust condition, so setting
    \code{dust\_recombination\_cooling=1} alone would silently pull in
    an extra, unintended cooling channel while still needing a second
    flag for H$_2$-on-dust.\footnote{Grackle's own
    \code{Parameters.rst} claims \code{dust\_recombination\_cooling} is
    "automatically set by \code{h2\_on\_dust}$>0$ or
    \code{dust\_chemistry}$>0$"; the actual code
    (\code{initialize\_chemistry\_data.c}) only auto-sets it inside the
    \code{dust\_chemistry}$>0$ block. A documentation/code drift on
    this one point: check the code, not Grackle's own docs, before
    relying on this behaviour.}

    \textbf{Note}: \code{chemistry\_data.h2\_on\_dust} defaults to $0$
    (not a $-1$ sentinel like the other two flags), and Grackle's own
    \code{initialize\_chemistry\_data.c} silently overrides
    \code{GrackleCooling:H2\_on\_dust=0} to $1$ whenever
    \code{dust\_chemistry=1} and H$_2$ is tracked
    (\code{primordial\_chemistry}$>1$): there is no way to keep
    H$_2$-on-dust off while using the bundle with H$_2$ tracking on.
    That is the desired behaviour for this channel (Sternberg et al.\
    2014's PDR structure needs H$_2$-on-dust active), but worth knowing
    before trying to isolate it off.

    \textbf{Note}: the H$_2$-on-dust rate scales linearly with
    \code{GrackleCooling:local\_dust\_to\_gas\_ratio} -- the rate
    formula's hardcoded reference dust-to-gas constant ($0.009387$)
    exactly matches this parameter's own default, so they cancel
    correctly for solar-normalized runs. Keep this parameter at its
    default unless intentionally testing dust-abundance sensitivity.
  \item \code{photoelectric\_heating=2}: the constant-$\epsilon=0.05$
    form of Wolfire et al.\ 1995 Eq.~1, which \code{dust\_chemistry=1}
    selects automatically when the flag is left unset. The alternative,
    \code{=3}, computes $\epsilon$ from the local electron density
    (Wolfire et al.\ 1995 Eq.~2), which \code{primordial\_chemistry}$>0$
    tracking does provide; Grackle's own documentation notes that this
    electron density is "most likely underestimated at low
    temperatures," since it ignores metal and dust-grain contributions.
  \item \code{use\_isrf\_field=1}: switches Grackle from the scalar
    \code{interstellar\_radiation\_field} to reading the per-particle
    \code{isrf\_habing} array (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-pe-grackle}).
  \item \textbf{\code{isrf\_habing} is allocated and filled by this
    codebase}, one value per gas particle, in Habing units, every time
    chemistry is solved. $u_i$ (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-units}) is a
    specific (${\rm erg\,g^{-1}}$) quantity; converting it back to a
    flux-like intensity needs the local density (to recover the
    volumetric energy density $\rho_i u_i$) and a factor of $c$ (the
    standard flux~$\sim c\times$energy-density relation for a
    quasi-isotropic radiation field, matching the moment definitions
    $E_\nu=\int(I_\nu/c)\,{\rm d}\Omega$, $F_\nu=\int I_\nu\,\mathbf
    n\,{\rm d}\Omega$ used in Section~\ref{sec:fuv-p1}):
    \begin{equation}
      G_{0,i} = \frac{c\,\rho_i\,u_i}{1.6\times10^{-3}\,
        {\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}}} ,
      \label{eq:fuv-g0-conversion}
    \end{equation}
    with $u_i$ the sum of both bands' specific energy
    ($L_{\rm FUV}+L_{\rm LW}$ injected per Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject}, per
    Section~\ref{sec:fuv-two-bands}). This is the one number this
    codebase is responsible for producing; everything else in this list
    is Grackle configuration, not new code.
  \item \textbf{This is independent of H$_2$ photodissociation
    coupling} (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lw-grackle}): that needs its own
    separate switches (\code{use\_radiative\_transfer=1},
    \code{primordial\_chemistry}$\in\{2,3\}$) and its own separately
    converted quantity ($L_{\rm LW}$ alone, not $L_{\rm FUV}+L_{\rm
    LW}$, into \code{RT\_H2\_dissociation\_rate}). Turning on
    \code{use\_isrf\_field} does \emph{not} also turn on H$_2$
    dissociation coupling, or vice versa: both must be configured if
    both channels are wanted.
  \item \textbf{The coupling exists only where a real per-particle
    cooling solve happens.} \code{isrf\_habing} is filled from
    Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-g0-conversion} in the cooling module itself,
    immediately before Grackle is called. Under the \code{cooling=none}
    subgrid configuration, the equivalent lookup is a hardcoded no-op
    that always returns zero: the coupling is structurally present but
    inert there. Not silently wrong, but worth knowing before assuming
    photoelectric heating is active in every cooling configuration.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Two explicit sub-bands, read from the feedback tables}
\label{sec:fuv-two-bands}

\textbf{The two channels do not draw on the same photon band.}
Photoelectric heating draws on the classical Habing band, historically
$\sim6$--$13.6\,$eV ($912$--$2000$\,\AA): any FUV photon energetic
enough to eject an electron from a dust grain's surface, essentially
the whole non-ionizing far-UV continuum. Lyman-Werner (H$_2$
dissociation) is narrower and harder: $11.2$--$13.6\,$eV
($912$--$1108$\,\AA), the specific energy range of H$_2$'s own
electronic transitions (Lyman $B^1\Sigma_u^+$--$X^1\Sigma_g^+$ and
Werner $C^1\Pi_u$--$X^1\Sigma_g^+$ bands): a sub-band sitting at the
hard end of the Habing range, not the whole thing.

The model therefore tracks \textbf{two explicit band luminosities per
star}: $L_{\rm FUV}$ ($6$--$11.2\,$eV) and $L_{\rm LW}$
($11.2$--$13.6\,$eV). These are two more threshold-integrals of each
star's own spectrum, on exactly the machinery already producing $Q_H$
(threshold above $13.6\,$eV, the HII photoionization theory document's
blackbody-fit section) and $L_{\rm bol}$ (the full integral,
Section~\ref{sec:rp-momentum}). Both are read directly from
\texttt{pychem} (\code{\textasciitilde/programs/pychem/})
$(M,Z)$-dependent stellar tables when the table provides them; a table
lacking either band integral falls back to a $T_{\rm eff}$-driven
Planck-function band-fraction integral computed from the star's own
effective temperature and bolometric luminosity. Both routes feed the
same injection formula (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject}); which one a given
star uses depends only on what its table provides, transparently to
everything downstream.

\textbf{Why two bands rather than one normalization and a fixed
spectral shape.} The cheaper alternative is to assume a single fixed
ISRF spectral shape (Draine 1978 / Mathis et al.\ 1983) and derive both
band-integrated quantities from one overall FUV normalization by a
fixed ratio. Grackle's own default
\code{interstellar\_radiation\_field=1.7} is the Habing-unit value of
exactly the standard Draine (1978) field ($\chi=1$ Draine
$\approx1.7\,G_0$ Habing), a sign that this fixed-shape approach is
that field's own default assumption, and under the same shape
\citet{DraineBertoldi1996} give
$k_{\rm LW}\approx1.1\times10^{-10}\,\chi\,{\rm s^{-1}}$ (commonly
quoted across the PDR literature; the coefficient is not independently
re-derived from the primary text here). That would work, but it borrows
a spectral shape calibrated to the solar neighbourhood's mixed stellar
population, which does not generally match a single star or young
cluster's actual SED hardness. Tracking the two bands explicitly
instead:
\begin{itemize}
  \item ties the FUV/LW split to each source's actual SED (which varies
    with stellar mass and temperature) instead of an imported,
    population-averaged ISM shape;
  \item lets the H$_2$ dissociation rate be built from a standard
    effective cross-section $\sigma_{\rm LW}$ (a narrow, well-defined
    atomic-physics quantity) rather than routed through
    \citeauthor{DraineBertoldi1996}'s shape-calibrated $k_{\rm
    LW}(\chi)$ fit: one fewer inherited approximation in the chain;
  \item makes photoelectric heating a direct band sum
    ($G_0\propto L_{\rm FUV}+L_{\rm LW}$, since the Habing band is
    defined over the same $\sim6$--$13.6\,$eV range), converted to
    Habing units via the standard normalization
    ($1\,{\rm Habing}=1.6\times10^{-3}\,{\rm erg\,s^{-1}\,cm^{-2}}$
    integrated over that range), with no shape assumption needed once
    the band integral itself is correct.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Units convention: $u$ and $\mathbf F$ are specific
(per-mass) fields}
\label{sec:fuv-units}

Adopted throughout this document: $u_i\equiv E_i/m_i$ is the specific
FUV/LW-band radiation energy carried by particle $i$ (${\rm
erg\,g^{-1}}$), and $\mathbf F_i$ its associated specific flux moment
(${\rm erg\,cm\,g^{-1}\,s^{-1}}$). This matches this codebase's own
naming convention: SPH particles already carry a field literally called
\code{u} for specific internal energy (e.g.\
\code{src/hydro/SPHENIX/hydro\_iact.h}'s \code{pi->u}), never a
volumetric energy density; and it is what makes the injection increment
$u_{{\rm inject},i}/m_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject}) dimensionally
consistent, energy divided by mass giving specific energy.

The moment equations of Section~\ref{sec:fuv-p1}
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}--\ref{eq:fuv-p1-first}) are written in the
standard (volumetric) RTE convention, so that they derive cleanly from
first principles and match $E_\nu$/$\mathbf F_\nu$ in \code{rt/GEAR}'s
own notation. Converting to the specific convention is the standard
Lagrangian-frame step already implicit throughout SPH: the same reason
SPH's own specific internal energy equation has the same \emph{form} as
the Eulerian energy-density equation, with the advection term absorbed
into the material derivative because particle mass is fixed, neither
created nor destroyed. Dividing the volumetric equations through by
$\rho$ and evaluating in the frame comoving with each particle's fixed
mass leaves Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}--\ref{eq:fuv-p1-first}
\textbf{structurally unchanged}, with $u$, $\mathbf F$, $\mathbf P$ now
read as specific quantities throughout. The screening rate
$c\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho$ already carries an explicit $\rho$ and needs
no further conversion.

\subsection{Injection: kernel deposit and receiver-side extinction}
\label{sec:fuv-injection}

A star deposits its two band luminosities onto its SPH-kernel gas
neighbours using the same normalized, mass-weighted kernel deposit
already implemented for radiation-pressure momentum injection
(\code{radiation\_iact.h}, \code{weight = mj * wi * si\_inv\_weight}
with \code{si\_inv\_weight} the star's own accumulated
$1/\sum_k m_k W_{\star k}$ normalisation, so weights sum to 1 over the
star's kernel). The energy deposited in particle $i$ over a step is,
per band,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
  u_{{\rm inject},i}^{\rm (FUV)} &= \Delta t\,\zeta_{{\rm FUV},i}
    \sum_\star w_{i\star}\,L_{\rm FUV,\star} , \qquad
  u_{{\rm inject},i}^{\rm (LW)} = \Delta t\,\zeta_{{\rm LW},i}
    \sum_\star w_{i\star}\,L_{\rm LW,\star} , \\
  w_{i\star} &= \frac{m_i\,W_{i\star}}{\sum_k m_k\,W_{\star k}} ,
\end{aligned}
  \label{eq:fuv-inject}
\end{equation}
summing over any star $\star$ whose kernel reaches particle $i$
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-two-bands}'s two band luminosities). Dividing by
$m_i$ gives a specific-energy increment, consistent with
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-units}.

\textbf{Receiver-side extinction.} $\zeta_{{\rm FUV},i},\zeta_{{\rm
LW},i}\in(0,1]$ is a \emph{local}, per-particle, band-specific dust
attenuation applied at the receiving end, not at the source and not
along the path from star to particle:
\begin{equation}
  \zeta_i = \exp\!\big(-\kappa_{\rm eff}\,\Sigma_{{\rm gas},i}\big) ,
  \label{eq:fuv-extinction}
\end{equation}
with $\Sigma_{{\rm gas},i}$ particle $i$'s own local gas column density
estimate and $\kappa_{\rm eff}$ built from the same per-band dust mass
opacity fit as $\kappa_{\rm FUV}(Z)$/$\kappa_{\rm LW}(Z)$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-lambda-def}), evaluated at $i$'s own local
metallicity.

This is a different physical effect from the propagation-decay opacity
$\kappa_{\rm FUV}/\kappa_{\rm LW}$ used in
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-propagation-scheme}: that opacity governs how the
field is removed \emph{as it propagates} through the diffuse medium
between sources and receivers, whereas $\zeta_i$ represents dust
immediately local to the receiving particle itself (e.g.\ a receiver
sitting inside its own dense clump), attenuating what that particle
picks up regardless of how the field got there. Extinction is applied
at injection, before any propagation step runs, and applies unchanged
when propagation is off (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-injection-off}).

\subsection{Injection alone is not a smooth ISRF}
\label{sec:fuv-propagation}

The deposit of Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject} reuses the same
density-loop-style gather/scatter already used for the HII budget (the
HII photoionization theory document's algorithm section) and radiation
pressure (Section~\ref{sec:radiation-pressure}). \textbf{That alone is
not a smooth ISRF}: injection only reaches particles within the star's
kernel radius $h_\star$, dropping sharply to zero outside it,
resolution-dependent (a smaller $h_\star$ at higher resolution shrinks
the field's reach, which is unphysical: the real radiation field does
not know about the simulation's resolution), and patchy between sources
rather than the smoothly-varying background a real, many-source,
dust-attenuated ISRF produces.

Closing that gap needs a propagation step. Propagating at the true
speed of light, as a full free-streaming or M1 solve would, imposes a
$\text{CFL}\sim h/c$ constraint far below any hydro or cooling
timescale of interest, and is not affordable here. The scheme of
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-propagation-scheme} instead keeps a genuinely
time-dependent, hyperbolic system while deliberately reducing its
propagation speed: a reduced-speed-of-light approximation, honestly
named as one, whose CFL condition is affordable by construction.

\subsection{Hyperbolic (M1-relaxation) propagation}
\label{sec:fuv-propagation-scheme}

Everything in this subsection describes the field with
\code{GEARFeedback:LW\_FUV\_propagation} enabled. With it off, which is
the default, none of this machinery runs and the field reduces to the
instantaneous injected snapshot of
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-injection-off}.

\subsubsection{Deriving a hyperbolic system from the RTE: the M1
closure and its flux limiter}
\label{sec:fuv-p1}

Take the first two angular moments of the radiative transfer equation
(matching the notation of this codebase's own \code{rt/GEAR} module,
\code{theory/RadiativeTransfer/GEARRT/2-rt-equations.tex}):
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf F
    &= -c\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho\, u + S ,
  \label{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth} \\
  \frac{\partial \mathbf F}{\partial t} + c^2\,\nabla\cdot\mathbf P
    &= -c\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho\, \mathbf F .
  \label{eq:fuv-p1-first}
\end{align}
$\mathbf P$ (the radiation pressure tensor) is unclosed: it depends on
the second angular moment of the specific intensity, which these two
equations alone do not determine.

\textbf{The isotropic (P1) closure, for reference.} The \textbf{Eddington
closure},
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf P = \frac{u}{3}\,\mathbf I ,
  \label{eq:fuv-eddington-closure}
\end{equation}
is exact only when the local radiation field is close to isotropic
(frequent scattering/absorption randomising photon direction locally).
That closure, combined with
Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}--\ref{eq:fuv-p1-first}, is the \textbf{P1
approximation}. It is not what is implemented here; it is quoted because
the implemented closure reduces to it exactly in the isotropic limit, and
because most of the discussion below (the relaxation structure, the CFL,
the discretization) is identical either way.

\textbf{The implemented closure is M1.} The M1 closure
\citep{Levermore1984}, also implemented in this exact SWIFT fork's own
\code{rt/GEAR} and \code{rt/SPHM1RT} modules
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-precedent}), makes the Eddington tensor a function
of the local \textbf{reduced flux} $f$:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf P &= \mathbf D(f)\,u , \qquad
  \mathbf D(f) = \frac{1-\chi}{2}\,\mathbf I + \frac{3\chi-1}{2}\,
    \hat{\mathbf n}\otimes\hat{\mathbf n} , \qquad
  \hat{\mathbf n}=\frac{\mathbf F}{|\mathbf F|} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-m1-closure} \\
  \chi(f) &= \frac{3+4f^2}{5+2\sqrt{4-3f^2}} , \qquad
  f = \min\!\left(\frac{|\mathbf F|}{c_{\rm hyp}\,u},\,1\right)
    \ \ (u>0) , \qquad f = 0 \ \ (u\le0) .
  \label{eq:fuv-m1-chi}
\end{align}
$\chi(0)=1/3$ recovers Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-eddington-closure} exactly
($\mathbf D=\mathbf I/3$: correct isotropic/diffusive behaviour), and
$\chi(1)=1$ gives $\mathbf D=\hat{\mathbf n}\otimes\hat{\mathbf n}$
(correct free-streaming behaviour: pure advection along
$\hat{\mathbf n}$, no unphysical sideways spreading). The reduced flux is
built with the \emph{reduced} light speed $c_{\rm hyp}$ of
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}, not the true $c$ --- see
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-m1} for why that is load-bearing rather than
cosmetic. $\mathbf D$, $\chi$ and $f$ are transient per-particle formula
evaluations, recomputed wherever they are needed and never stored: the
closure upgrade adds no particle state and no communicated field.

The two clamps in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi} are not defensive decoration.
$f>1$ is genuinely reachable in the implemented staggered update, because
the closure is evaluated on an $\mathbf F$ that was limited against the
\emph{previous} step's $u$ while $u$ has since been updated
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}); without the $\min(\cdot,1)$, $4-3f^2$
goes negative and $\chi$ is NaN. The $u\le0$ branch covers the bounded
undershoots the discretization leaves behind
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-dissipation}), where no direction can be defined.
$\hat{\mathbf n}$ itself is computed as $\mathbf F/|\mathbf F|$ only when
$|\mathbf F|>0$ and set to $\mathbf 0$ otherwise; $\mathbf F=\mathbf 0$ is
every particle's initial condition and its permanent far-field state, so
this is the common case, not a corner case.

\textbf{The flux limiter.} The closure alone is not sufficient. Once per
step, per particle, per band, the tracked flux is rescaled onto the M1
admissible set:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf F \;\leftarrow\; \mathbf F\,
    \min\!\left(1,\ \frac{c_{\rm hyp}\,u}{|\mathbf F|}\right)
    \quad (u>0) , \qquad
  \mathbf F \;\leftarrow\; \mathbf 0 \quad (u\le0) .
  \label{eq:fuv-flux-limiter}
\end{equation}
This enforces $|\mathbf F|\le c_{\rm hyp}u$, the statement that radiation
energy cannot stream faster than the (reduced) speed of light, which the
moment equations do not enforce on their own: they are two coupled linear
transport equations, and nothing in them forbids a state with a large
$\mathbf F$ and a vanishing $u$. That state is exactly the P1 failure
this scheme was upgraded to remove --- a front that carries flux with
almost nothing behind it, so that any numerical ripple exceeds the field
itself and its sign fails. With Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter} in force,
the field behind a front is bounded below by $|\mathbf F|/c_{\rm hyp}$ by
construction, at every $h/\lambda$, rather than by a ratio that vanishes
with the opacity. It is the limiter, not the Eddington tensor, that
removes the failure mode; the tensor is what makes the resulting
directional transport physically correct rather than merely bounded.
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter} conserves $\sum_i m_i u_i$ exactly (it
touches only $\mathbf F$); the flux moment is deliberately not a conserved
budget here. The identical limiter, in the identical place, is what
\code{rt/SPHM1RT} applies to its own $(E,\mathbf F)$ pair
(\code{rt\_unphysical.h}: rescale by $c_{\rm red}E/|\mathbf F|$ when
$|\mathbf F|>c_{\rm red}E$, zero the flux when $E\le0$).

\textbf{Substituting the closure.} Putting
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-closure} into Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-first},
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial \mathbf F}{\partial t}
    + c^2\,\nabla\cdot\big(\mathbf D(f)\,u\big)
    = -\frac{\mathbf F}{\tau_{\rm phys}} , \qquad
  \tau_{\rm phys} = \frac{1}{c\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-tau-phys}
\end{equation}
which at $f\to0$ reduces to the P1 form
$\partial_t\mathbf F + (c^2/3)\nabla u = -\mathbf F/\tau_{\rm phys}$.
This is \textbf{exactly} a Cattaneo-type flux-relaxation equation. A
sibling SWIFT checkout maintained in the same research group already
integrates the identical structure for hyperbolic metal diffusion
(\code{src/chemistry/GEAR\_FVPM\_DIFFUSION/hyperbolic/}, documented in
\code{theory/ParabolicDiffusion/GEAR\_FVPM\_Diffusion\_Theory.tex}):
\code{dflux/dt + K/tau*grad Z = -flux/tau}, integrated with the exact
analytic decay $\exp(-\Delta t/\tau)$ for the relaxation source term.
Matching Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-tau-phys}, in its isotropic ($f\to0$) limit, to
that form identifies
\begin{equation}
  D_{\rm phys} = c^2\,\tau_{\rm phys}/3 = \frac{c}{3\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho}
   = \frac{c\,\lambda}{3} ,
  \qquad
  \tau_{\rm phys} = \frac{\lambda}{c} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-D-phys}
\end{equation}
the familiar radiative diffusion coefficient and photon mean-free-time:
they fall out of the derivation rather than being asserted
independently. The hyperbolic signal speed of this exact system is
\begin{equation}
  c_{\rm hyp,phys} = \sqrt{D_{\rm phys}/\tau_{\rm phys}} = \frac{c}{\sqrt3} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-chyp-phys}
\end{equation}
a \textbf{universal constant close to the speed of light}, independent
of opacity, and the free-streaming ($f\to1$) characteristic speed is $c$
itself. \textbf{This is the catch}: taken at face value, the rigorously
RT-derived moment system has exactly the same expensive
$\Delta t\sim h/c$ CFL that motivates reduced-speed-of-light
approximations in full M1 codes (\code{rt/GEAR}'s own
\code{reduced\_speed\_of\_light} parameter,
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-precedent}). The moment closure gives a real
derivation, not by itself a cheap one.

\subsubsection{Tying $\lambda$ to physics, $c_{\rm hyp}$ to
affordability: deriving $D$ and $\tau$}
\label{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}

\textbf{The screening length.} The physical lengthscale over which dust
removes the field is
\begin{equation}
  \lambda = \frac{1}{\kappa_{\rm FUV}(Z)\,\rho} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-lambda-def}
\end{equation}
the same "how far before dust absorbs it" quantity already entering
$\kappa_{\rm IR}\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ in the radiation-pressure term
(Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-tauIR}, Section~\ref{sec:rp-sobolev}): not a new
physical input, but an ingredient already computed elsewhere in this
codebase, with $\kappa_{\rm FUV}=2000\,(Z/Z_\odot)\,{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}$
from the same \citet{Hopkins2020} fit as $\kappa_{\rm IR}$
(Section~\ref{sec:radiation-pressure}). It is called a \emph{screening}
length because it is the scale over which the field's own steady state
is cut off exponentially, in both closure limits: as $e^{-r/\lambda}/r^2$
when the field streams (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-freestream}) and as
$e^{-\sqrt3 r/\lambda}/r$ when it diffuses
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-diffusion}, a screened Poisson / modified
Helmholtz steady state whose point-source Green's function is the Yukawa
form). Geometric dilution multiplied by an exponential cutoff on the
scale $\lambda$ is the shape a real photon field has; which power of $r$
and which factor in the exponent apply is what the closure decides.

\textbf{The reduced-speed-of-light substitution.} Rather than using the
true, expensive $(D_{\rm phys},\tau_{\rm phys},c_{\rm hyp,phys})$ of
Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-D-phys}--\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-phys}, the moment system is
solved with the true light speed replaced by a reduced one,
$c\to c_{\rm hyp}$, \emph{everywhere it appears} --- in the absorption
rate, in the pressure-term coefficient, and in the reduced flux $f$ of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi} --- with the source term rescaled by
$c_{\rm hyp}/c$ to keep the field's amplitude right. $c_{\rm hyp}$ is
this scheme's reduced light speed $c_{\rm M}$ in the usual M1 sense; it
is written $c_{\rm hyp}$ throughout this document and carried in the code
as \code{feedback\_part\_data.c\_hyp}. The implemented system is
therefore
\begin{align}
  \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} + \nabla\cdot\mathbf F
    &= -\frac{u}{\tau} + \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c}\,S ,
  \label{eq:fuv-rsol-zeroth} \\
  \frac{\partial \mathbf F}{\partial t}
    + c_{\rm hyp}^2\,\nabla\cdot\big(\mathbf D(f)\,u\big)
    &= -\frac{\mathbf F}{\tau} , \qquad
  \tau = \frac{1}{c_{\rm hyp}\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho}
       = \frac{\lambda}{c_{\rm hyp}} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-lambda-chyp-solve}
\end{align}
together with the flux limiter (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter}), which is
also written against $c_{\rm hyp}$. The corresponding effective
diffusion coefficient, in the isotropic limit, is
$D=c_{\rm hyp}^2\tau/3=c_{\rm hyp}\lambda/3$: not an independent free
choice, but a consequence of $\lambda$ (fixed by dust opacity) and
$c_{\rm hyp}$ (picked for affordability).

\textbf{Why the substitution is applied uniformly, and not tuned.} An
alternative, used by this scheme's earlier P1 form, is to treat $D$ and
$\tau$ as two free knobs and pick them so that one chosen steady state
comes out exactly right: taking $\tau=\lambda/c_{\rm hyp}$,
$D=c_{\rm hyp}\lambda$ (i.e.\ a pressure coefficient $c_{\rm hyp}^2$ with
\emph{no} $1/3$, the $1/3$ having been absorbed into the tuning) and a
source rescale $3c_{\rm hyp}/c$ makes the P1 diffusive steady state a
Yukawa profile with screening length exactly $\lambda$. That tuning is
self-consistent only for P1, and only for that one limit. Under M1 the
scheme has \emph{two} limits it must reproduce, free streaming and
diffusion (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-steady-limits}), and the tuned constants
make them mutually inconsistent by a factor $\sqrt3$: the tuning buys an
exact screening length in one limit by putting the other one wrong. The
uniform substitution above makes \emph{both} limits come out exact
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-steady-limits} works them through), at the price of
a diffusive screening length $\lambda/\sqrt3$ rather than $\lambda$ ---
which is not an error, but the correct P1/M1 diffusion result, the same
$\sqrt3$ that appears in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-phys}. The only cost the
substitution actually carries is the slower transient of a reduced light
speed, which this scheme accepted the moment it chose a finite
$c_{\rm hyp}$ at all.

\textbf{Two changes, one decision, and no cancellation between them.}
Relative to the tuned P1 constants, exactly two things moved: the
pressure-term coefficient lost its factor $3$ (the $1/3$ now sits inside
$\mathbf D$, where the closure puts it), and the source rescale went from
$3c_{\rm hyp}/c$ to $c_{\rm hyp}/c$. These are \emph{not} a cancelling
pair: the first sets the screening length (hence the $\lambda\to
\lambda/\sqrt3$ above), the second sets the amplitude (which drops by a
factor $3$, Section~\ref{sec:fuv-steady-limits}). They are matched only
in both following from the single decision to substitute $c\to c_{\rm
hyp}$ uniformly. Note also what did \emph{not} change: $\tau$ is
$\lambda/c_{\rm hyp}$ under both parametrisations, which is why the
exact-relaxation integrator and its $a=c_{\rm hyp}\kappa\rho\,\Delta t$
argument (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}) are untouched by the closure
upgrade.

\textbf{$c_{\rm hyp}$ is emergent, not a chosen target speed.} No new
\code{\_timestep()} criterion is introduced for this field: instead of
deriving $\Delta t$ from a chosen $c_{\rm hyp}$, $c_{\rm hyp}$ is
derived, per particle and per step, from the particle's own
already-decided hydro/cooling timestep $\Delta t_i$ and smoothing
length $h_i$,
\begin{equation}
  c_{{\rm hyp},i} = \min\!\left(C_{\rm hyp}\,\frac{h_i}{\Delta t_i},\; c\right) ,
  \label{eq:fuv-chyp-choice}
\end{equation}
clamped at the true speed of light. $C_{\rm hyp}$
(\code{GEARFeedback:LW\_FUV\_c\_hyp\_margin}, default $0.5$, valid range
$(0,1.7]$) is the single free, dimensionless affordability/stability
dial this scheme exposes; the upper end of its range is set by the
explicit-stability requirement of the staggered exact-relaxation scheme
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}), not by a target physical speed.

\textbf{Why not use $D_{\rm phys}$/$\tau_{\rm phys}$ directly.} The
diffusive regime those coefficients assume ($\lambda_{\rm mfp}\ll h$,
many scatterings per resolution element) is not robustly satisfied at
this project's resolutions. Checked against a snapshot of the
\texttt{ClumpSmith2021} example
(\code{examples/SubgridTests/SubgridRadiation/ClumpSmith2021}, a
reproduction of \citet{Smith2021}'s Figure~2: four co-located ionizing
sources in a uniform ambient background next to a dense, resolved
clump, used throughout this document as a two-phase, ambient/clump
density-contrast regression check), $\lambda$ at solar metallicity
comes out to $\approx0.75\,$pc in the ambient gas
($n_H\approx130\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$, $h\approx2.3\,$pc) and
$\approx0.008\,$pc in the clump
($n_H\approx1.25\times10^4\,{\rm cm^{-3}}$, $h\approx0.5\,$pc): in
\emph{both} regions $\lambda<h$, including in the diffuse gas.
The reduced-speed-of-light choice of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-lambda-chyp-solve} is therefore the robust one, not a
shortcut: it keeps the system hyperbolic and lets the closure decide
locally how diffusive to be, instead of assuming a diffusive regime that
this project's resolutions do not deliver.

\textbf{$\tau$ in the diffuse ISM is comparable to an OB star's
lifetime.} Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-lambda-chyp-solve} gives $\tau=\lambda/c_{\rm
hyp}$; at $C_{\rm hyp}=0.5$ and the diffuse-phase gas properties this
project targets ($h\sim9\,$pc, $\lambda_{\rm FUV}\sim3.2\,$kpc at
$Z=10^{-2}Z_\odot$), $\tau$ depends on the gas timestep alone, since
$c_{{\rm hyp},i}=C_{\rm hyp}h_i/\Delta t_i$:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
$\Delta t$ & $c_{\rm hyp}$ & $\tau=\lambda/c_{\rm hyp}$ \\
\hline
$10^4\,$yr & $4.4\times10^3\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ & $0.7\,$Myr \\
$10^5\,$yr (typical WNM CFL step) & $440\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ & $7\,$Myr \\
$10^6\,$yr & $44\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ & $70\,$Myr \\
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
An OB star's lifetime is 3-30~Myr, so across this range $\tau$ is
comparable to, or longer than, the star's entire active life: a young
star's field in diffuse gas may spend most of its life inside the
transient shell of Section~\ref{sec:fuv-p1}, never reaching the steady
plateau at all. This is a direct, unavoidable consequence of
the reduced-speed-of-light closure (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-choice}), not a
numerical defect: at the true speed of light $\tau=\lambda/c\sim10\,$kyr
and the shell would be gone long before any cooling step could notice
it.

\subsubsection{The two steady-state limits, recovered exactly}
\label{sec:fuv-steady-limits}

This subsection works through the two analytic limits the constants of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-lambda-chyp-solve} must reproduce, in full, because they
are what the Tier~1 regression check
(\code{ISRFHyperbolicPropagation}/\code{isrf\_hyperbolic\_propagation\_check.py})
ultimately validates against, and because the previous, tuned P1
constants got exactly one of them right. Setting: a static, uniform
medium of constant $\lambda$, a point source of band luminosity $L$ at
the origin ($S=L\,\delta^3(\mathbf r)$ in
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-rsol-zeroth}), spherical symmetry, steady state
($\partial_t=0$). Writing $1/\tau=c_{\rm hyp}/\lambda$, the two moment
equations reduce to
\begin{align}
  \nabla\cdot\mathbf F &= -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{\lambda}\,u
     + \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c}\,L\,\delta^3(\mathbf r) ,
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-zeroth} \\
  c_{\rm hyp}^2\,\nabla\cdot\big(\mathbf D\,u\big)
    &= -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{\lambda}\,\mathbf F .
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-first}
\end{align}

\textbf{Limit 1: free streaming ($f=1$, $\chi=1$,
$\mathbf D=\hat{\mathbf n}\otimes\hat{\mathbf n}$).} Take
$\hat{\mathbf n}=\hat{\mathbf r}$ and $\mathbf F=c_{\rm hyp}u\,
\hat{\mathbf r}$, which is the flux limiter
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter}) active at its boundary. Then
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-zeroth} reads, away from the origin,
\begin{equation}
  \frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{r^2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\big(r^2 u\big)
    = -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{\lambda}u
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\big(r^2u\big) = -\frac{r^2u}{\lambda}
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  r^2 u = A\,e^{-r/\lambda} .
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-fs-ode}
\end{equation}
The first-moment equation must give the \emph{same} answer, or the
solution is not a solution of the system. For a purely radial pressure
tensor $\mathbf P=u\,\hat{\mathbf r}\otimes\hat{\mathbf r}$ the spherical
divergence is
$(\nabla\cdot\mathbf P)_r={\rm d}u/{\rm d}r + 2u/r$ (the transverse
components of $\mathbf P$ vanish at $\chi=1$, which is exactly why the
$-(P_{\theta\theta}+P_{\phi\phi})/r$ term drops out), so
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-first} becomes
\begin{equation}
  c_{\rm hyp}^2\left(\frac{{\rm d}u}{{\rm d}r}+\frac{2u}{r}\right)
    = -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{\lambda}\,c_{\rm hyp}u
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \frac{1}{r^2}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}r}\big(r^2u\big)
    = -\frac{u}{\lambda} ,
\end{equation}
identical to Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-fs-ode}. The two moments are
consistent, and $f=1$ is self-consistent throughout
($|\mathbf F|/(c_{\rm hyp}u)=1$ by construction). The amplitude follows
from integrating Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-zeroth} over a small sphere,
where absorption is negligible:
$4\pi r^2|\mathbf F| \to (c_{\rm hyp}/c)L$, and with
$|\mathbf F|=c_{\rm hyp}u$ this gives $A=L/(4\pi c)$. Hence
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;u(r) = \frac{L}{4\pi c\,r^2}\,e^{-r/\lambda}\;}
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-freestream}
\end{equation}
--- geometric $1/r^2$ dilution times pure dust extinction over the
\emph{physical} mean free path $\lambda$, which is the exact answer for a
point source in a purely absorbing (non-scattering) medium, and the
correct $1/c$ amplitude of the true, unreduced light speed.
$c_{\rm hyp}$ has cancelled out of both the profile and the amplitude:
the reduced light speed changes how long the field takes to get there,
not where it ends up. This is the single most important property the
$c_{\rm hyp}/c$ source rescale buys; with the previous $3c_{\rm hyp}/c$
rescale the amplitude came out three times too large here.

\textbf{Limit 2: diffusion (the isotropic branch, $f\to0$,
$\mathbf D=\mathbf I/3$).} Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-first} gives
\begin{equation}
  \frac{c_{\rm hyp}^2}{3}\nabla u = -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{\lambda}\mathbf F
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \mathbf F = -\frac{c_{\rm hyp}\lambda}{3}\,\nabla u ,
\end{equation}
Fick's law with the effective diffusion coefficient
$D=c_{\rm hyp}\lambda/3$ already identified in
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}. Substituting into
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-zeroth} and dividing through by $c_{\rm hyp}$:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda}{3}\,\nabla^2 u = \frac{u}{\lambda}
    - \frac{L}{c}\,\delta^3(\mathbf r)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \nabla^2 u - \frac{3}{\lambda^2}\,u
    = -\frac{3L}{\lambda c}\,\delta^3(\mathbf r) ,
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-helmholtz}
\end{equation}
a screened Poisson (modified Helmholtz) equation with inverse screening
length $\sqrt3/\lambda$. Its Green's function
($\nabla^2-k^2$ against $-A\delta^3$ gives $A\,e^{-kr}/4\pi r$) yields
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;u(r) = \frac{3L}{4\pi c\,\lambda\,r}\,e^{-\sqrt3\,r/\lambda}
    = \frac{3\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho\,L}{4\pi c\,r}
      \,e^{-\sqrt3\,r/\lambda}\;}
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-diffusion}
\end{equation}
again with $c_{\rm hyp}$ cancelling exactly. The screening length of the
\emph{diffusive} field is $\lambda/\sqrt3$, not $\lambda$: the same
$\sqrt3$ as in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-phys}, and the textbook P1 result.
The earlier, tuned P1 constants produced $\lambda$ here instead, by
construction --- correct-looking, but only because the tuning had been
chosen to make it so, and at the price of Limit~1's amplitude.

\textbf{Which branch a given configuration actually sits on, and why
both matter.} These are not "the same field in two regimes of one
parameter"; they are two different closures of the same constants, and
$f$ decides between them. Substituting
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-diffusion} back into the definition of $f$ gives
$f=1/\sqrt3+\lambda/(3r)$, i.e.\ $f\to0.577$, \emph{not} $f\to0$: the
isotropic branch is not self-consistent for a single point source. That
is not a defect of the algebra but the physical content of the closure
upgrade --- a purely absorbing medium never isotropizes a point source's
field (there is no scattering term to do it), so the field around one
star streams, and M1 selects Limit~1 there while P1 was structurally
unable to. The isotropic branch is the right one where the field really
is near-isotropic: a diffuse background built from many sources in many
directions, which is precisely this module's production use case. Both
limits are therefore live, and both must come out exact --- which is why
the uniform $c\to c_{\rm hyp}$ substitution was chosen over a tuning that
can only serve one of them (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}).

\textbf{A closure-independent amplitude identity.} Integrating
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-zeroth} over the whole (periodic) domain kills the
divergence term exactly, leaving
\begin{equation}
  \int u\,{\rm d}V = \tau\,\frac{c_{\rm hyp}}{c}\,L
    = \frac{\lambda\,L}{c} ,
  \qquad\text{discretely}\qquad
  \sum_i m_i u_i = \frac{\lambda}{c}\sum_j w_j\,L\,\zeta_j ,
  \label{eq:fuv-steady-amplitude}
\end{equation}
with $w_j$, $\zeta_j$ the injection weights and receiver-side extinction
of Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject}. Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-amplitude} is written
for a single shared $\lambda$; dividing each term by particle $i$'s own
$\lambda_i$ instead generalizes it to the per-particle form
$\sum_i m_i u_i/\lambda_i = (1/c)\sum_j w_j\,L\,\zeta_j$, which is what
\code{isrf\_hyperbolic\_propagation\_check.py} actually computes (each
particle carries its own metallicity- and density-dependent $\lambda_i$).
This holds for \emph{any} closure --- it uses
only that $\mathbf D$ never enters the zeroth moment --- and it holds
exactly in the discrete scheme too, because the divergence operator
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-div}) is built as a mirrored credit/debit pair and
so satisfies $\sum_i m_i(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_i=0$ identically. It is a
first-principles check on the total field amplitude with no fit and no
profile shape involved, and Tier~1 gates on it directly. Under the
previous, tuned constants the same identity read $3\lambda L/c$; the
factor $3$ drop is the source rescale's whole effect, and is independent
of the $\sqrt3$ that the pressure-term change produces in
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-steady-diffusion}.

\subsubsection{The hyperbolic CFL condition}
\label{sec:fuv-cfl}

The hyperbolic CFL condition for this system reads
\begin{equation}
  \Delta t \le C_{\rm CFL}\,\frac{h}{c_{\rm hyp}} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-hyperbolic-cfl}
\end{equation}
\textbf{linear in $h$}, like ordinary hydrodynamics and unlike a
parabolic diffusion bound's $h^2/D$.

Because $c_{\rm hyp}$ is emergent rather than chosen
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-choice}), this is not a bound to check each step:
it is the defining relation for $c_{\rm hyp}$. Setting $C_{\rm
hyp}\equiv C_{\rm CFL}$, the generic radiation-system coefficient of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-hyperbolic-cfl}, and building $c_{{\rm hyp},i}=C_{\rm
hyp}\,h_i/\Delta t_i$ from the particle's own already-decided $\Delta
t_i$, makes Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-hyperbolic-cfl} hold as an equality by
construction, before the speed-of-light clamp, for every particle,
every step: no separate criterion is ever evaluated and there is
nothing that can be violated.

$C_{\rm hyp}$ is \code{GEARFeedback:LW\_FUV\_c\_hyp\_margin}, an
independently-tunable dial. It is related to \code{SPH:CFL\_condition}
only in spirit (both are Courant-type stability dials, and $c_{\rm
hyp}$ is built from $\Delta t_i$, which the hydro step already set),
and is never inherited from it. The only real bound that remains
anywhere in this scheme is the joint $(\alpha_{\max},C_{\rm hyp})$
stability condition the artificial dissipation term needs
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-stability}).

\textbf{The speed-of-light clamp.} When a particle's own timestep is
short enough relative to its smoothing length that
$C_{\rm hyp}\,h_i/\Delta t_i$ would exceed $c$ (an unusually fine hydro
step, e.g.\ near a supernova or an actively accreting sink), $c_{{\rm
hyp},i}$ is capped at $c$ instead. This can only make the update more
conservative, never less: the clamped speed gives an effective Courant
number $c_{{\rm hyp},i}\Delta t_i/h_i<C_{\rm hyp}$, strictly below the
nominal target for that step, so the clamp cannot destabilize the
update.

\subsubsection{Divergence and gradient discretization, and the
staggered update}
\label{sec:fuv-operators}

The state carried per particle, per band, is $u_i$ (scalar) and
$\mathbf F_i$ (3-vector). $\nabla\cdot\mathbf F$ and $\nabla u$ are
each built directly from a plain pairwise SPH kernel-gradient operator
using $\nabla W_{ij}$ itself, with no matrix (FVPM geometry)
correction, no reconstruction of left/right interface states, and no
Riemann flux.

\textbf{Divergence (density loop).} A shared-coefficient, double-sum
estimator:
\begin{equation}
  \Phi_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf F_i\cdot\mathbf{dx}}{\rho_i}\,w_{i,dr}\,r_{ij}^{-1}
            + \frac{\mathbf F_j\cdot\mathbf{dx}}{\rho_j}\,w_{j,dr}\,r_{ij}^{-1},
  \qquad
  (\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_i \mathrel{+}= m_j\,\Phi_{ij}, \qquad
  (\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_j \mathrel{+}= -m_i\,\Phi_{ij},
  \label{eq:fuv-impl-div}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{dx}=\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j$ and $w_{i,dr}\,r_{ij}^{-1}\,
\mathbf{dx}=\nabla_i W_{ij}$ (particle $i$'s own kernel gradient at its
own smoothing length). A single shared scalar $\Phi_{ij}$, built from
\emph{both} particles' own kernel-gradient terms, credited to one side
and debited from the other with mirrored mass and sign: exactly
conserving under transport alone for any $h_i\ne h_j$, $\rho_i\ne
\rho_j$, provided both particles are active this step. An
active/inactive pair leaves one side undebited until its own next
active step, the pre-existing, non-sign-correlated cross-bin asymmetry
already accepted for this class of pairwise operator.

\textbf{Pressure-tensor divergence (gradient loop).} The term the flux
equation actually needs is $\nabla\cdot\mathbf P=\nabla\cdot(\mathbf D
u)$, not $\nabla u$; under the isotropic closure the two differ only by
the constant $1/3$, which is why this accumulator is still called
\code{grad\_u} in the code. It is a difference estimator of the same
Brookshaw/Cleary--Monaghan family, on the volumetric field
$U\equiv\rho u$ (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-units}) contracted with each
particle's own closure tensor:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf T_{ij} = U_i\,\mathbf D_i\!\cdot\!\mathbf{dx}
                 - U_j\,\mathbf D_j\!\cdot\!\mathbf{dx} , \qquad
  \left(\frac{\nabla\cdot\mathbf P}{\rho}\right)_i \mathrel{+}=
    -\frac{m_j}{\rho_i^2}\,\mathbf T_{ij}\,w_{i,dr}\,r_{ij}^{-1} ,
  \qquad
  \left(\frac{\nabla\cdot\mathbf P}{\rho}\right)_j \mathrel{+}=
    -\frac{m_i}{\rho_j^2}\,\mathbf T_{ij}\,w_{j,dr}\,r_{ij}^{-1} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-impl-grad}
\end{equation}
each particle using \emph{its own} kernel derivative ($w_{i,dr}$ for
$i$, $w_{j,dr}$ for $j$; no shared average) and no grad-$h$ correction
factor. At $\mathbf D_i=\mathbf D_j=\mathbf I/3$ this is exactly the
older scalar form with the $1/3$ made explicit. Each particle's
$\mathbf D$ is built inside this loop from its own $(u,\mathbf F)$ via
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-closure}, the same place and the same way
\code{rt/SPHM1RT} builds its own (\code{rt\_iact.h}); the cost is four
tensor constructions per symmetric pair, not a separate loop.

The $1/\rho^2$ (rather than $1/\rho$) normalization is not an arbitrary
choice: with it, Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad} is the exact negative
adjoint of Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-div}'s divergence, which is the property
the staggered time integration below wants on a disordered particle
distribution. Under an anisotropic closure that statement needs to be
made precisely, and it is worth doing so because the precise version is
weaker than the P1 one and the difference is not a bug.

\textbf{What adjointness survives under M1, exactly.} Write
$\langle a,b\rangle=\sum_i m_i\rho_i\,a_i b_i$. Under P1 the pair
$(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F,\nabla u)$ of
Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-div}--\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad} satisfies
$\langle u,\nabla\cdot\mathbf F\rangle=-\langle\mathbf F,\nabla
u\rangle$ identically, pair by pair, for any $h_i\ne h_j$,
$\rho_i\ne\rho_j$. Under M1 this survives, but only in a
$\mathbf D^{-1}$-weighted metric:
\begin{equation}
  \big\langle \mathbf F,\ \mathbf D^{-1}\,\rho^{-1}\nabla\cdot\mathbf P
    \big\rangle
  \;=\; -\big\langle u,\ \nabla\cdot\mathbf F \big\rangle ,
  \qquad\text{exactly, whenever } \mathbf D_i=\mathbf D_j ,
  \label{eq:fuv-adjointness}
\end{equation}
for arbitrary $h_i\ne h_j$ and $\rho_i\ne\rho_j$ --- including the
$\mathbf F=\mathbf 0$ background, every particle's initial condition and
permanent far-field state. This is what selects the own-derivative form
in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad}: a shared averaged kernel derivative (the
form \code{rt/SPHM1RT} itself uses) breaks Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-adjointness}
at variable $h$, carrying a measured $0.1\%$--$0.8\%$ residual in the
near-isotropic background regime that converges only at first order and
is worst where $h$ contrasts sharply, where the own-derivative form is
exact to machine precision. Where $\mathbf D_i\ne\mathbf D_j$ a residual
$O(h\,|\nabla\mathbf D|)$ remains, the discrete image of the same
$\nabla\mathbf D$ term that breaks the continuum estimate for variable
coefficients; nothing removes it, and it is an ordinary consistency-order
error.

\textbf{What does \emph{not} survive, and why that is correct.} In the
plain isotropic metric a second residual remains, proportional to the
anisotropy $\mathbf A=\mathbf D-\mathbf I/3$. It is nonzero whenever the
closure is anisotropic at all --- including at perfectly uniform $h$
with the same constant anisotropic $\mathbf D$ on both particles, where
it has been measured at order unity. This is \textbf{not} a
discretization defect and must not be chased as one: the continuum M1
equations themselves do not conserve the isotropic quadratic functional
$\int(u^2+3|\mathbf F|^2/c_{\rm hyp}^2)$. For $\mathbf D=\mathbf I/3$
the two transport terms combine into a pure divergence and the
functional is conserved --- that is the P1 property, which is why an
exact discrete analogue existed --- and for any $\mathbf D\ne\mathbf I/3$
the cancellation fails. A discretization that conserved it exactly would
be consistent with P1, not with M1. What M1's transport part does admit
is symmetrization by $\mathbf D^{-1}$, giving the conserved quadratic
$\int(u^2+\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf D^{-1}\mathbf F/c_{\rm hyp}^2)$ for
frozen $\mathbf D$, which is exactly the metric of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-adjointness}.

\textbf{Bound on that claim.} Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-adjointness} is
frozen-coefficient skewness in a time-varying, per-particle metric. It
is not an unconditional stability proof, and M1 has none. The metric
itself degenerates in the free-streaming limit: $\mathbf D$'s transverse
eigenvalue $(1-\chi)/2\to0$ as $f\to1$, so $\mathbf D^{-1}$ diverges and
transverse flux perturbations are not controlled by it --- and the flux
limiter explicitly permits $f=1$. \textbf{In the free-streaming limit,
stability rests on the CFL bound on $c_{\rm hyp}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-hyperbolic-cfl}) and on the artificial dissipation of
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-dissipation}, not on any adjointness argument.}
The full derivation of this subsection, with the numerical evidence, is
in \code{.claude/dev/M1\_gradient\_adjointness\_investigation\_2026-09-11.md}.

\textbf{Where the flux limiter runs.} Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter} is
applied once per particle per step, in the \code{end\_force} task, after
the Stage-1 dissipation correction has produced this step's \emph{final}
$u$ and after the extra ghost has already written this step's
$\mathbf F$. The ordering is load-bearing rather than incidental:
clamping against the earlier, pre-correction $u$ (the only $u$ the extra
ghost can see) would leave $|\mathbf F|>c_{\rm hyp}u$ at exactly the
near-front, low-$u$ particles the correction moves the most, which are
the ones the limiter exists to protect. One consequence, already noted
under Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi}: the next step's gradient loop evaluates
the closure on an $\mathbf F$ limited against the previous step's $u$
and a $u$ that has since been updated, so $f>1$ is reachable there and
the clamp in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi} is required, not decorative.

\textbf{Exact relaxation, and where the screening term lands.}
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}'s own local sink, $-c\,\kappa_{\rm
FUV}\rho\,u$ (the dust-screening/absorption term), is itself a plain
linear decay, $du/dt=-u/\tau_{\rm phys}$, with \textbf{exactly the
same} $\tau_{\rm phys}=1/(c\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho)$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-tau-phys}) that already governs $\mathbf F$'s own
Cattaneo relaxation. That is not a coincidence but a structural feature
of the moment system: both moments are removed from the field by the
\emph{same} physical absorption events, so they share the same rate.
Both therefore admit the same exact exponential integrator,
\begin{equation}
  u \to u\,e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{\rm phys}} , \qquad
  \mathbf F \to \mathbf F\,e^{-\Delta t/\tau_{\rm phys}} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-joint-relax}
\end{equation}
unconditionally stable regardless of how $\Delta t$ compares to
$\tau_{\rm phys}$, since $e^{-\Delta t/\tau}\in(0,1]$ for any
$\Delta t\ge0$: this is the ODE's exact analytic solution, not a
forward-Euler approximation of it.

\textbf{Treating the relaxation exactly is not optional, it is required
by stiffness.} Checked against the same \texttt{ClumpSmith2021}
snapshot numbers used in Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}: in the
clump, $\tau_{\rm phys}=\lambda/c_{\rm hyp}\approx0.008\,{\rm
pc}/(5\times0.33\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}})\approx5\times10^3\,$yr, shorter
than a typical hydro $\Delta t$ there ($\sim10^4$--$10^5\,$yr):
$\Delta t\gg\tau_{\rm phys}$, a genuinely stiff relaxation. An explicit
(forward-Euler) treatment of Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-joint-relax} would need
its own $\Delta t\ll\tau_{\rm phys}$ sub-cycling on top of the
hyperbolic CFL; the exact exponential integrator needs none, at zero
extra cost.

\textbf{Staggered time integration.} Consistent with the adjoint
property above, the two fields are not relaxed at the same point in the
step. Each step, $u$ is relaxed first, from the \emph{previous} step's
$\nabla\cdot\mathbf F$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-div}, frozen at that value
for the relaxation); $\mathbf F$ is relaxed second, from the gradient
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad}) of the \emph{newly}-relaxed $u$: a
leapfrog-like, staggered scheme rather than a single explicit sub-step
advancing both fields from the same accumulated fluxes at once. The
Stage-1 artificial dissipation of Section~\ref{sec:fuv-dissipation} is
applied as a correction on top of this same staggered sequence, after
both relaxations, not as a third field of its own.

\subsubsection{Injection cadence: a per-particle dose reservoir}
\label{sec:fuv-dose-reservoir}

\textbf{The problem: injection cadence versus relaxation cadence.} A
star's own feedback task touches an illuminated gas particle once per
\emph{star} step. If that particle sits on a finer time bin than its
star (an ordinary situation: the gas particle's own hydro/cooling
timescale, not the star's, sets its bin), depositing the whole
star-step budget as a single lump would put it all on the one substep
that coincides with the star's touch, leaving every other substep
between touches with no source term at all. On this scheme's exact
relaxation update the result is a sawtooth in $u$: a spike at the
touched substep, decaying purely by $e^{-\Delta t/\tau}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-joint-relax}) until the next touch. Cooling reads this
sawtooth's \emph{trough}, not its time-average, because cooling runs
before the star's own feedback task within the same step in the
task-graph ordering: a particle can therefore be significantly
under-heated during a cooling substep even though the
snapshot-visible, star-step-averaged budget it received is correct.

\textbf{Fix: accumulate a dose, drain it at a constant rate.} Each
illuminating star deposits exactly the specific-energy amount computed
by Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject} (superposing additively over any number of
simultaneously-illuminating stars), but into a per-particle, per-band
\textbf{dose reservoir} $R_i$ (same specific-energy units as $u_i$)
rather than directly into $u_i$. Alongside the deposit, the star's
touch extends the reservoir's \textbf{drain horizon} to (at least) one
star step past the last touch. At the start of every one of \emph{its
own} steps, the receiving particle converts a fraction of whatever
remains in the reservoir into a locally constant source rate for that
step,
\begin{equation}
  f_i = \min\!\left(1,\;\frac{\Delta t_i}{t_{{\rm rem},i}}\right), \qquad
  \dot u_{{\rm inject},i} = \frac{f_i\,R_i}{\Delta t_i}, \qquad
  R_i \to (1-f_i)\,R_i ,
  \label{eq:fuv-dose-drain}
\end{equation}
with $t_{{\rm rem},i}$ the physical time remaining until the drain
horizon and $\Delta t_i$ the particle's own physical timestep. $f_i=1$
whenever the horizon falls within this step (draining the reservoir to
exactly zero by the horizon); otherwise $\Delta t_i/t_{{\rm rem},i}$
drains it at a rate that stays constant across the particle's own
substeps between star touches. This removes the mismatch between the
star's cadence and the receiving particle's own relaxation cadence
entirely: exact for any number of stars on any combination of time
bins, not merely an improved approximation to the lump form.

\textbf{Folded into the same exact integrator, not added afterwards.}
Adding injection \emph{after} the exact decay, as a separate additive
term, would be accurate to $O(\Delta t)$ for the injection itself and
exact only for the pure decay. The fully exact solution of the
inhomogeneous ODE $du/dt=-u/\tau+S/m$ is $u(\Delta t)=u_0\,
e^{-\Delta t/\tau}+(S\tau/m)(1-e^{-\Delta t/\tau})$, and that is what
the reservoir realizes: $\dot u_{{\rm inject},i}$ enters as a genuine
additional frozen source in the same exact-relaxation ODE that already
advances $u$ each step (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-joint-relax}), rescaled by the
same reduced-speed-of-light factor $c_{\rm hyp}/c$ that
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-rsol-zeroth} carries ($c_{\rm hyp}$ in place of the true
$c$ throughout, Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}). The reservoir itself
always
holds the raw, physical dose; the rescale is applied exactly once,
where the drained rate enters the relaxation, not at deposit time.

\textbf{Edge case: a particle whose smoothing-length search finds no
neighbours.} The amount drawn down from the reservoir for a given step
is only actually consumed once the exact-relaxation update that uses it
runs; if a particle's density-loop $h$-iteration gives up with no
neighbours found, that update never runs for it this step. The drawn
amount is restored to the reservoir in that case, rather than silently
discarded.

\textbf{Bound on the mechanism}: the reservoir's value is specific to
the particle's mass at deposit time and is not rescaled if that mass
changes (a mass-changing feedback event, e.g.\ a supernova kick or mass
return) between the deposit and the drain.

\subsubsection{Numerical stabilization: artificial dissipation}
\label{sec:fuv-dissipation}

\textbf{This term is a numerical/artificial stabilization device, not a
physical source or sink.} It does not belong in, and must not be folded
into, the governing equations' own source term $S$ or their physical
absorption term $-c\,\kappa_{\rm FUV}\rho\,u$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}): those represent real photon emission and
dust absorption; the term below represents nothing physical at all,
only a correction to the discrete scheme's own behaviour.

\textbf{Why it is needed.} The transport update
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}) is built from a pairwise SPH
kernel-gradient operator, not from a Riemann-solver flux the way a
Godunov/finite-volume hyperbolic code would use. A Riemann solver
carries its own built-in numerical dissipation, which damps exactly the
short-wavelength oscillations a discretized hyperbolic system can
develop near a steep gradient. The pairwise kernel-gradient scheme used
here has no such mechanism: nothing intrinsic to it damps an
overshoot/undershoot near a strong, localized source sitting next to
optically thick gas, and $u$ can be driven negative there, an
unphysical state cooling cannot consume safely. This is the identical
role artificial viscosity plays in ordinary SPH hydrodynamics, which
also has no Riemann-solver dissipation of its own: a genuine physical
fluid never needs the fix, but the discrete SPH gradient estimate does.

\textbf{Formula (Stage~1: a triggered scalar conductivity).} Write
$U_i\equiv\rho_i u_i$ for the volumetric form of the specific field
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-units}) and $\bar W_{ij}=\tfrac12\big(\nabla
W_{ij}^{(i)}+\nabla W_{ij}^{(j)}\big)$ for the symmetrized kernel
gradient used consistently throughout this project's own pairwise
operators: never mix kernel-gradient prescriptions between a
dissipative and a non-dissipative term acting on the same field, since
doing so has produced serious SPH instabilities in the literature. Per
pair $(i,j)$, per band,
\begin{equation}
  v_{{\rm sig},ij} = \alpha_{ij}\,\min\!\big(c_{{\rm hyp},i},\,c_{{\rm hyp},j}\big),
  \qquad \alpha_{ij}=\max(\alpha_i,\alpha_j),
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-vsig}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \Psi_{ij} = v_{{\rm sig},ij}\,\frac{U_i-U_j}{\rho_i\rho_j}\,\bar W_{ij},
  \qquad
  \dot u_i\big|_{\rm diss} = \sum_j m_j\,\Psi_{ij}, \qquad
  \dot u_j\big|_{\rm diss} = -\sum_i m_i\,\Psi_{ij},
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-formula}
\end{equation}
manifestly antisymmetric in the exchanged mass-weighted contribution: for
any pair where both $i$ and $j$ are active and dispatched together this
step, $\sum_i m_i\,\dot u_i|_{\rm diss}=0$ pairwise and $\sum_i m_i u_i$
is conserved to machine precision. This is not unconditional: the
force-loop dispatch writes only the active particle's own accumulator,
so an active/inactive pair contributes on the active side alone, with no
matching debit until (if ever) the inactive particle is itself
dispatched on its own step. Because $\alpha_{ij}=\max(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-vsig}), the term can fire from either side of the
pair, not only from the active particle's own $U_i<0$; the leak's sign
follows $\operatorname{sign}(U_i-U_j)$ for the triggered pair, so it can
add to or subtract from the active particle's energy depending on which
side triggered and the relative sign of $U_i$ and $U_j$, though it
remains correlated with the trigger rather than averaging out over many
substeps. This is a known, structural limitation of Stage~1, not a
corner case. In the continuum limit the term reduces to
an ordinary diffusion of the volumetric field,
$\dot u_i|_{\rm diss}\to(D_{\rm eff}/\rho_i)\,\nabla^2 U_i$ with
$D_{\rm eff}$ an $O(1)$-kernel-dependent multiple of $\alpha\,v_{\rm
sig}\,h$: a genuine numerical conductivity, of exactly the same
character as artificial viscosity's own diffusive continuum limit, with
no claim to represent any physical transport process.

Because $\alpha_i$ is triggered locally rather than fixed, $D_{\rm eff}$
is in general spatially non-uniform, and the term's conservation of
$\sum_i m_i u_i$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-formula}) does not extend to its
first spatial moment: under a persistent relative velocity between a
radiation source and the surrounding gas, the negativity trigger fires
preferentially at the trailing edge of the source's injection kernel,
the region where the field is being depleted, leaving the triggered
coefficient one-sided there. The propagated field's centroid then trails
the source by a small additional displacement beyond the underlying
transport scheme's own prediction. This displacement is bounded, not
growing, with the relative velocity once the latter reaches the
propagation speed, and its natural unit is $\lambda$, not $h$: measured at
0.2-1.8\% of $\lambda$ (2-7\% of $v_{\rm rel}\tau$) at the shipped
$\alpha_{\max}$ across every relative velocity and resolution tested so
far ($h/\lambda$ from 1.0 down to 0.30). The triggered region scales with
the field's own physical size rather than with one smoothing kernel, so in
units of $h$ the displacement grows as $h/\lambda$ shrinks (measured a
factor 6-7 in $h$ when $h$ is halved at fixed physics); whether the
$\lambda$-relative fraction itself saturates or keeps growing below
$h/\lambda\sim0.30$ is not yet established. It does not accumulate across
multiple sources or over the duration of a run: it is a property of the
field's steady state rather than a term that integrates in time. It is a
quantified numerical characteristic of this stabilization, not a
transport error: the underlying transport scheme's own
relative-velocity moment identity, exact at $\alpha_{\max}=0$, holds only
up to this bounded term once the stabilization is active.

The signal velocity uses $\min$, not the $\max$ hydro convention
(Rosswog 2015a, SPHENIX's own $v_{\rm sig}$) or a harmonic/arithmetic
mean: under this scheme's closure $c_{{\rm hyp},i}\propto h_i/\Delta
t_i$, a particle pinned to an especially short timestep can have a
$c_{{\rm hyp},i}$ many orders above its neighbours', a purely numerical
artefact of its own step rather than a real wave speed. A quantity
shared by a \emph{link} between two particles, rather than owned by
either one, has to be combined into a single symmetric per-link value,
and which combination rule is right follows from the failure mode being
guarded against: here that failure mode is bin-ratio-driven, and $\min$
is the only combination that keeps the resulting dissipation number
bounded independent of the timestep-bin ratio between $i$ and $j$.
$\alpha_{ij}=\max(\alpha_i,\alpha_j)$ follows the same SPH philosophy
as $v_{\rm sig}$'s own hydro convention: whichever particle in the pair
actually needs dissipation gets it at full strength with every
neighbour.

\textbf{The trigger: a reactive negativity switch, not an anticipatory
one.} $\alpha_i\in[0,\alpha_{\max}]$ is not a fixed global constant; it
ramps up only when particle $i$'s own field has actually gone negative,
relative to its neighbours' local field scale,
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon_i = \frac{\max(-U_i,0)}{\max\!\big(\langle|U|\rangle_i,\,-U_i\big)}
  \in[0,1], \qquad
  x_i=\min\!\Big(\frac{\varepsilon_i}{\varepsilon_1},1\Big), \qquad
  \alpha_{{\rm aim},i}=\alpha_{\max}\,x_i^2(3-2x_i),
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-trigger}
\end{equation}
with $\langle|U|\rangle_i$ the kernel-mean $|U_j|$ over $i$'s own
neighbours and $\varepsilon_1$ the saturation scale of the smooth cubic
ramp $x_i^2(3-2x_i)$: any negative $U_i$ produces some dissipation, and
$\alpha_i$ reaches its ceiling $\alpha_{\max}$ once $\varepsilon_i$ reaches
$\varepsilon_1$, not before. $\alpha_i$ jumps to
$\alpha_{{\rm aim},i}$ immediately when it rises, and otherwise relaxes
back down as
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i \to \alpha_{{\rm aim},i} + (\alpha_i-\alpha_{{\rm aim},i})\,
  \exp\!\left(-\frac{c_{{\rm hyp},i}\,\Delta t_i}{L_{\rm decay}\,h_i}
  - c_{{\rm hyp},i}\,\kappa_i\,\rho_i\,\Delta t_i\right),
\end{equation}
with $L_{\rm decay}$ a dimensionless decay-length parameter
(\code{RADIATION\_LW\_FUV\_DISSIPATION\_DECAY\_LENGTH}, default~5) and
$\kappa_i\rho_i$ this band's own linear absorption rate
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}) at particle $i$: not a fixed memory time,
but a per-particle rate that speeds up in optically thick gas, so a
particle that is no longer negative does not keep the term engaged
indefinitely. No
velocity-divergence-based switch (Balsara, Cullen \& Dehnen) applies
here: this field carries no bulk velocity to build one from, so the
switch is built directly from the field's own sign instead. Both bands
carry independent coefficients, since the more strongly absorbed band
can fail independently of the other. Explicit stability with the term
included bounds the admissible ceiling jointly with the propagation-speed
closure coefficient $C_{\rm hyp}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-choice}),
\begin{equation}
  6.2\,\alpha_{\max}\,C_{\rm hyp} + 0.70\,C_{\rm hyp}^2 \le 2 .
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-stability}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Choosing $\alpha_{\max}$.} The shipped default is 0.25
(\code{parameter\_example.yml}), calibrated on a uniform-density test
($Z/Z_\odot=1$, $n_H=10^3\,{\rm cm}^{-3}$) across a coarse/baseline/fine
ladder of gas-particle masses (0.8, 0.1, 0.0125~$M_\odot$, a factor of 4
in $h$, $h/\lambda_{\rm FUV}=1.22/0.61/0.30$). The minimum coefficient
that closes the sign error rises as $h$ falls: 0.15 at the coarse rung,
0.20-0.25 at the baseline rung, 0.25 at the fine rung, evaluated against
an energy-weighted, normalization-free comparison (sign closure on
$|E_{\rm neg}|/E_{\rm bulk}$; transport fidelity on the shift of the radii
enclosing 50\% and 90\% of the bulk energy against the
$\alpha_{\max}=0$ run). At $h/\lambda\lesssim0.3$ the undissipated
transport itself rings: its innermost shell is negative (14.5\% of the
bulk energy negative for a static source at that rung), and behind a
moving source the pre-dissipation field undershoots by 20-60\% of the
peak. The exact $P_1$ solution for a point source switched on at $t=0$ is
non-negative and time-constant everywhere behind a delta-function shell at
the causal front, so this ringing, and the resulting apparent plateau in
the raw field maximum, is numerical, not physical; a comparison normalized
against that plateau is not a measure of transport fidelity. The shell
itself carries a fraction $\exp(-t/\tau)\approx1-t/\tau$ of all injected
energy at small $t/\tau$; $t/(2\tau)$ sits in the plateau behind it and a
further $t/(2\tau)$ has already been absorbed, the three summing to 1
exactly. The shell's amplitude is $\lambda/w_{\rm eff}$ above the plateau
at the same radius, $w_{\rm eff}$ the shell's resolved width; at the
diffuse, low-metallicity, low-density corner this project also targets
this ratio is 80-800. A purely
negativity-triggered dissipation coefficient cannot see this shell at
all, since it is positive everywhere, which is the reactive trigger's
structural blind spot addressed there. Under the energy-weighted
comparison, $\alpha_{\max}=0.25$ closes the sign error at the fine rung
and leaves the field's cumulative energy distribution unchanged from the
un-dissipated run to under 1\% at every radius checked: the term damps
the ringing without moving bulk energy either outward or inward. Its
side effect is a centroid shift of the field of 0.2-1.8\% of $\lambda$
(2-7\% of $v_{\rm rel}\tau$), measured up to $h/\lambda\simeq0.30$; the
shift grows in units of $h$ as $h/\lambda$ shrinks, and whether the
$\lambda$-relative fraction itself keeps growing below this rung is not
yet established. This is not acausal propagation: the signed margin
between the propagated front and the analytic causal front stays within a
fraction of a smoothing length of the un-dissipated control at every
tested coefficient and rung. This project's production particle masses
(10 to a few $\times10^4\,M_\odot$) sit an order of magnitude or more
coarser in $h$ than every tested rung, on the side of the ladder where the
required coefficient is smaller and the measured side effect is smaller,
so 0.25 is a conservative default across the production range even though
production resolution itself was not directly tested. The low-metallicity
and low-density regimes, which lower the same $h/\lambda$ ratio tested at
the fine rung and can do so at any particle mass, remain untested below
that rung and are a more plausible route into a smaller-$h/\lambda$
regime than particle mass refinement alone.

\textbf{Placement in the update sequence.} The transport update itself
is the staggered relax-$u$-then-$\mathbf F$ sequence of
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}. Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-formula}
is accumulated over the existing force-loop hook
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-tasks}, alongside that loop's own transport
work), which runs after both of those relaxations and after the trigger
of Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-trigger} has already been evaluated from this
step's own relaxed $u$; the accumulated correction is then applied once
per active particle in a force-loop finalization step that the
task-graph ordering places immediately before cooling. A coefficient
raised by an undershoot this step therefore corrects this same step's
$u$ before cooling ever reads it, rather than only the following step's.

\paragraph{A floor under the trigger, for the diffuse-phase optically-thin
corner}
\label{sec:fuv-diss-floor}

\textbf{The trigger's structural blind spot.} Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-trigger}
raises $\alpha_i$ only when particle $i$'s own field has gone negative.
The delta-function shell at the causal front (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-p1})
is positive everywhere by construction, so the trigger is exactly zero on
every particle inside it, in every trigger-gated run measured, regardless
of $\alpha_{\max}$: raising the ceiling damps the dispersive wake the
shell sheds behind it more strongly once it forms, but never touches the
shell itself, which is the wake's source. At the diffuse, low-metallicity,
low-density corner this project also targets ($h/\lambda\ll1$, well below
the fine rung of the calibration above), this blind spot lets the wake
regenerate every step, so the field a particle reads stays a bimodal mix
of exact zero and a positive overshoot of a few times the plateau value,
not decaying with more steps.

\textbf{Formula.} A second, unconditional contribution is combined with
the trigger's own output by a per-band, per-particle maximum,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_i = \max\!\left(\alpha_{{\rm trig},i},\;
  \frac{\alpha_{\rm floor}}{1+\big(h_i\kappa_i\rho_i/\varepsilon_\lambda\big)^2}
  \right) ,
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-floor}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha_{{\rm trig},i}$ the trigger's own value
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-trigger} and its decay), $h_i\kappa_i\rho_i$ this
band's own $h/\lambda$ at particle $i$ (the code's own \code{kappa}
variable already holds the linear rate $\kappa\rho$; this document's
$\kappa$ is the mass opacity defined in
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-lambda-chyp}, so the density factor is written
explicitly here), and two new parameters:
$\alpha_{\rm floor}$ (\code{GEARFeedback:LW\_FUV\_dissipation\_alpha\_floor},
shipped default 0.5) and $\varepsilon_\lambda$
(\code{GEARFeedback:LW\_FUV\_dissipation\_floor\_h\_over\_lambda}, shipped
default 0.05), the screening-length error budget the roll-off is gated
on. Unlike the trigger, the floor is not conditioned on negativity: it
is present on every particle, positive or not, wherever $h/\lambda$ is
not much larger than $\varepsilon_\lambda$. $\alpha_{\rm floor}=0$
disables it and recovers the trigger-only behaviour of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-trigger} exactly. The joint stability bound of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-stability} is extended to
$\max(\alpha_{\max},\alpha_{\rm floor})$, since the floor can dissipate
even where the trigger never fires.

\textbf{Bounded steady-state cost.} The always-on constant-dissipation
term this document earlier rejected (the argument leading to
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-trigger} itself, not repeated here) distorts the
screening length by a fraction growing with $\alpha h/\lambda$
unboundedly as $h/\lambda$ grows. The roll-off in
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-floor} bounds this by construction: the product
$\alpha_{{\rm floor},i}\,(h_i\kappa_i\rho_i)$ is maximized at
$h_i\kappa_i\rho_i=\varepsilon_\lambda$, where it equals
$\alpha_{\rm floor}\varepsilon_\lambda/2$, giving
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\lambda_{\rm eff}}{\lambda} \le
  \sqrt{1+0.27\,\alpha_{\rm floor}\,\varepsilon_\lambda} ,
\end{equation}
$1.0034$ (0.34\%) at the shipped defaults: the floor's cost is negligible
everywhere the calibration above was performed ($h/\lambda\gtrsim0.3$,
where $\varepsilon_\lambda=0.05$ has already rolled it off to a small
fraction of $\alpha_{\rm floor}$), and only becomes significant in the
diffuse corner it targets.

\textbf{What the floor does and does not fix.} It supplies dissipation
exactly where the trigger structurally cannot, closing the sign error in
the wake once the front has passed: measured against the pre-floor
baseline's 20-54\% zero-clamped mass everywhere behind the front, the wake
clears fully at the shipped-corner rung and clears beyond a residual band
a few $h$ behind the pulse peak, shrinking with more steps, at the two
diffuse rungs tested. It does not, and cannot, remove the sign error in a
thin band close to the source ($r\lesssim\lambda/300$), where the exact
$P_1$ plateau itself is smaller than the scheme's own sub-resolution
launch ripple: that band is a property of the $P_1$ closure (there is no
positive field there for any dissipation coefficient to protect), not a
numerical defect, and no value of $\alpha_{\rm floor}$ removes it.

\textbf{This calibration predates the M1 closure, and is on that account
conservative.} The whole characterization above was measured under the
isotropic ($P_1$) closure, where nothing bounds $|\mathbf F|$ against
$u$. The flux limiter (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter}) removes the
shell/wake disproportion at its source, including in the near-source
band, leaving an ordinary bounded Gibbs-type over/undershoot at the front
instead of a sign failure. Nothing in the trigger or the floor is
closure-specific --- both are built on the $\rho u$ jump and on
$c_{\rm hyp}$, neither of which changes meaning under M1 --- so both
carry over mechanically and the stability bound is unchanged. Whether
their shipped \emph{values}, and indeed their functional shapes, are
still the right ones for a bounded M1 front is an open question at the
time of writing, not something this calibration answers; the defaults
below are the $P_1$-era ones. One further
caveat: because $\alpha_{{\rm trig},i}$ is combined with the floor by a
running max (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-floor}) and the trigger's own decay
memory stores this combined value, a particle leaving the diffuse regime
relaxes back toward the trigger-only coefficient over the trigger's
$L_{\rm decay}=5$-step memory rather than instantly; this is benign in a
static glass, where $h/\lambda$ changes only on the density/metallicity
timescale, far slower than 5 steps.

\textbf{Stage~2 (compiled optionally, off by default): a slope-limited
reconstruction of the Stage-1 jump.} Rather than using the raw
difference $d_{ij}=U_i-U_j$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-formula}, Stage~2
reconstructs it to the pair midpoint using each particle's own gradient
(a van~Leer-type limiter, following Chan et al.\ 2021):
\begin{equation}
  A_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf g_i\cdot\mathbf{dx}}{\mathbf g_j\cdot\mathbf{dx}} ,
  \qquad
  \Phi_{ij} = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(\frac{4A_{ij}}{(1+A_{ij})^2},\,0,\,1\right)
    \times\begin{cases}
      \exp\!\big(-25(\eta_{ij}-\eta_{\rm crit})^2\big) & \eta_{ij}<\eta_{\rm crit} \\
      1 & \eta_{ij}\ge\eta_{\rm crit}
    \end{cases} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-vanleer}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  d_{ij}^{\rm rec} = d_{ij} - \Phi_{ij}\,\tfrac12(\mathbf g_i+\mathbf g_j)\cdot\mathbf{dx} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-reconstructed}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf g_i=\nabla U_i$ (the previous step's finalized gradient,
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad}, applied to $U=\rho u$) and $\eta_{ij}=
r_{ij}/\max(h_i,h_j)$, $\eta_{\rm crit}=1$. $\Phi_{ij}=0$ for
opposite-sign gradients (the exact single-particle-dip configuration
Stage~1 exists for), so $d_{ij}^{\rm rec}$ reduces to the raw jump there
and Stage~2 changes nothing at the case that matters most; it only
softens the jump between two smoothly-varying, same-sign-gradient
neighbours. $d_{ij}^{\rm rec}$ replaces $d_{ij}$ in
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-formula}, unchanged otherwise.

\textbf{Stage~3 (compiled optionally, off by default): an anisotropic
flux-dissipation term for $\mathbf F$ itself.} Stage~1 only damps $u$;
a flux-driven oscillation in $\mathbf F$ that never actually drives $u$
negative can still pass Stage~1's trigger by. Following the analogous
switch already used by this fork's own \code{rt/SPHM1RT} module
(Chan et al.\ 2021), define per particle a unit flux direction
$\hat{\mathbf n}_i=\mathbf F_i/|\mathbf F_i|$ and an anisotropic
diffusivity $D_{f,i}=\alpha_{f,i}\,v_{\rm sig}\,h_i$ ($v_{\rm sig}=
\min(c_{{\rm hyp},i},c_{{\rm hyp},j})$, the same pairing as Stage~1),
then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf t_i = \rho_i\,D_{f,i}\,\psi_i\,(\hat{\mathbf n}_i\cdot\mathbf{dx})\,
    \hat{\mathbf n}_i , \qquad \psi_i\equiv(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_i ,
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-flux-t}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \dot{\mathbf F}_i\big|_{\rm diss} =
    -\sum_j m_j\,\frac{(\mathbf t_i-\mathbf t_j)\,\bar W_{ij}}{\rho_i^2\,r_{ij}} ,
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-flux-formula}
\end{equation}
accumulated in the gradient loop (where $\psi_i$ is already final) and
applied as a third frozen term of the $\mathbf F$ relaxation
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}). Not antisymmetric in the same
way as Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-formula}: $\mathbf F$ is a genuinely
transported quantity, not a globally conserved sum, so nothing requires
it to be. Its own trigger fires only in compression
($(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_i<0$) and estimates a local shock/oscillation
strength from the time-rate of change of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf F$ itself,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{f,{\rm aim},i} = \mathrm{clamp}\!\left(
    -A\,\frac{h_i^2}{u_{V,i}\,c_{{\rm hyp},i}^2}\,
    \frac{(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_i-(\nabla\cdot\mathbf F)_i^{\rm prev}}{\Delta t_i} ,\,
    0,\,1\right) ,
  \label{eq:fuv-diss-flux-trigger}
\end{equation}
with $A=200$ as shipped and the same reactive-decay memory as
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-trigger} otherwise. Neither Stage changes the
physical content of Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-p1-zeroth}--\ref{eq:fuv-p1-first};
both remain refinements of the same artificial-stabilization idea as
Stage~1.

\subsubsection{Task-graph placement: reusing the existing hydro loops}
\label{sec:fuv-tasks}

SWIFT's existing hydro density/gradient/force pairwise loops
(\code{TASK\_LOOP\_DENSITY}, \code{TASK\_LOOP\_GRADIENT},
\code{TASK\_LOOP\_FORCE} branches of the same generated-function
template in \code{src/runner\_doiact\_functions\_hydro.h}) are already
shared by several physics modules at once: e.g.\
\code{runner\_iact\_chemistry} is called inline inside the density-loop
branch, from the plain \code{chemistry/GEAR/} kernel-average module.
Adding a new physics channel this way means adding new
\code{runner\_iact\_*} functions called from these same branches, not
standing up new tasks in the scheduler.

\textbf{The concrete mapping is}: three loop hooks, all in
\code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation\_propagation\_iact.h}.
\textbf{Density loop} (\code{runner\_iact\_[nonsym\_]isrf\_propagation}):
accumulates $\nabla\cdot\mathbf F$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-div}) and the
dissipation trigger's kernel-mean reference accumulator
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-dissipation}).
\textbf{Gradient loop} (\code{runner\_iact\_[nonsym\_]isrf\_gradient}):
accumulates $\nabla u$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad}). \textbf{Force
loop} (\code{runner\_iact\_[nonsym\_]isrf\_dissipation}): accumulates
the Stage-1 artificial-conductivity exchange
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-formula}). No new task \emph{type} is added to
the scheduler: three existing loop hooks, the same "add an iact
function" pattern used throughout, plus one new per-particle finalizer
(\code{feedback\_end\_force}, applying the dissipation correction,
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-dissipation}) alongside the existing
density/gradient ghosts. Being embedded directly in existing hydro
pairwise loops, the update runs on every active particle's own hydro
timestep, not gated behind any separate, rarer cadence.

This does not exempt the new field ($\mathbf F_i$) from the usual
cell-link/\code{cell\_clean\_links} bookkeeping this project's own
task-registration checklist requires for any new per-particle state.
In this case there is nothing to reset: no new \code{struct link*} cell
field was added, only plain per-particle POD fields in
\code{struct feedback\_part\_data}, which the existing whole-struct
restart dump/restore path covers automatically. That was checked
directly rather than assumed, with a kill-mid-run/resume comparison
against an uninterrupted control run.

\subsection{Propagation off: the instantaneous-field mode}
\label{sec:fuv-injection-off}

With \code{GEARFeedback:LW\_FUV\_propagation} off, which is the
\textbf{default}, none of Section~\ref{sec:fuv-propagation-scheme}'s
transport or dissipation machinery runs at all: $u_{\rm FUV}$/$u_{\rm
LW}$ are written directly by injection, with no dose reservoir, no
hyperbolic relaxation, and no Stage-1/2/3 dissipation.

In this mode, a star's touch resets $u_{\rm FUV}$/$u_{\rm LW}$ to zero
on the \emph{first} touch of the step (by any star), then every touch
that step, including the first, adds
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-inject}'s deposit divided by $m_i$: a second
simultaneously-illuminating star superposes onto what the first just
wrote, but the value carried over from a previous step is discarded
rather than decayed or propagated. The field a particle carries is
therefore an \emph{instantaneous} snapshot of this step's own
illumination alone, not an accumulated or smoothed quantity: reached
only within each illuminating star's own kernel radius, with the sharp
kernel-edge cutoff and patchiness Section~\ref{sec:fuv-propagation}
already describes as injection's own limitation. Receiver-side
extinction (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-extinction}) still applies in this mode,
unchanged; only the propagation step and the dose reservoir are absent.

This is the simpler of the two runtime modes, useful as a baseline or
for isolating whether an effect is due to injection/extinction/Grackle
coupling at all, independent of anything propagation adds; it is not
a fallback approximation to the propagation-on field, since it has no
mechanism to reach any particle a star's kernel does not directly
touch.

\subsection{Composability with the metagalactic/interstellar UV
background}
\label{sec:fuv-background-composability}

This model produces only the \emph{local, stellar-sourced} contribution
to $G_0$. It is not meant to reproduce a metagalactic or
galaxy-averaged background: the codebase already has a separate,
spatially-uniform, time-varying UV background at the Grackle level
(\code{GrackleCooling:with\_UV\_background}, present in every current
example's \code{params.yml}), and a spatially-varying background (from
the collaboration's SPHINX simulation data) is separately planned.
\textbf{The local field composes additively with both, and duplicates
neither}: the per-particle \code{isrf\_habing} field
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-pe-grackle}) is meant to hold \emph{the local
$u_i$ alone}, converted to Habing units and \emph{added} to whatever
the background module supplies at that particle's position, not as a
replacement for it. No background floor term is needed in the update
equations themselves.

\subsection{Known, accepted limitations: shadows and crossing beams}
\label{sec:fuv-limits}

\textbf{Residual shadow-smearing.} A real photon field shows almost zero
flux directly behind a dense clump and full flux to the side; an
isotropic, diffusion-type field leaks some flux around the clump via
neighbouring particles, smearing that shadow into something softer than
reality. This is a direct consequence of the isotropy assumption in
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-eddington-closure}, and is the sharpest reason the
implemented closure is M1 rather than $P_1$: $f\to1$ near a strong,
little-scattered source (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi}) lets the field stream
directionally instead of leaking sideways. It improves the shadow
substantially; it does not fully fix it, and no moment closure does ---
the residual is bounded by how well two angular moments can represent a
sharply directional field, not by the numerics.

\textbf{M1's own, distinct limitation: crossing beams.} M1 is a
single-direction closure: at any point it assumes all local flux
points one way, $\hat{\mathbf n}=\mathbf F/|\mathbf F|$. When two
genuinely separate beams from two different sources cross at a gas
particle, M1 cannot represent "two beams passing through each other
unaffected": it merges them into one averaged direction, a
spurious artifact at the crossing point, well documented in the RT
literature and not fixed by a better (more expensive) M1 solver; it is
intrinsic to the closure. Directly relevant here since this project
already cares about multi-source/angular behaviour (the HEALPix HII
splitting, documented in the HII photoionization theory document's
algorithm section).

\textbf{Why, physically: moment-closure RT is to full kinetic RT what
fluid dynamics is to kinetic gas dynamics.} Both P1/M1 and ordinary
Euler hydrodynamics are moment truncations of an underlying kinetic
(Boltzmann-type) transport equation: radiative transfer's RTE for
photons, the Boltzmann equation for a gas of particles. Both inherit
the identical failure mode: a moment/fluid description collapses any
locally multi-modal distribution (two distinct particle streams that
have not thermalised together; two distinct photon beams that have not
scattered together) into one local bulk description (one flow velocity;
one flux direction), because a finite set of angular moments simply
cannot distinguish "two populations moving differently" from "one
population with the combined average motion." Representing genuinely
crossing, non-interacting populations correctly requires a kinetic (or
here, ray-based/multi-group) description, not a better moment closure;
this is a structural property of the moment-truncation approach, not a
correctable numerical shortcoming.

\subsection{Why M1 and not P1, and what the closure costs}
\label{sec:fuv-m1}

The closure itself is stated once, where it is derived
(Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-closure}--\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi},
Section~\ref{sec:fuv-p1}). This subsection records why it is the closure
this scheme uses, and what that choice does and does not cost.

\textbf{What P1 could not do.} $P_1$'s closure
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-eddington-closure}) is \emph{always}
$\mathbf P=u/3\,\mathbf I$, regardless of how directional the true local
field is. Two consequences follow, and the second is the one that forced
the upgrade. First, shadows are smeared (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-limits}).
Second, and more seriously, $P_1$ places nothing between the flux and
the energy: around a point source in optically thin gas its interior
field is smaller than the streaming value by a factor
$\sim3r/\lambda$, which vanishes as $\kappa\to0$, so the field behind
the front becomes arbitrarily small compared with the front itself and
any numerical ripple at any amplitude eventually exceeds it and the
field's sign fails. No amount of artificial dissipation fixes that: the
dissipation can damp a ripple, but it cannot manufacture a positive
field where the closure puts none. M1 removes the failure by
construction --- the flux limiter (Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-flux-limiter}) makes
the interior field $|\mathbf F|/c_{\rm hyp}$ at every $h/\lambda$ --- and
the Eddington tensor then makes the resulting directional transport
physically right rather than merely bounded.

\textbf{The closure swap is cheap, and needs no new numerical
machinery.} It requires no new state: $(u,\mathbf F)$ is already
tracked by the hyperbolic system of Section~\ref{sec:fuv-p1}, and $f$,
$\chi(f)$, $\mathbf D$ are local, per-particle formula evaluations,
recomputed inside the gradient loop and never stored or communicated.
Nor does it require the machinery of a full M1 solver: the proper M1
solver in \code{rt/GEAR} (\code{rt\_riemann\_HLL.h}) needs a precomputed
$100\times100$ lookup table of eigenvalues \citep{Gonzalez2007},
because M1's true characteristic speeds are a nontrivial function of
both $f$ and the angle between the flux and the face normal. None of
that is needed here: those characteristic speeds are bounded within
$[-c_{\rm hyp},c_{\rm hyp}]$ by construction (M1's defining design
property is that the reduced flux never exceeds 1), so the existing
transport operators (Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-impl-div}--\ref{eq:fuv-impl-grad})
carry the closure with $\mathbf D(f)\,u$ in place of $u/3\,\mathbf I$ in
the pressure term, and the CFL bound of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-hyperbolic-cfl} is unchanged in form: $c_{\rm hyp}$ is
defined as the fastest M1 characteristic ($f=1$), the same interval the
$P_1$ signal speed already bounded, so the joint
$(\alpha_{\max},C_{\rm hyp})$ stability condition
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-diss-stability}) needs re-verification against that
reading, not re-derivation.

\textbf{Implementation subtlety to get right}: under a
reduced-speed-of-light scheme, $f$ must be built with the \emph{same}
reduced $c_{\rm hyp}$, not the true $c$. If $f=|\mathbf F|/(c\,u)$ used
true $c$ while $\mathbf F$ is dynamically bounded by $c_{\rm hyp}\,u$
(with $c_{\rm hyp}\ll c$), $f$ would always come out tiny regardless of
how beamed the field really is, silently collapsing the closure back to
$P_1$-like behaviour and defeating the point of using M1 at all. The
same applies to the limiter, which is written against $c_{\rm hyp}$ for
the same reason.

\textbf{What the closure costs.} Three things, all accepted
deliberately. (i) Crossing beams are merged rather than superposed
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-limits}) --- structural to any moment closure, and
benign for this module's actual product, a many-source diffuse
background where overlap regions have $f\to0$ and the closure correctly
reverts to isotropy. (ii) The transport operator's exact
skew-adjointness now holds in a $\mathbf D^{-1}$-weighted metric rather
than the isotropic one, and degenerates as $f\to1$
(Section~\ref{sec:fuv-operators}); stability in the streaming limit
rests on the CFL and the dissipation stages instead. (iii) The system is
now nonlinear in $(u,\mathbf F)$ through $\mathbf D(f)$ and the limiter,
so the closure is time-frozen once per step, evaluated from the
post-density-ghost state, exactly as $\nabla u$ already was; no
implicit or iterative solve is introduced.

\subsection{Relation to existing SWIFT modules}
\label{sec:fuv-precedent}

This design borrows validated formulas and structure from modules
already present in this SWIFT fork, or in a sibling checkout maintained
in the same research group, rather than inventing them:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \code{chemistry/GEAR\_FVPM\_DIFFUSION/hyperbolic/} (sibling
    checkout, documented in
    \code{theory/ParabolicDiffusion/GEAR\_FVPM\_Diffusion\_Theory.tex}):
    the Cattaneo relaxation-equation structure
    ($\partial\mathbf F/\partial t + (K/\tau)\nabla u=-\mathbf F/\tau$,
    $c_{\rm hyp}=\sqrt{\|K\|/\tau}$), its exact analytic
    ($\exp(-\Delta t/\tau)$) integration of the relaxation source term,
    and its hyperbolic CFL ($\Delta t=C_{\rm CFL}\,\Delta x/v_{\max}$),
    all mirrored in
    Sections~\ref{sec:fuv-p1}--\ref{sec:fuv-cfl} above, for a different
    transported quantity. That module's own timestep section states the
    parabolic/hyperbolic trade-off in its own words: a parabolic
    $\Delta t\le C_{\rm CFL}\,\Delta x^2/\|K\|_F$ (quadratic in
    particle size) against its hyperbolic sibling's ordinary
    $\Delta t = C_{\rm CFL}\,\Delta x/v_{\max}$ (linear, like hydro),
    with the parabolic equation recovered exactly as the
    relaxation-time-to-zero limit of the hyperbolic one;
  \item \code{rt/GEAR} and \code{rt/SPHM1RT}: full, working M1-closure
    RT solvers already in this codebase, confirming
    Eqs.~\ref{eq:fuv-m1-closure}--\ref{eq:fuv-m1-chi} exactly (verbatim
    match against \code{theory/RadiativeTransfer/GEARRT/2-rt-equations.tex}),
    and the source of the \code{reduced\_speed\_of\_light} precedent
    (\code{rt/GEAR/rt\_properties.h}) for Eq.~\ref{eq:fuv-chyp-choice}
    and of the Chan et al.\ 2021 flux-dissipation switch that
    Section~\ref{sec:fuv-dissipation}'s Stage~3 follows.
\end{itemize}
Neither is reused as a full module: they are more expensive solvers
built for different purposes (exact metal mass conservation; full
radiation hydrodynamics), but each already validates a specific piece
of this scheme's own physics or numerics, which is worth more than an
independent derivation would be.
