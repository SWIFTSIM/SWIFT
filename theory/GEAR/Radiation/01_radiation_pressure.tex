\section{Radiation pressure}
\label{sec:radiation-pressure}

This section documents the radiation-pressure feedback channel
(\code{GEARFeedback:radiation\_pressure\_efficiency},
\code{radiation\_policy\_radiation\_pressure}), a separate mechanism
from the HII photoionization budget documented in the algorithm
section of the HII photoionization logbook
(\code{radiation.tex}/\code{radiation.pdf}, this same directory):
photoionization tags and re-floors gas
temperature, while radiation pressure injects momentum directly into
gas neighbouring the star. The two channels draw on \emph{different}
integrals of the star's spectrum ($Q_H$ vs.\ $L_{\rm bol}$, see
Section~\ref{sec:rp-vs-hii} below) and use the same SPH gather around
the star, but are otherwise independent -- a star can have one policy
enabled without the other. Not yet covered anywhere else in this
logbook; written up here for the first time (2026-07-19).

\subsection{Physical distinction from photoionization}
\label{sec:rp-vs-hii}

Two genuinely different physical mechanisms in a real star, not two
bookkeeping categories of the same effect (clarified 2026-07-20).

\textbf{Photoionization} is a quantum/atomic process: a photon with
energy above $13.6\,$eV (Lyman continuum, $\lambda<912\,$\AA) is
absorbed by a bound electron in hydrogen, ejecting it; the photon's
energy above the ionization threshold becomes the photoelectron's
kinetic energy, which thermalizes via collisions -- this is what heats
the gas to $\sim10^4\,$K, balanced against recombination/line cooling.
Only photons above that specific energy threshold count, and the
relevant budget is $Q_H$ (ionizing photon \emph{number} rate, the
blackbody-fit section of the HII photoionization logbook), not energy.
This is fundamentally a \textbf{thermal/chemical} effect -- it sets the
gas's ionization state and temperature, and in this code drives
dynamics only indirectly, through the resulting thermal-pressure
contrast against the cold ambient medium (the classical Spitzer D-type
expansion that STARBENCH, validated in the HII photoionization
logbook's own validation section, checks).

\textbf{Radiation pressure} is a mechanical process: \emph{any}
absorbed or scattered photon, at any wavelength, transfers momentum
$p=E/c$ to whatever absorbs or scatters it. This draws on $L_{\rm
bol}$ -- the star's entire spectrum, dominated for most stars by the
non-ionizing UV/optical continuum, not the ionizing band specifically
(the mass-luminosity fit vs.\ the separate ionizing-rate fit behind
$Q_H$, both in the HII photoionization logbook's blackbody-fit section
-- two different fits of the same underlying blackbody, not the same
fit reused). It is
a \textbf{dynamical} effect -- a direct push, independent of whether
the interaction happens to ionize anything, and in this code it acts
through Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate} directly, with no thermal
intermediary.

Two consequences worth keeping precise:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{They are not disjoint at the photon level.} An
    ionizing-photon absorption \emph{also} transfers momentum
    $h\nu/c$ in that same event -- momentum conservation does not care
    about the atomic-physics outcome. But that ionizing-photon
    momentum is a small slice of the total budget, since $Q_H$
    photons are only part of $L_{\rm bol}$ for most stars, and dust
    reprocessing/IR trapping (the $(1+\tau_{\rm IR})$ term,
    Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-tauIR}) adds momentum from photons that were never
    ionizing to begin with. This code does not attempt to avoid
    double-counting this overlap -- see the open interaction question
    in Section~\ref{sec:rp-open}.
  \item \textbf{They drive HII region expansion through different,
    competing channels.} Photoionization heats gas against a cold
    ambient medium ($\sim10$--$100\,$K) -- the resulting
    \emph{thermal-pressure} contrast drives the D-type expansion.
    Radiation pressure drives expansion through \emph{direct momentum}
    deposition instead, no heating required.
    \citet{KrumholzMatzner2009} -- already cited above for the Tier-2
    validation proposal -- is literally a paper about comparing these
    two channels: thermal-pressure-driven expansion dominates for the
    modest OB associations STARBENCH-style tests represent, while
    radiation pressure only becomes the dominant \emph{dynamical}
    driver for far more luminous, compact sources (starburst clusters,
    super star clusters) where $L$ is large and the region is
    dense/compact enough for $\tau_{\rm IR}$ to matter.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Momentum injection: single-scattering plus IR trapping}
\label{sec:rp-momentum}

Each active star's momentum injection rate is
(\code{radiation\_get\_star\_physical\_radiation\_pressure},
\code{src/feedback/GEAR/radiation.c}):
\begin{equation}
  \dot p_{\rm rad} = \frac{L_{\rm bol}}{c}\,(1+\tau_{\rm IR}),
  \label{eq:rp-momentum-rate}
\end{equation}
the standard single-UV-scattering-plus-dust-IR-trapping recipe: direct
absorption/scattering of the star's own UV/optical photons deposits
$L_{\rm bol}/c$ of momentum per unit time; photons re-radiated in the
IR by dust can scatter multiple times in an optically thick shell
before escaping, boosting the momentum transfer by a further factor of
$(1+\tau_{\rm IR})$ over a single scattering.

\textbf{Citation confirmed (2026-07-23), direct primary-source check.}
Darwin confirmed this codebase's implementation is transcribed from
\citet{Hopkins2020} (the FIRE-2 radiative-feedback methods paper,
arXiv:1811.12462, distinct from the FIRE-2 numerics paper
\citet{Hopkins2018} already cited elsewhere in this logbook). Fetched
and grepped the full text directly: \citet{Hopkins2020}'s Appendix~A
gives \textbf{exactly}
$(\kappa_{\rm FUV},\kappa_{\rm NUV},\kappa_{\rm opt},\kappa_{\rm IR}) =
(2000, 1800, 180, 10)\,(Z/Z_\odot)\,{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}$ -- this
codebase's $\kappa_{\rm IR}=10\,{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}\,(Z/Z_\odot)$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-tauIR}) matches precisely, not just order-of-magnitude.
\citet{Agertz2013}/\citet{Marinacci2019} remain plausible independent
lineages sharing the same functional form but are no longer the
open question -- \citet{Hopkins2020} is confirmed as the actual source.

\textbf{New finding from the same primary-source check: a term this
codebase is missing.} \citet{Hopkins2020} \S4.4, footnote 16 gives the
full LEBRON momentum-coupling formula as
$\dot p = \tilde\tau\,L_{\rm bol}/c$, with
$\tilde\tau = (1-e^{-\tau_{\rm single}})\,(1+\tau_{\rm eff,IR})$ --
\emph{not} simply $(1+\tau_{\rm IR})$ as
Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate} implements. The
$(1-e^{-\tau_{\rm single}})$ factor is the fraction of the
\emph{input} (non-ionizing UV/optical) luminosity actually absorbed
before reaching the dust that reprocesses it into the trapped IR
term -- this codebase's formula implicitly assumes
$\tau_{\rm single}\to\infty$ (i.e.\ $(1-e^{-\tau_{\rm single}})=1$,
full absorption), which only holds deep in a dense cloud. Hopkins et
al.\ report their own galaxy-scale simulations couple only
$\langle\tilde\tau\rangle\sim0.4$--$0.7$ of the bolometric luminosity
on average (an order-unity fraction of sightlines are optically thin
even in the optical) -- so at Darwin's production resolution
(10\,$M_\odot$ to a few $10^4\,M_\odot$ per gas particle), where much
of the coupling volume around a star can be diffuse, this
missing factor likely causes this codebase to \emph{over}-estimate
direct/single-scattering radiation pressure, on top of (and largely
independent from) the $\kappa_{\rm IR}$ bug's IR-trapping
\emph{under}-estimate (Section~\ref{sec:rp-bugs-20260723}) -- the two
are separate errors, not a cancelling pair, and there is no reason to
expect them to offset in general. Implementing this requires an
opacity for the input band(s), which \citet{Hopkins2020}'s own
$\kappa_{\rm FUV}/\kappa_{\rm NUV}/\kappa_{\rm opt}$ spread by over an
order of magnitude (2000 vs.\ 180\,${\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}$) -- a single
effective $\kappa_{\rm single}$ (e.g.\ luminosity/temperature-weighted
via the blackbody fit already computed for $T_K$, in the HII
photoionization logbook) would capture the leading effect without adopting
\citet{Hopkins2020}'s full 5-band architecture. Not yet implemented;
this is judged the single most valuable remaining physics gap in this
channel, ahead of the resolution/banding items below.

$L_{\rm bol}$ is \code{sp->feedback\_data.radiation.L\_bol}, set from
the same blackbody mass--luminosity fit as the ionizing photon rate
(the HII photoionization logbook's blackbody-fit section) and scaled
by \code{radiation\_pressure\_efficiency} (\code{feedback\_common.c}) --
an overall dial on this channel independent of the photoionization
budget, default behaviour unaffected unless explicitly set $\ne1$.

The IR optical depth is
\begin{equation}
  \tau_{\rm IR} = \kappa_{\rm IR}\,\Sigma_{\rm gas}, \qquad
  \kappa_{\rm IR} = 10\,\frac{\rm cm^2}{\rm g}\,\frac{Z}{Z_\odot},
  \label{eq:rp-tauIR}
\end{equation}
(\code{radiation\_get\_physical\_IR\_optical\_depth},
\code{radiation\_get\_physical\_IR\_opacity}) -- a metallicity-linear
dust opacity of $10\,{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}$ at solar metallicity, a
standard order-of-magnitude value for dust-processed IR opacity in this
class of model. $Z$ here is \code{sp->feedback\_data.Z\_star}, the
SPH-kernel-weighted gas metallicity at the star's own location
(accumulated in \code{radiation\_iact\_nonsym\_feedback\_density}
alongside $\nabla\rho_\star$ below, normalized by the same
$1/h^{d+1}$ factor in \code{feedback\_prepare\_radiation\_feedback}
that finishes $\nabla\rho_\star$; both are zero, not divided-by-zero,
for a star with no gas neighbours this step, since
\code{enrichment\_weight} being zero is checked before the divide).

\subsection{Gas column density: Sobolev approximation}
\label{sec:rp-sobolev}

$\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ in Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-tauIR} uses the Sobolev
approximation, the same length-scale trick used for line-of-sight
column densities in stellar-atmosphere and ISM radiative-transfer
codes:
\begin{equation}
  \Sigma_{\rm gas} = \rho_\star \times
  \left(h_\star\,\gamma_k + \ell_{\rm Sobolev}\right), \qquad
  \ell_{\rm Sobolev} = \frac{\rho_\star}{|\nabla\rho_\star|},
  \label{eq:rp-sobolev}
\end{equation}
(\code{radiation\_get\_comoving\_gas\_column\_density\_at\_star}), i.e.\
the local gas density times an effective path length: the kernel
support radius $h_\star\gamma_k$ (a floor -- always included, not just
a fallback) plus the Sobolev length $\rho/|\nabla\rho|$, the
characteristic distance over which the density itself changes by a
factor of $e$. $\rho_\star$ is \code{enrichment\_weight} (the star's
own SPH-averaged local gas density, already computed by the regular
enrichment density loop, reused here rather than a separate
accumulator); $\nabla\rho_\star$ is
\code{sp->feedback\_data.grad\_rho\_star}, an ordinary SPH kernel-gradient
density estimate accumulated in the same density-loop pass as $Z_\star$
above. \textbf{Degenerate case handled:} a locally uniform density
field (zero gradient -- e.g.\ unperturbed glass/grid initial
conditions, or a star with too few neighbours to resolve a gradient)
makes $\ell_{\rm Sobolev}$ formally undefined (0/0); the code checks
$|\nabla\rho_\star|>0$ explicitly and falls back to the kernel-radius
term alone in that case, rather than propagating a NaN.

\textbf{Confirmed against the primary source (2026-07-23).}
\citet{Hopkins2020} \S4.4 gives their own local column-density
estimate as $\langle\Sigma\rangle \approx \rho\,[h + \rho/|\nabla\rho|]$,
with $h=(m_i/\rho)^{1/3}$ the local resolution-element size --
structurally identical to Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-sobolev}'s
$\rho_\star(h_\star\gamma_k + \ell_{\rm Sobolev})$: same
floor-plus-Sobolev-length sum, just a slightly different (arguably
more physically motivated, since it is the actual SPH kernel support
radius rather than a generic mass/density cube root) floor term.
\textbf{One correction to an earlier assumption in this discussion}:
Darwin recalled FIRE capping the $\rho/|\nabla\rho|$ term at the
smoothing length; the formula as quoted in \citet{Hopkins2020} \S4.4
is a plain sum, no $\min(\cdot)$ -- i.e.\ no cap is visible in this
specific paper. This may be in \citet{Hopkins2018} (their ``Paper~I'',
not yet checked) or a later code update, or simply a misremembering --
not resolved this pass. Practically: this codebase's formula already
matches \citet{Hopkins2020}'s own documented approach as-is, so the
resolution-robustness concern raised below (production runs at
10--$10^4\,M_\odot$ per particle) is about noise in $|\nabla\rho|$
at coarse resolution, not about deviating from the primary source --
a cap would be a robustness \emph{improvement over} what
\citet{Hopkins2020} documents, not a correction to match it.

\subsection{Distributing the kick to gas particles}
\label{sec:rp-distribute}

$\dot p_{\rm rad}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate}) is a single
star-level rate; distributing it to individual gas neighbours uses the
same SPH-kernel-weighted-share pattern already used for chemical
enrichment (\code{radiation\_iact\_nonsym\_feedback\_apply}):
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\vec p_j = -\,\dot p_{\rm rad}\,\Delta t_\star\,
  \frac{m_j w_{ij}}{\rho_\star}\,\hat{\vec r}_{j\star},
  \label{eq:rp-per-particle}
\end{equation}
where $\Delta t_\star$ is the star's own current timestep, $w_{ij}$ the
kernel weight, $m_j$ the gas particle's mass, and $\hat{\vec r}_{j\star}$
the unit vector pointing radially outward from the star to the gas
particle (the code computes $-dx/r$ with $dx \equiv \vec x_\star -
\vec x_j$, i.e.\ away from the star, with the usual comoving/physical
$\times\,a$ conversion applied). The result accumulates into
\code{xpj->feedback\_data.radiation.delta\_p} -- a field distinct from
the top-level \code{xpj->feedback\_data.delta\_p} used for
supernova/wind momentum (\code{feedback\_struct.h}), applied separately
in \code{feedback\_update\_part\_radiation}
(\code{feedback\_data\_iact.h} -- see the bug fixed in the HII
photoionization logbook's bugs-and-fixes section,
\code{hit\_by\_radiation} was never actually
set until 2026-07-19, so this accumulated momentum was silently
discarded every step until then).

\subsection{Bugs found and fixed (2026-07-23)}
\label{sec:rp-bugs-20260723}

Two bugs found during T8 code review, both fixed the same day (commits
\code{eb105ee62}, \code{f6d93460e}):

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Inverted unit conversion in $\kappa_{\rm IR}$}
    (\code{radiation\_get\_physical\_IR\_opacity}). The code multiplied by
    the AREA/MASS cgs conversion factor instead of dividing by it --
    verified via an SI-unit cross-check (10\,cm$^2$/g should convert to
    exactly 1\,m$^2$/kg; the buggy formula gave 100). In this project's
    unit system this underestimated $\tau_{\rm IR}$ by a factor of
    $\sim$4.4, suppressing the IR-trapping boost. The sibling
    $\sigma_{\rm HI}$ conversion a few lines above already divides
    correctly, confirming this was an inconsistency, not a deliberate
    choice.
  \item \textbf{Stale $L_{\rm bol}$ past star death}
    (\code{feedback\_will\_do\_feedback}). Unlike
    \code{supernovae.energy\_ejected}/\code{winds.energy\_ejected}, which
    are explicitly zeroed every step, \code{radiation.L\_bol} was never
    reset -- every early-return path for a dead star skipped the code
    that would otherwise overwrite it with a fresh value. A dead star's
    remnant kept exerting its last living radiation-pressure kick on gas
    neighbours indefinitely. Invisible in every currently-validated
    example (\code{radiation\_pressure\_efficiency=0} already zeroes
    $L_{\rm bol}$ every living step too), so this would only have
    surfaced once the channel was actually enabled on a run outliving
    the shortest stellar lifetime -- exactly the T8 scenario.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Open questions, not investigated this pass}
\label{sec:rp-open}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tier 1 example now exists}
    (\code{examples/SubgridTests/SubgridRadiation/RadiationPressureMomentumCheck},
    added 2026-07-23) but has not yet been run -- queued behind the
    ongoing T5 STARBENCH regression check. See
    Section~\ref{sec:rp-validation-plan}.
  \item Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-tauIR}'s $\kappa_{\rm IR}$ citation and value are
    now both fully confirmed against \citet{Hopkins2020}'s primary text
    (Section~\ref{sec:rp-momentum} above) -- no longer open.
  \item \textbf{The missing $(1-e^{-\tau_{\rm single}})$ absorption
    factor} (Section~\ref{sec:rp-momentum} above) is now judged the
    top-priority physics gap in this channel, ahead of the two items
    below.
  \item \textbf{Resolution-dependent robustness of the Sobolev length,
    not yet addressed.} Darwin's production runs span gas particle
    masses of $\sim$10--a few $\times10^4\,M_\odot$ (currently 95 and
    760\,$M_\odot$) -- far coarser than this Tier-1 test's box. At coarse
    resolution, $\ell_{\rm Sobolev}=\rho/|\nabla\rho|$ estimated from a
    kernel sum over $\sim$57 neighbours is inherently noisy. As now
    confirmed (Section~\ref{sec:rp-sobolev} above),
    \citet{Hopkins2020}'s own formula does not cap this term either --
    so a cap would be a robustness improvement beyond the primary
    source, not a fix to match it. Concrete proposal, unchanged:
    $\ell_{\rm Sobolev} \to \min(\rho_\star/|\nabla\rho_\star|,\,
    h_\star\gamma_k)$, capping $\Sigma_{\rm gas}$
    (Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-sobolev}) at $2\,\rho_\star h_\star\gamma_k$. A
    thermal/turbulent Jeans length was considered as an alternative
    length scale entirely (also used elsewhere in the subgrid-feedback
    literature) but is not recommended as a \emph{replacement}: it
    assumes local gravitational (Jeans) equilibrium, which need not hold
    for gas actively being swept up by the same radiation
    pressure/photoionization event whose column density it would be
    used to estimate -- a category mismatch the density-gradient Sobolev
    estimate does not have, since it measures the actual local density
    structure regardless of dynamical state. The cap above is the
    targeted fix for the noise problem without introducing that
    mismatch.
  \item \textbf{Band-splitting: ionizing/non-ionizing plus one effective
    single-scattering opacity, not a fuller split.} Darwin considered a
    full \citet{Hopkins2020}-style multi-band split
    (ionizing/FUV/NUV/optical/IR) but ruled out tree-based transport for
    any of it -- that is reserved for the separate FUV/ISRF work
    (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-isrf}). \textbf{Correction to an initial
    claim made in this discussion}: dust opacity is \emph{not} close to
    grey from UV through optical -- \citet{Hopkins2020}'s own
    $\kappa_{\rm FUV}=2000$, $\kappa_{\rm NUV}=1800$,
    $\kappa_{\rm opt}=180\,{\rm cm^2\,g^{-1}}\,(Z/Z_\odot)$ span more
    than an order of magnitude among themselves, well before reaching
    $\kappa_{\rm IR}=10$. This directly motivates the
    $(1-e^{-\tau_{\rm single}})$ item above needing \emph{some}
    band-aware opacity, even if not full separate per-band budgets: an
    effective, luminosity/temperature-weighted $\kappa_{\rm single}$
    (blended from the existing blackbody fit) is the pragmatic middle
    ground -- capturing the leading effect without \citet{Hopkins2020}'s
    full 5-band architecture or its long-range tree-based propagation.
    \textbf{Concrete construction, added 2026-07-24}: this does not need
    a new fitting effort -- the FUV/ISRF module already builds two band
    luminosities per star via blackbody-curve threshold integrals,
    $L_{\rm FUV}$ and $L_{\rm LW}$ (Section~\ref{sec:fuv-two-bands}); a
    third threshold pair over the NUV/optical range, on the same curve,
    gives exactly the ingredients for a luminosity-weighted
    $\kappa_{\rm single}=\sum_{\rm bands}L_{\rm band}\kappa_{\rm
    band}/L_{\rm bol}$, reusing that machinery rather than inventing a
    separate blend. Not yet implemented; naturally sequenced after the
    FUV/ISRF module's own band-integral code exists, not before.
    Confirmed useful precedent for keeping this local, no-tree: their
    \S4.3.4/\S4.4.1 ablation (disabling only the tree-propagated
    ``long-range'' component of LEBRON while keeping the local,
    kernel-scale ``short-range'' component) found radiation pressure
    specifically is \emph{dominated} by the short-range term -- removing
    only long-range RP gave results ``very similar to simply disabling
    all radiation pressure''. The long-range/tree component matters more
    for diffuse-gas photo-\emph{heating} in their tests, not
    radiation-pressure momentum -- consistent with treating that as an
    ISRF-module concern, not this channel's.
  \item \textbf{Interaction with photoionization -- a concrete plan, not
    yet implemented.} An ionizing-photon absorption event physically
    transfers momentum $h\nu/c$ in the same event that ionizes/heats the
    gas (Section~\ref{sec:rp-vs-hii}). Currently $L_{\rm bol}$ (used for
    the generic SPH-kernel-shared radiation-pressure momentum,
    Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-per-particle}) includes the ionizing band, so that
    momentum is distributed via the kernel-share weighting, \emph{not}
    the angular-pixel/flux-based weighting the HII algorithm already
    uses to decide who actually absorbs those specific photons -- a
    mismatch, not a double-count of energy, since the two channels draw
    on different budgets ($Q_H$ number rate vs.\ $L_{\rm bol}$ energy
    rate) but currently apply momentum from the same photons via two
    different, uncoordinated distribution schemes. Proposed fix: exclude
    the ionizing band's energy from the $L_{\rm bol}$ used specifically
    in Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate}, and instead give momentum directly
    at the photoionization-tagging call site
    (\code{feedback\_iact\_HII\_ionization}), using the same per-particle
    ionizing flux T5's rate-coupling already computes there. Requires one
    new quantity not currently available: the ionizing band's
    \emph{energy} rate (not just $Q_H$, its \emph{number} rate) --
    $\dot E_{\rm ion} = Q_H\,(E_{\rm threshold}+\langle E_{\rm
    excess}\rangle)$, a straightforward combination of quantities T5
    already computes. Not yet implemented; queued behind Tier~1/Tier~2
    validation.
  \item \textbf{Sobolev-cap source corrected (2026-07-23).} Darwin
    clarifies the smoothing-length cap is not from \citet{Hopkins2020} --
    it is from elsewhere in the literature (paper not yet pinned down),
    which uses a second-order least-squares gradient-matrix estimator
    (as in meshless-finite-mass/volume methods) rather than the plain
    SPH kernel-gradient sum this codebase uses, and so computes
    $\nabla\rho$ more accurately in the first place. This is a plausible
    reason \citet{Hopkins2020}'s own formula (Section~\ref{sec:rp-sobolev}
    above) needs no cap while an SPH-based code might still want one:
    the noise this cap would guard against is method-dependent, not a
    universal feature of the Sobolev approach itself.
  \item \textbf{Fundamental resolution caveat for self-gravitating
    production runs, via \citet{Krumholz2018}.} Darwin flagged this
    paper (MNRAS 480, 3468, arXiv:1805.00210) as worth checking; fetched
    and read directly. Its core result: whether radiation pressure can
    reverse a gravity-driven \emph{accretion flow} onto a source is set
    by physics at the dust sublimation radius,
    $r_s = 780\,L_6^{1/2}Q_{-2}^{-1/2}T_{s,3}^{-2}\,{\rm AU}$
    ($L_6\equiv L/10^6 L_\odot$) -- e.g. $\sim$300 AU for a 26.75
    $M_\odot$ star (STARBENCH's own star). Resolving this needs mass
    resolution $\sim 0.01$--$0.09\,M_\odot$ (their \S4), which the paper
    states explicitly is ``orders of magnitude beyond state of the art
    galactic or cosmological simulations'' -- including, unavoidably,
    this codebase's own 10--$10^4\,M_\odot$ production resolution.
    \textbf{Mechanism}: gravity delivers momentum smoothly and is
    \emph{under}-delivered when softened at low resolution near a
    source, while radiation delivers a fixed \emph{total} momentum
    regardless of where (i.e.\ how far out) it happens to be absorbed --
    this asymmetry systematically biases unresolved simulations toward
    \emph{overestimating} radiation's ability to overcome gravity and
    disperse/halt infall, and conventional resolution-convergence tests
    can fail to reveal it (the solution switches sharply between two
    qualitative outcomes rather than converging smoothly).
    \textbf{Scope, to state precisely}: Krumholz's specific subgrid fix
    (Eq.~56-57 of that paper: suppress direct radiation force above a
    critical accretion-rate threshold) targets an actively-accreting
    source competing against its own inflow -- most directly analogous
    to this codebase's sink-particle star-formation module, not (yet)
    coupled to radiation pressure here at all, rather than to
    already-formed \code{spart}s pushing on ambient ISM gas as this
    channel currently does. The \emph{general} lesson (gravity-softening
    vs.\ radiation's resolution-insensitive total momentum can bias a
    gravity-vs-radiation contest) is still a relevant caution wherever
    a production run has both self-gravity and radiation pressure on
    near a young, still-forming/marginally-bound star cluster or clump
    -- e.g.\ the physical scenario \code{StromgrenSphereClump*}
    represents, though those specific examples currently run with
    gravity off, sidestepping this particular failure mode (for better
    or worse) rather than resolving it. Not actionable as a code change
    without first deciding whether/how to extend radiation pressure to
    the sink/accretion phase -- flagged here as an open, unscoped
    question, not a bug.

    \textbf{2026-07-23 follow-up}: Darwin noted sinks do not (and will
    not, for now) emit radiation pressure, so \citet{Krumholz2018}'s
    specific subgrid fix has no code to attach to yet. Still worth
    keeping two things from it: (a) the \emph{general} gravity-vs-radiation
    caveat above, which applies to any self-gravitating production run
    regardless of sinks, and (b) the methodological warning that ordinary
    resolution-convergence tests can fail to expose this class of error
    -- directly relevant to how any future convergence study of this
    channel should be designed (don't just check smooth convergence;
    check for a sharp qualitative flip between resolutions).

  \item \textbf{A second, more directly actionable numerical-method
    paper: \citet{HopkinsGrudic2019}} (MNRAS 483, 4187, arXiv:1803.07573,
    ``Numerical Problems in Coupling Photon Momentum (Radiation
    Pressure) to Gas''), found while re-checking the original FIRE-2
    citation and fetched in full at Darwin's prompt for ``other papers
    on radiation pressure''. Distinct from \citet{Krumholz2018}: this is
    about how momentum gets distributed to gas \emph{numerically}, not
    about a physical resolution scale. Their core result: a naive
    ``cell-centred''/``cell-integrated'' coupling -- evaluating the
    absorbed-flux integral (their Eq.~4) using field values at a single
    point/cell rather than integrating it towards the true geometric
    faces between fluid elements (their Eq.~7, the ``face-integrated''
    fix) -- can under-estimate the RP force near an unresolved point
    source by up to an order of magnitude, and this is \textbf{not}
    limited to moments-based (M1/FLD) methods: their own star-cluster
    formation test (\S5, Fig.~3) shows it just as strongly for
    \textbf{ray-based LEBRON}, the method this codebase's approach is
    architecturally closest to -- cell-centred LEBRON left RP with
    ``almost no effect'' on cluster SFE, while face-integrated LEBRON
    gave RP ``a dramatic effect''. They attribute at least one published
    literature disagreement (Grudi\'c et al. 2018 vs.\ Raskutti et al.
    2016, an order-of-magnitude SFE difference on similar clouds)
    directly to this choice.

    Their Appendix~A gives an explicit SPH/mesh-free ``effective face''
    construction to fix this:
    $A_{ba} = [\bar n_a^{-2}\,\partial W(|x|_{ba},H_a)/\partial|x|_{ba} +
    \bar n_b^{-2}\,\partial W(|x|_{ba},H_b)/\partial|x|_{ba}]\,\hat x_{ba}$
    -- built from the \emph{kernel gradient} $\partial W/\partial r$, then
    integrated towards each face. \textbf{This is structurally different
    from Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-per-particle}}, which distributes momentum by
    plain kernel-\emph{value} mass-weighting ($m_j w_{ij}/\rho_\star$,
    weights summing to 1) with no reference to the kernel gradient or
    any face-like construction. \textbf{Honest uncertainty, not yet
    resolved}: this codebase's scheme is not exactly their ``cell-centred''
    failure mode either (each neighbour's share does vary with its own
    distance from the star via $w_{ij}(r)$, unlike a single value applied
    uniformly) -- whether it reproduces some fraction of the same
    under-estimate, or is safe, has not been determined by inspection
    alone and would need either a careful term-by-term comparison against
    their Appendix~A formula, or (more practically) a dedicated
    resolution test in the spirit of their Figs.~1-3: run the same
    physical setup (e.g.\ the new Tier-1
    \code{RadiationPressureMomentumCheck} example, Section
    \ref{sec:rp-validation-plan}) at several particle counts and check
    whether the coupled momentum \emph{converges}, or instead shrinks
    towards zero as resolution improves -- the latter would be the
    diagnostic signature of this specific failure mode.

    \textbf{2026-07-23 follow-up -- already solved elsewhere in this
    codebase, not an open research question.} Darwin: the effective-face
    approach is already implemented for mechanical (SN/wind) feedback,
    in a separate branch, via angular weights. So the path forward for
    radiation pressure is to \emph{adapt that existing implementation}
    to this channel, not to run a from-scratch convergence study or
    derive a new SPH-specific face construction -- de-scoped from
    ``open question requiring investigation'' to ``known fix, needs
    porting.'' The convergence-test idea above is no longer needed to
    \emph{establish} whether this matters (it's the same class of error
    the mechanical-feedback work already addressed); it could still be
    useful later purely as a regression check once the port is done.

  \item \textbf{Effective-face port: name the actual branch/file before
    starting, added 2026-07-24.} The item above decides \emph{that} the
    fix should be ported from the existing mechanical (SN/wind) feedback
    implementation, not derived fresh -- but the specific branch and
    file were never pinned down in this logbook. First concrete step,
    not yet done: identify exactly which branch and which function
    implements the mechanical-feedback angular-weight/effective-face
    construction, before attempting the port itself.

  \item \textbf{Multi-timestep consistency of the momentum \emph{rate},
    added 2026-07-24.} Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate} gives a
    \emph{rate}, $\dot p_{\rm rad}$; turning it into an actual kick
    needs multiplying by a $\Delta t$, and stars and their gas
    neighbours generally sit on different individual timesteps in this
    code's hierarchical time-stepping. Not yet checked: whether the
    current implementation integrates $\dot p_{\rm rad}$ over the
    \emph{correct} $\Delta t$ when a star's own active step spans
    several of a neighbouring gas particle's (or vice versa) --
    analogous to the multi-timestep bookkeeping mechanical (SN) feedback
    already has to get right, but not confirmed to have been checked for
    this channel specifically.

  \item \textbf{Cosmological (comoving-to-physical) unit handling, added
    2026-07-24.} Every validated example to date is a non-cosmological,
    isolated box. Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate}/\ref{eq:rp-tauIR}'s
    $\Sigma_{\rm gas}$ (via the Sobolev density/gradient estimate,
    Section~\ref{sec:rp-sobolev}) and the resulting momentum kick have
    not been checked for correct physical-vs-comoving conversion in a
    cosmological run -- this whole channel's cosmological path is
    currently untested, not merely unvalidated against a specific target.

  \item \textbf{A cheap unit-level check, complementing Tier~1, added
    2026-07-24.} Tier~1 (Section~\ref{sec:rp-validation-plan}) validates
    the momentum formula only via a full simulation's accumulated
    output. A standalone unit test evaluating
    Eqs.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate}--\ref{eq:rp-sobolev} directly against
    hand-computed values for a few fixed particle configurations (no
    \code{swift} run required) would be a faster, more targeted
    regression check for this channel specifically, closer in spirit to
    \code{testRadiationRebuildCheck}'s role for the HII algorithm than
    Tier~1's full-simulation approach -- not yet proposed as its own
    test target.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Proposed validation plan (2026-07-20)}
\label{sec:rp-validation-plan}

Two tiers, deliberately kept separate since they check different
things and cost very different amounts of effort.

\textbf{Tier 1 -- momentum-conservation self-check (cheap, code
correctness).} Single star, uniform-density box, radiation pressure
only: \code{radiation\_pressure\_efficiency=1}, photoionization
disabled, cooling and self-gravity off. Log $L_{\rm bol}$ and
$\tau_{\rm IR}$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate}--\ref{eq:rp-tauIR}) every
step and integrate $\int \dot p_{\rm rad}\,dt$ from the code's own
diagnostics; compare against the actual total momentum gained by the
gas. This checks only that the implementation does what
Eq.~\ref{eq:rp-momentum-rate} says -- no missing factor, no
double-counting, no silently-dropped kick (the class of bug already
found and fixed in the HII photoionization logbook's bugs-and-fixes
section, \code{hit\_by\_radiation}). It is self-referential (code output
checked against code output) and does \emph{not} validate that the
underlying physical model is correct -- that is a separate question,
addressed by Tier 2.

\textbf{Tier 2 -- physics validation against
\citet{KrumholzMatzner2009}.} \citeauthor{KrumholzMatzner2009}
(\citeyear{KrumholzMatzner2009}, ApJ 703, 1352) derive an analytic
radius--time solution for a momentum-driven H\,\textsc{ii}-region
shell around a star -- the direct radiation-pressure analog of the
Spitzer (1978) D-type solution that STARBENCH (the HII photoionization
logbook's own validation section) already validates for pure
photoionization. Reproducing their idealized single-star setup and
comparing the simulated shell-radius growth to their analytic
expression would make this a genuine fourth validation target
(its own idealized IC, its own analytic-check script), not merely an
internal consistency check like Tier~1. \citet{Draine2011} (ApJ 732,
100, static equilibrium dusty H\,\textsc{ii} regions with radiation
pressure) is a possible cross-check for the static/equilibrium limit
if the dynamical \citet{KrumholzMatzner2009} setup proves awkward to
reproduce, but is not itself a radius(t) expansion test and does not
substitute for one.

\textbf{Sequencing.} Tier~1 is cheap enough to run alongside other
work and should happen first -- it would have caught the
\code{hit\_by\_radiation} bug on its own. Tier~2 is a new, separate
validation-target proposal (not yet scoped as its own example
directory or IC) and is queued behind it.
